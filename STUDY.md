# 🤖 AI Study Sessions — Replacing the "Read This Book" Assignments

Great instinct. Expecting a couple to devour *Ina May's Guide to Childbirth* (350+ pages), *The Birth Partner* (500+ pages), and *HypnoBirthing* (300+ pages) on top of exercises, classes, work, and nesting is a recipe for guilt and skipped learning. Here's a structured replacement: **targeted AI prompt deep dives** that extract the 20% of each resource that drives 80% of birth readiness, delivered in 10–20 minute interactive sessions.

---

## How to Use These

- **Copy-paste** each prompt into your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.)
- Treat it as a **conversation**, not a one-shot query — follow up, ask "why?", ask for examples
- Do **one session per topic per week** (15–20 min each)
- The partner does their own sessions separately, then you **discuss together** over dinner
- Save the AI's responses in a shared doc for quick reference on birth day

## 🟢 PHASE 1: WEEKS 25–28

### Week 25 — Session 1: The Birth Mindset (replaces *Ina May's Guide*, Part 1)

> **Prompt:**
>
> You are a experienced midwife who has attended 3,000+ births, in the teaching style of Ina May Gaskin. I'm 25 weeks pregnant preparing for a natural birth. In a warm, conversational tone, teach me:
>
> 1. The "sphinx" vs. "sphincter" principle — why fear and tension physically close the cervix, and how calm literally opens it. Give me the physiology in plain language.
> 2. Three real (anonymized) birth stories that illustrate how a woman's mental state changed the trajectory of her labor — one where fear stalled labor, one where a mindset shift turned it around, one where a calm environment led to a remarkably fast birth.
> 3. The single most important thing I can do *right now at 25 weeks* to build a fearless mindset.
> 4. What does "trusting the process" actually look like in practice during a contraction? Walk me through the inner monologue.
>
> End with 3 questions I should ask myself this week to uncover any hidden birth fears I haven't acknowledged yet.

**Follow-up prompts:**
- *"Tell me more about the fear-tension-pain cycle. Draw it as a flowchart in text, then tell me exactly where I can interrupt it."*
- *"What are the 5 most common fears first-time mothers have, and what does the evidence say about each one?"*
- *"Give me a 5-minute script I can read aloud to myself when I feel anxious about birth."*

### Week 28 — Session 5: Interventions — When, Why, and How to Navigate Them (replaces class session 4)

> **Prompt:**
>
> You are a balanced, evidence-based OB/GYN who respects physiological birth but also knows when interventions save lives. I'm 28 weeks pregnant, planning a natural birth, but I want to be *informed*, not naive. Teach me:
>
> 1. **The intervention cascade:** How does one intervention (e.g., early amniotomy) lead to the next (Pitocin → continuous monitoring → epidural → C-section)? Walk me through the chain, step by step, with the logic at each link.
> 2. **Each common intervention — the honest breakdown:**
>    - Pitocin (induction/augmentation)
>    - Amniotomy (breaking water)
>    - Continuous vs. intermittent fetal monitoring
>    - Epidural
>    - Episiotomy
>    - Vacuum/forceps
>    - C-section (planned and emergency)
>    For each: When is it *genuinely medically necessary*? When is it *convenient but optional*? What are the real risks and benefits? What questions should I ask before agreeing?
> 3. **My rights:** What can I legally decline? What's "informed consent" vs. "informed refusal"? What phrases can I use to buy time ("I need 10 minutes to discuss with my partner")?
> 4. **When to say YES:** What are the genuine red flags where I should *want* the intervention? Help me distinguish "this is scary but normal" from "this is actually dangerous."
> 5. **How to have a positive birth even if interventions happen.** I don't want to feel like I "failed."
>
> Be balanced. I don't want fear-mongering OR blind optimism. I want to walk in with eyes open.

**Follow-up prompts:**
- *"Write me a script for discussing my birth preferences with my OB at my 28-week appointment. What to say, what to ask, how to gauge if they're supportive."*
- *"What's the evidence on induction at 41 weeks vs. waiting until 42? What are the actual risks of going 'overdue'?"*

## 💡 Pro Tips for AI Study Sessions

1. **Do them together.** Sit with your partner, read the AI response aloud, discuss. *"Wait, what does 'asynclitism' mean? Ask it to explain again."*

2. **Save everything.** Copy the best responses into a shared Notes doc or Notion page titled **"Birth Playbook."** On the day, you'll want a 2-page summary, not 15 chat logs.

3. **Ask for role-play.** The most valuable prompts are the *"Role-play: you are a nurse suggesting X"* ones. They build actual conversational muscle memory.

4. **Request the "explain like I'm 5" version.** If a response is too clinical, say: *"Explain that again like I'm a smart 12-year-old. Use an analogy."*

5. **Challenge the AI.** *"Are you sure? What does the Cochrane review say? What's the opposing view?"* You want balanced information, not just confident-sounding text.

6. **Record the relaxation scripts.** When the AI writes you a HypnoBirthing script, **record yourself reading it** in a calm voice. Listen to your own voice during practice. It's more powerful than a stranger's recording.

7. **One session per week max.** Don't binge. Let the information settle. Discuss it over dinner. Sleep on it. Come back with follow-up questions the next day.
