# Chapter 1 — The Final Countdown (Weeks 36 to Birth)

*Read this in about 15 minutes. This is the "what's normal, what's not, and what to have ready" chapter for the stretch you're in right now: 36 weeks, due November 1, full term on October 11.*

> This chapter is education, not medical advice. Your gynae/midwife's instructions always win. Anything here marked **[confirm with TMC/your gynae]** is something I couldn't verify — ask at the next appointment.

---

## 1. What's happening in her body right now

At 36+ weeks the baby is essentially done growing the important stuff — lungs are maturing, he's gaining weight fast (~200g/week), and he's running out of room. For you, the visible changes from here are:

- **Lightening ("the baby dropped").** He's head-down (confirmed since week 35), and as he settles deeper into the pelvis her belly looks lower. She'll breathe easier but need to pee constantly. *This does NOT mean labour is imminent* — it can happen weeks before, especially in first babies.
- **More Braxton Hicks.** Tightening of the belly that comes and goes. Uncomfortable, irregular, stops if she changes position or drinks water.
- **Pelvic pressure, backache, trouble sleeping.** Standard. Not a warning sign by itself.
- **Mucus plug / bloody show.** A thick, jelly-like discharge, sometimes streaked pink or brown. It means the cervix is starting to change. Labour *can* follow within hours or days — or not for a week. Worth knowing, not worth panicking about. (Heavy bright-red bleeding is different — see red flags below.)

The honest truth: none of these predict *when*. First babies come anywhere from 38 to 42 weeks. Your job is to be ready from now, not to decode every symptom.

## 2. Is this labour? The honest sorting system

### Braxton Hicks vs real contractions

| Braxton Hicks | Real contractions |
|---|---|
| Irregular — no pattern | Come at regular intervals |
| Feel like tightening, usually painless | Build in intensity; she can't talk through them |
| Stop when she moves, rests, or drinks water | Keep coming no matter what she does |
| Stay in the front of the belly | Often start in the back and wrap around |

### The 5-1-1 rule (when to act)

Start timing when contractions feel *real* — painful enough that she has to pause what she's doing:

- **5** — contractions are ~5 minutes apart (measured start-to-start)
- **1** — each one lasts ~1 minute
- **1** — this pattern has held for 1 hour

When you hit 5-1-1: call TMC (+65 6250 2222, 24 hours) and plan to head in. First labour — do NOT wait for the classic "every 2–3 minutes" you see in movies. TMC triage will confirm whether to come in or stay home a bit longer; being sent home from triage is normal and not a failure (see CASE ⑨ in ../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md).

### Waters breaking — what to actually do

A gush or a steady trickle she can't control = likely the waters. Note the **time, colour, and amount**:

- **Clear or slightly pink** → call TMC, they'll usually ask you to come in within a few hours even if contractions haven't started.
- **Green or brown** → possible meconium (baby's first stool) → tell TMC and come in promptly.
- **Bright red bleeding** → emergency → 995.

Put a pad on, not a tampon. No bath after waters break (infection risk) — showers are fine. Contractions may start within hours — or not; see "induction" in Chapter 3.

## 3. Kick counts — the one rule that matters most

From 36 weeks, the daily habit: pick a time when he's usually active (often after a meal), and count movements until you reach **10 movements within 2 hours**. That's the standard check.

The rule is absolute and non-negotiable:

> **Reduced or stopped movement = call TMC/the gynae IMMEDIATELY. Do not wait. Do not "see how it goes overnight."**

Movement does NOT naturally slow down right before labour — "he's running out of room so he moves less" is a myth. Pattern change is the thing, not a specific number. If he's quieter than his usual: cold drink, lie on her left side, count for an hour. Still quiet → call. Every midwife would rather see you for a false alarm than the alternative. This is also CASE ② in ../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md.

Your job: be the person who *insists* on calling. She may feel silly. Back her anyway.

## 4. Red flags — call the gynae or TMC about these NOW

Don't time these, don't wait-and-see. Call immediately:

1. **Vaginal bleeding** — bright red, or soaking a pad in an hour or less (CASE ①).
2. **Fluid leaking** that is green/brown, or any leak before 37 weeks.
3. **Regular painful contractions before 37 weeks** (October 11 is your full-term date) — could be pre-term labour (CASE ③).
4. **Severe headache, vision changes (blurry/flashing), or severe upper-abdominal pain** — possible preeclampsia warning (CASE ⑤).
5. **Fever ≥ 38°C**, especially with contractions or fluid leaking.
6. **Reduced fetal movement** (see above — always on this list).
7. **Sudden swelling of face/hands** with any of the above.

Numbers to keep in your phone *today*: TMC main line **+65 6250 2222** (24h — call this when labour starts; the delivery suite is reached through it). Emergency: **995** (ambulance), **999** (police), **1777** (non-emergency ambulance).

## 5. The GBS swab

Between 35–37 weeks the gynae does a routine rectovaginal swab for Group B Streptococcus — standard in Singapore, quick and painless. If positive, she gets IV antibiotics during labour to protect the baby. It changes nothing about the birth plan except the drip in her arm. The week-36 reading in ../weeks/week-36/reading.md covers the evidence on this and cervical ripening — worth your 10 minutes.

## 6b. The last appointments — what actually happens from here

From 36 weeks, checkups go **weekly**. Knowing the routine stops the appointments feeling ominous:

- **Belly measurement + baby's position** (hands on the bump — confirming he stays head-down).
- **Heartbeat check** (doppler — quick).
- **Her blood pressure + urine test** (watching for preeclampsia — this is why you go weekly).
- **Internal exam?** Sometimes, to check cervical dilation/effacement — she can decline if she wants; it doesn't predict timing well.
- **GBS swab** (35–37 weeks — see section 5).

**Your job at appointments:** be there if you can, take notes on your phone (dates, instructions, "call us if…"), and ask the **[confirm with TMC/your gynae]** questions from this chapter while you're in the room with an expert instead of at midnight on Google.

## 6c. Nesting — hers and yours

**Nesting** is the surge of energy where she suddenly needs the nursery perfect *tonight*. It's hormonal, it's normal, and the correct response is to help, not to reason her out of it. Your version of nesting: the checklist in section 6. Channel the anxiety into preparation — that's what this chapter is for.

## 7. Final prep checklist — do this week

These are the "tonight at 10pm" tasks. The night-of playbook (PART 0 and PART 1 of ../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) has the full versions — this is the minimum:

- [ ] **Pre-admission done?** TMC has a pre-admission process — do it BEFORE the day. [confirm with TMC/your gynae for the exact process and whether it's online or in-person].
- [ ] **PART 0 table filled?** Hospital numbers, OBGYN's direct line, insurance details, your IC/passport, her IC/passport. One page, printed or in your phone notes. You will not be thinking clearly at 3am.
- [ ] **Hospital bag by the door.** Full list in PART 1. Minimum: her documents + this chapter's numbers, phone chargers, snacks and water for you, change of clothes for you, baby's going-home outfit, car seat installed if you're driving.
- [ ] **Transport decided.** Driving? Car seat installed and you know the TMC route at night. Grab? Know that a labouring woman + Grab at 3am is fine, but have a backup plan.
- [ ] **Who-to-call list written down.** Not in your head — on paper or a shared note: parents (yours flying in from Jakarta), her family, your boss/HR (leave), confinement help. Decide NOW who gets told when ("call parents when we're admitted, not when contractions start" — whatever you agree).
- [ ] **Deposit money ready.** TMC requires a deposit on admission; check the current amount when you do pre-admission [confirm with TMC/your gynae].
- [ ] **Your own leave sorted.** Know exactly who covers you at work and what you told them.

### If the baby comes TONIGHT — the 5-minute version

1. Time contractions. 5-1-1 → call TMC +65 6250 2222.
2. Waters broke? Note time + colour, call TMC.
3. Grab the bag, the PART 0 page, chargers, snacks.
4. Go. Everything else can wait.

## 7. One thing about money and paperwork (so it doesn't blindside you)

The hospital **reports** the birth to the authorities. **You** (the parents) register the birth online via the **LifeSG app with Singpass** — hospital counters stopped doing it in May 2022. Deadline: **42 days**. Fee: **S$18** including the digital birth certificate. You'll need both parents' passports/NRICs, your marriage certificate, and the baby's name decided.

Two honest notes since both of you are Indonesian work-pass holders:

- **Baby Bonus, CDA, MediSave Grant, and government-paid parental leave all require the child to be a Singapore Citizen.** Your son isn't, so these don't apply to you. Don't budget around them.
- In hospital, the standard newborn jabs — **BCG and Hepatitis B dose 1** — are given before discharge and are included in the TMC maternity packages. Worth confirming the 2026 package prices directly with TMC, since the official sheet I have is from July 2024 (hospital charges only, excluding doctor/paediatrician/anaesthetist fees): normal vaginal 2-day stay ranged from **S$2,960.44** (4-bedded) to **S$10,098.85** (suite). Epidural top-up was roughly **S$390–490** depending on room class. Prices move — confirm.
