# Chapter 9 — Singapore Admin: The 42-Day Paperwork Chain

> Read this in ~12 minutes. As foreign parents, you have a strict 42-day sequence. Nobody chases you — you chase it.

Because you and Ervina are Indonesian work-pass holders, your baby is **not** a Singapore citizen. That changes the paperwork and the benefits. This chapter is the checklist, in order, with everything confirmed as of October 2026. **Confirm everything with ICA / MOM / the embassy before you act — rules change.**

---

## 1. The 42-day chain — in order

The hospital **reports** the birth to the Registrar-General. Everything after that is on you. Late registration is an offence — don't be late.

### Step 1 — Register the birth online (within 42 days)

- **Where:** the **LifeSG app**, with your **Singpass**. (Hospital birth-registration counters stopped in May 2022 — you cannot do this at a counter.)
- **Fee:** **S$18**, which includes the digital birth certificate.
- **Documents you'll need:** both parents' passports/NRICs, your marriage certificate, and **the baby's name**.
- **Decide the NAME before you start the application.** Indonesian naming conventions, spelling, order of names — agree on this *this week*, not at 3am on day 41. A typo here costs weeks.
- ☐ Name decided and spelled out, agreed by both parents
- ☐ Singpass access working (test it now)
- ☐ Marriage certificate located (original or certified copy)

### Step 2 — Indonesian passport via the KBRI embassy

You need the digital birth certificate from Step 1, plus both parents' passports.

- **Book early.** Embassy slots can be days out — don't start this on day 35.
- You'll both likely need to be present (confirm with KBRI Jakarta's Singapore mission — rules change).
- ☐ KBRI appointment booked within the first 2 weeks after birth
- ☐ Parents' passports valid (check expiry dates NOW — if either expires within 6 months, renew first)

### Step 3 — Baby's pass to stay in Singapore (within 42 days)

Without this, your newborn has no legal right to remain in Singapore. This is the hard deadline.

- Apply via **MOM** for a **Dependant's Pass (DP) or Long-Term Visit Pass (LTVP)** for the baby.
- You **need the passport from Step 2 first** — so the chain is: birth cert → passport → pass. No skipping steps.
- ☐ MOM application submitted as soon as passport is in hand
- ☐ Keep copies of every submission and receipt

### Then, as soon as you can

- **Baby health insurance.** As a non-citizen baby, there's no MediSave grant and no automatic coverage — arrange private insurance early. Confirm waiting periods and what's covered for newborns.
- **Her 6-week postnatal check.** Book it before the birth (Chapter 7).

## 2. What does NOT apply to you — plain facts

Your baby is not a Singapore citizen, so the following do **not** apply. No sugar-coating, no resentment — just facts so you don't plan around money that isn't coming:

- **Baby Bonus cash gift** (S$11,000) — citizen children only.
- **CDA First Step Grant** (S$5,000) and **CDA co-matching** (up to S$4,000) — citizen children only.
- **MediSave Grant for Newborns** (S$5,000) — citizen children only.
- **Government-Paid Paternity Leave** — requires a citizen child. What *you* get is whatever your employment contract gives you. Read it this week (see Chapter 8, Section 7).

Her **12-week maternity leave under the Employment Act** is a separate matter — that's an employment right tied to her contract, not citizenship. Confirm the details with her employer.

## 3. Vaccinations — what happens when

### In hospital, before discharge (included in TMC packages)

- **BCG** (tuberculosis) — one dose.
- **Hepatitis B, dose 1** — first dose of the series.

You don't need to do anything — this happens automatically. Just know it's happened.

### After discharge — the National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (NCIS)

Your paediatrician or a GP clinic will follow this. Put the approximate dates in your calendar now:

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| **1 month** | Hep B dose 2 |
| **2 months** | 6-in-1 (diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Hib, Hep B) dose 1 |
| **4 months** | 5-in-1 (diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Hib) dose 2, pneumococcal (PCV) dose 1 |
| **6 months** | 6-in-1 dose 3, pneumococcal (PCV) dose 2 |
| **12 months** | MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) dose 1, varicella (chickenpox) dose 1 |

**Paediatrician follow-ups:**
- **Jaundice check within days of discharge.** Newborn jaundice is extremely common; the doctor checks bilirubin levels and tells you whether it's the normal kind that fades or needs treatment. Don't skip this appointment.
- **1-month review** — weight gain, feeding, development check.

As a non-citizen baby, subsidised vaccination rates may not apply the same way — confirm pricing with the clinic. [Confirm with the clinic — subsidy rules change.]

## 4. Infant care (from 2 months)

ECDA-licensed infant care centres accept babies from **2 months old**.

- **Subsidies:** the Basic Subsidy for a working mother (working ≥56 hours/month) is **S$600/month** for full-day infant care, S$150/month for non-working mothers. An Additional Subsidy of up to **S$710/month** applies for household income ≤S$12,000.
- Subsidy eligibility for non-citizen children is restricted — **confirm with ECDA whether your baby qualifies before budgeting around it.**
- **Waitlists are real.** Good centres near you fill months ahead. Start visiting and registering interest *now*, not when you need the slot.

## 5. Money — get TMC's numbers in writing

The Labour Day Guide covers this in [Part 9.1](../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md). The short version:

- **Get TMC's written bill estimate before the day.** Ask pre-admission for the current 2026 price sheet.
- For reference, their July 2024 sheet listed hospital charges only (before doctor's fees): **2-bedded S$3,265.64**, **1-bedded S$4,152.90**, suites up to **S$10,098.85**. **Confirm current 2026 prices with pre-admission** — do not budget off 2024 numbers.
- As foreign patients, expect to pay a deposit at admission. Know the number in advance.
