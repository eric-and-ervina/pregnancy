# Chapter 10 — Emergencies: Quick Reference

> Read once in ~10 minutes. Then **bookmark this on your phone** and print the numbers box for the fridge. In a real emergency you won't be calm enough to search.

This is education, not medical advice. When in doubt, **call**.

---

## Her — red flags (call 995; don't drive her yourself if she's unstable)

- Soaking a full pad in **an hour** (see Chapter 7 for the full bleeding guide)
- Passing **large clots** (golf ball or bigger), especially with heavy bleeding
- **Fainting**, feeling about to faint, or can't stay conscious
- **Seizure**
- **Severe headache with vision changes** (blurring, spots, flashing lights)
- **Severe upper abdominal pain** (under the ribs, right side especially)
- **Chest pain** or **trouble breathing**
- **Calf pain or swelling** on one side (possible blood clot — do not massage it)
- **Fever ≥38°C with foul-smelling discharge** (possible infection)
- **Thoughts of harming herself or the baby** (call SOS 1-767 or IMH 6389 2222 — now, not tomorrow)

---

## Baby — red flags (call 995 or go to TMC/KKH emergency immediately)

**Fever of 38°C or above in a baby under 3 months = hospital immediately. No waiting, no "let's see."** Newborns can't regulate or fight infection the way older babies do. Take the temperature rectally (most accurate) if you suspect fever.

Also urgent:

- **Breathing difficulty** — breathing fast, noisy, ribs pulling in, nostrils flaring
- **Blue or gray lips, face, or tongue**
- **Poor feeding / refusing feeds** (skips two feeds in a row)
- **Fewer wet diapers** — fewer than 4–6 wet diapers in 24 hours after day 4 (dehydration)
- **Excessively sleepy / hard to wake** — you should always be able to rouse him
- **Seizures or jerky, repetitive movements**
- **Persistent vomiting** (not spit-up — forceful, repeated)
- **Bloody stool**
- **Cord stump:** redness spreading from the stump, swelling, pus, or foul smell
- **Jaundice** spreading to his legs, or getting worse after day 4

Trust your gut. If something feels wrong, go. Nobody at the emergency department will judge a first-time dad for being careful.

## Baby choking — what to do

Newborns choke on milk or mucus. Learn this **before** the birth.

1. **Sit down.** Hold the baby **face-down on your forearm**, head lower than the chest, supporting his jaw and head with your hand (don't press on the throat).
2. **5 back blows** — heel of your hand, firmly, between the shoulder blades.
3. If nothing comes out, turn him **face-up on your other forearm** (head still lower than chest).
4. **5 chest thrusts** — two fingers on the centre of his chest, just below the nipple line, pushing down about 4 cm.
5. **Repeat 5-and-5** until the object comes out or he starts crying/breathing.
6. **Call 995** as soon as you can (shout for someone else to call, or call on speakerphone while you work).

**Never** put your fingers blindly into his mouth to fish something out — you'll push it deeper.

## Infant CPR — the basics

If he's unresponsive and not breathing normally:

1. Lay him on his back on a firm, flat surface.
2. **30 compressions:** two fingers on the centre of his chest, ~4 cm deep, at **100–120 per minute** (think the beat of "Stayin' Alive").
3. **2 breaths:** tilt his head back slightly, lift his chin, cover his nose and mouth with your mouth, and give two gentle breaths (just enough to make his chest rise).
4. **Repeat 30:2** until help arrives or he responds.

Call 995 first (or have someone else call). Keep going — don't stop until paramedics take over.

## Take a first-aid course BEFORE the birth

Reading this page is not the same as doing it with your hands on a dummy. Strongly recommended:

- **Singapore Red Cross Academy — Child First Aid Course for Infant/Child Care Providers (ChFA):** covers infant/child CPR and choking, full certification.
- **Singapore Red Cross — Basic Child and Infant First Aid Workshop:** shorter, parent-friendly, ~S$150.
- **Beloved Bumps — "First Aid for Parents":** parent-focused option.

Book one this week. There are about 3–4 weeks left.
