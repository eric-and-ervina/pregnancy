## 🔍 Symptom Deep-Dive 1: Leg Cramps (Calf & Shin Muscles) ⭐

*She's experiencing this right now — here's the full picture.*

### Why they happen
- The growing uterus presses on the **inferior vena cava** (the big vein returning blood from the legs), slowing circulation and irritating the muscles
- **Electrolyte shifts** — pregnancy changes how the body handles magnesium, calcium, and sodium
- **Muscle fatigue** from carrying extra weight, plus postural changes
- Often worse **at night** and in the **third trimester** — classic timing

### Immediate relief (when a cramp hits)
1. **Stretch it out:** Straighten the leg, then *gently* flex the foot/toes upward toward the shin (dorsiflexion). Hold through the cramp — it's uncomfortable but it stops the spasm fastest. *(Do NOT point the toes down — that worsens it.)*
2. **Massage** the calf and shin muscles gently, working toward the heart
3. **Warmth:** A warm compress or warm bath relaxes the muscle. (No heat if there's any swelling/redness — see red flags below)
4. **Walk it off:** A slow, gentle walk helps blood circulate

### Prevention (daily habits)
- **Stay hydrated** — 2.5–3 L/day is the program's baseline; even mild dehydration triggers cramps
- **Magnesium-rich foods:** dark chocolate, almonds, avocado, bananas, leafy greens, pumpkin seeds (the week-28 program already calls for this — it directly targets cramps)
- **Calcium:** yogurt, cheese, tofu, fortified milk
- **Pre-bedtime calf stretch:** Have her stand an arm's length from a wall, step one foot back, and gently push the back heel into the floor for 30 seconds each side. Do this every night — it's the single most effective prevention
- **Avoid standing for long periods;** take sitting breaks with legs elevated
- **Warm bath before bed** can reduce nighttime cramps
- **Discuss magnesium supplement with her OB** (e.g., magnesium glycinate ~200–400 mg) — evidence is mixed but many women benefit. Never start without provider approval

### 🚨 When a "cramp" is NOT a cramp — DVT red flags
Pregnancy increases blood-clot (deep vein thrombosis, DVT) risk ~5×. **Call the hospital immediately** if the leg shows:

| Normal cramp | ⚠️ DVT warning signs |
|---|---|
| Sudden spasm that releases in minutes | **Persistent** pain or tenderness that doesn't let up |
| Both legs or alternating | **One-sided** swelling (one calf noticeably bigger) |
| No skin changes | Redness, warmth, or a tight/shiny look on one leg |
| No pain when walking normally | Pain when flexing the foot / walking (calf "muscle strain" feeling) |

> If she has any of the right-column signs, this is a **same-day emergency** — call KKH 24-hr line or go to A&E. A simple ultrasound rules it out.

## 🔍 Symptom Deep-Dive 2: Heartburn / Acid Reflux ⭐

*"Feeling not well" that she suspects is heartburn — here's how to confirm and manage it.*

### Why it happens
- **Progesterone** relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter (the valve that keeps stomach acid down) — this is a *hormonal* effect that peaks in the third trimester
- The **growing uterus** pushes the stomach upward, increasing pressure on that valve
- Result: stomach acid creeps up into the esophagus → burning behind the breastbone, sour taste, burping, sometimes nausea

### Lifestyle & diet fixes (try these first)
1. **Small, frequent meals** — 5–6 mini-meals instead of 3 big ones. A full stomach has nowhere to go but up
2. **No lying down within 2–3 hours of eating** — sit upright after meals, and finish dinner early
3. **Elevate the head of the bed** — raise the mattress/pillows 15–20 cm (a wedge pillow works). Gravity is your friend
4. **Sleep on the LEFT side** — anatomically keeps the stomach below the esophagus; also best for circulation
5. **Trigger foods to limit:** spicy, fried, fatty, citrus, tomatoes, chocolate, mint, carbonated drinks, caffeine, onions, garlic. Everyone has different triggers — have her keep a mini food log for 3 days to find hers
6. **Eat slowly, chew well, wear loose waistbands**
7. **A glass of milk or a plain biscuit** can temporarily buffer acid — but don't over-rely on it

### Safe medication ladder (confirm all with her OB/pharmacist)
| Step | Option | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Antacids** (calcium carbonate / Tums, or magnesium-based) | First-line, fast relief. ⚠️ **Don't take within 2 hours of iron/folic acid** — antacids block absorption of both |
| 2 | **Alginate** (Gaviscon) | Forms a protective "raft" over stomach acid; very well-tolerated in pregnancy |
| 3 | **H2 blocker** (famotidine / Pepcid) | If antacids aren't enough — commonly recommended in pregnancy, ask the doctor |
| 4 | **PPI** (omeprazole) | For stubborn reflux; omeprazole has the most human safety data — only on medical advice |

> Heartburn is uncomfortable but **rarely dangerous**. However, **severe upper-abdominal pain under the ribs + headache + visual changes** is NOT heartburn — it can be a sign of **pre-eclampsia**. See Warning Signs below.

## 🔍 Other Common Symptoms: Quick Management Cheat Sheet

| Symptom | What Helps |
|---|---|
| **Swelling (ankles/feet)** | Elevate legs when sitting, walk regularly, avoid standing long, cut back on salty food. ⚠️ *Sudden* swelling of face/hands/feet = pre-eclampsia check |
| **Back / pelvic pain** | Cat-cow, pelvic tilts, warm bath, support belt, avoid heavy lifting, sleep with a pillow between knees |
| **Shortness of breath** | Slow down, sit up straight, practice slow breathing; tell the doctor if it's sudden or severe |
| **Braxton Hicks** | Change position, hydrate, empty bladder. They should be irregular & fade — if they become regular/timed, see warning signs |
| **Insomnia** | Warm bath before bed, pregnancy pillow, breathing practice, limit screens; nap when possible |
| **Hemorrhoids / constipation** | Fiber, water, prune juice, don't strain, warm sitz bath; discuss safe creams with doctor |
| **Fatigue** | Rest is a *task* now, not a luxury. Shorten walks, nap, delegate |
| **Anxiety / overwhelm** | Daily breathing practice, HypnoBirthing audio, journaling, talk it out with partner or doula |
| **Stretch marks / itchy belly** | Moisturize daily; ⚠️ *intense, all-over itching especially on palms/soles* → tell doctor (could be cholestasis, needs a blood test) |

## 🥗 Third Trimester Nutrition

- **Protein:** 80–100 g/day (eggs, chicken, fish, tofu, lentils, yogurt) — supports baby's growth & tissue elasticity
- **Iron:** baby is now building iron stores for its first 6 months — red meat, spinach, beans + vitamin C to absorb. Continue prenatal vitamins & DHA
- **Magnesium & calcium:** dark chocolate, almonds, dairy, leafy greens — directly supports cramp prevention
- **Hydration:** 2.5–3 L/day; add electrolytes (coconut water, banana, a pinch of sea salt)
- **Dates:** 4–6 Medjool dates/day from **week 36** — evidence shows it shortens labor & reduces induction need (check with OB if she has gestational diabetes)
- **Red raspberry leaf tea:** continue 2–3 cups/day as per program
- **Limit:** caffeine <200 mg/day (about 1 small coffee), raw/fish-alcohol risks as usual, very salty food (swelling)

## 🏃‍♀️ Exercise & Movement in the Third Trimester

Continue the weekly physical routines from [`PROGRAM.md`](PROGRAM.md) — they're already adapted for this phase:

- **Daily walking:** 30–40 min (scale back to 20–30 min after week 35)
- **Birth ball / chair hip circles** (no ball needed — week-28 guide has the adapted version)
- **Deep squats, cat-cow, pelvic tilts, forward-leaning inversions** (skip inversions if she has heartburn, high BP, or provider says not to)
- **Perineal massage:** start week 34–35 (3–5×/week, 5 min)
- **Listen to the body:** by week 35+, rest matters more than reps. Fatigue, pelvic pressure, or pubic pain = modify or stop

**Stop exercising and call the doctor if:** bleeding, fluid leak, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling, decreased fetal movement, or regular contractions before 37 weeks.

## 🚨 Warning Signs: When to Call the Hospital IMMEDIATELY

Print this. Keep it on the fridge. Call KKH's 24-hour line for any of these — **do not wait for an appointment:**

### Pre-eclampsia signs (headache version — NOT heartburn)
- **Severe, persistent headache** that doesn't go away with rest/paracetamol
- **Visual changes:** blurring, flashing lights, spots
- **Severe pain under the ribs / upper right belly**
- **Sudden swelling** of the face, hands, or feet
- High blood pressure reading at home

### Preterm labor (<37 weeks)
- **Regular contractions:** 4–6+ per hour, getting closer/stronger
- **Menstrual-like cramping** or low back pain that comes in waves
- **Pressure** in the pelvis/vagina ("the baby is pushing down")
- **Fluid leaking** or gushing (water breaking) — note color, amount, time
- **Vaginal bleeding** (any amount beyond light spotting)

### Other non-negotiables
- **Decreased fetal movement** — fewer than her usual pattern
- **Calf swelling + pain/redness/warmth** in one leg (DVT — see above)
- **Severe dizziness, fainting, chest pain, or sudden breathlessness**
- **Fever** ≥38°C
- **Severe, unrelenting abdominal pain**
- **Frequent vomiting** or inability to keep fluids down

> When in doubt, **call**. That's what the 24-hour line is for. No question is too small — triage nurses are used to everything.

## 👨 Partner's Role in the Third Trimester

Your job shifts from "preparing" to "supporting" as the weeks go on:

- **Take over the mental load:** appointments, logistics, family updates, hospital registration paperwork — she shouldn't have to think about any of it
- **Handle the symptom duty:** midnight leg-cramp massage (stretch → massage → warmth), heartburn snacks, water refills, pregnancy pillow fluffing
- **Do the practical prep:** pack the bag, install the car seat, prep/freeze meals, confirm route to KKH, keep her phone charged
- **Protect her peace:** shield her from "any news yet?" texts, stressful conversations, and pressure
- **Keep practicing your labor skills:** counter-pressure, hip squeeze, relaxation scripts, reading her cues
- **Be her advocate:** practice the phrases from the Week 28 reading — "I need 15 minutes to discuss this with my partner" is a legitimate sentence
- **Take care of yourself too:** eat, sleep, and breathe — you're her anchor, and anchors don't run on empty

## 🧠 Mental & Emotional Corner

- **Expect some anxiety in these final weeks — it's normal.** The breathing practice isn't just for birth; it works for the waiting too
- **Nesting is real:** light organizing feels good; exhausting yourself doesn't. Keep nesting gentle
- **Talk about the "what ifs"** (induction, C-section, NICU time) now, calmly, so they're decisions — not shocks
- **Celebrate the milestones:** full term at 37 weeks is a big one — when you hit it, breathe
- **Remind her (and yourself):** her body has done this. The program has done its job. The rest is trust.
