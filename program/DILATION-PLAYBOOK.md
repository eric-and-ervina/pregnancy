## 1. What Dilation Actually Is (and What Else Is Happening)

**Dilation** is the opening of the cervix, measured 0 → 10 cm. It is the headline number, but it is one of **three** simultaneous processes:

| Process | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Effacement** | The cervix thins from ~4 cm thick to paper-thin (0%) | Often 50–100% effaced *before* much dilation happens, especially in first labours. A cervix can be fully effaced and 1 cm dilated for days |
| **Dilation** | The cervix opens, 0 → 10 cm | The number everyone tracks — but see Section 2's honest math |
| **Station** | Where baby's head sits in the pelvis, −5 (floating above) → 0 (engaged) → +5 (crowning) | Descent is what actually ends the birth. You can be 10 cm with a high head and still have hours of work; you can be 7 cm with a low head and be close |

**The useful reframe:** a labour that is 5 cm dilated but effacing fast and descending well is *further along* than a labour stuck at 6 cm with no descent. Dilation is a scoreboard; **effacement and station are the game.** ([Week 31](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) covers why position controls the mechanics of descent.)

**What physically opens the cervix:** uterine contractions (driven by **oxytocin**) pull the cervix open from the top; the baby's head presses down against it; and the tissue itself must *soften and yield* — the same "release not grip" quality the pelvic floor work has trained since Week 25. That is why every real lever below either (a) supports oxytocin, (b) supports descent, or (c) keeps the tissues soft. And it is why **fear slows dilation in a literal, physiological way**: adrenaline competes with oxytocin, and a clamped jaw pairs with a clamped pelvic floor ("sphincter law" — [PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md) key principles).

## 2. The Honest Timeline — What "Normal" Speed Looks Like

Classic teaching (the 1950s **Friedman curve**) says active labour dilates ~1.2 cm/hour. **Modern research has retired that expectation.** Large contemporary studies (Zhang et al., 2010; the ACOG/SMFM safe-prevention-of-labour consensus, 2014) show normal first labours are **slower and more variable**:

| Phase | Cervix | Realistic pace (first baby) | The honest version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latent | 0 → ~6 cm | Hours to **a day or more**; often 0.5 cm/h or slower | Active labour doesn't reliably start until ~6 cm. Slow here is *normal*, not "failure to progress" |
| Active | ~6 → 8 cm | ~1 cm/h average — but 0.5 cm/h over a couple of hours is within normal | The Friedman-era "active labour starts at 4 cm" caused generations of unnecessary Pitocin |
| Transition | 8 → 10 cm | Fast: 15 min – 1.5 h — the steep part of the curve | The shortest phase; often arrives with the "I can't do this" feeling |
| Pushing | 10 cm → birth | 30 min – 3 h | Dilation is done; this is descent and rotation |

**Two things to write in pen:**

1. **Nobody walks in at 0 cm and has a baby an hour later.** The average first labour is 12–18 hours of labour; "fast" is still half a day. The program's stamina work (walking, squats, rest discipline) exists precisely for this.
2. **Slow ≠ stalled.** A stall is diagnosed by *no meaningful change over hours* with contractions ongoing — not by "not as fast as the textbook." The midwife reads dilation *and* effacement *and* station *and* contraction pattern together. You only get the full picture from her.

> ⚠️ **Cervical checks are information, not homework.** The birth preferences ([LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) §1.4) ask for minimal vaginal exams. Each check is just a snapshot; between checks, progress is read from behaviour — contraction pattern, her energy, the urge to push — not from centimetres.

## 3. What Slows Dilation (The Four Thieves)

Know these cold, because every lever in Section 4 is a counter-move:

| Thief | The mechanism | What it looks like on the day |
|---|---|---|
| **Adrenaline** | Fear/anxiety → catecholamines → oxytocin inhibited → contractions weaken and space out | Bright lights, strangers, "any progress?" chatter, her feeling watched or judged. A labour can visibly *stall at the front door of the hospital* for this reason |
| **Exhaustion** | Fatigue flattens contraction quality — especially a latent phase that ate the whole night | She's been awake 20+ hours at 4 cm. Rest is not a pause in labour; it is often the *engine* of the next phase |
| **Fetal position** | An occiput-posterior (sunny-side-up) baby presents a wider diameter and must rotate while descending — slower, more back pain | Persistent backache, contractions felt mostly in the back, progress that "sticks" at the same number. [Week 31's flowchart](../weeks/week-31/reading.md): hands-and-knees, lunges, asymmetric movement |
| **The full bladder** | Physically blocks descent and dulls contraction efficiency | The most boring cause of slow labour, and the easiest fix. Remind her every hour (partner: this is in your [PART 5 checklist](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md)) |

One more that gets forgotten: **arriving at the hospital too early**. Triage + monitors + bright rooms + interruptions is an adrenaline machine — that's why the 5-1-1 rule and the latent-phase-at-home playbook ([LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) §2.4) protect dilation speed as much as anything else on this page.

## 4. What Speeds Dilation — The Evidence, Ranked

Every lever here is already planted in the program's daily work. This table is the *why*.

### Tier 1 — Strong evidence, do unconditionally

| Lever | Evidence | Where it lives in the program |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuous labour support** (partner/doula) | Cochrane (Bohren et al., 2017): continuous support shortened labour ~40 min on average, reduced caesarean and oxytocin use — the largest behavioural effect on labour length we have | The partner's entire role in [LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) PART 5; doula decision at Week 27 |
| **Freedom to move + upright positions** | Gravity assists descent; squatting widens the outlet ~28% (Russell's radiographic work — [week-31 reading](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) Section 1); movement supports the baby's internal rotation | Weeks 29–31 positions; the [position flowchart](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) printed in the hospital bag |
| **Calm, dark, quiet environment** | Undisturbed birth space keeps adrenaline low so oxytocin can work (the fear-tension-pain physiology from [Session 1](STUDY.md)) | HypnoBirthing daily since Week 25; the environment section of the birth preferences |
| **Empty bladder every 1–2 hours** | Unblocks descent; improves contraction efficiency | Partner reminder in [PART 5](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) §5.2 |
| **Eating light + steady hydration** | Labour is athletic; low fuel flattens contraction quality over hours | The latent-phase-at-home rules (§2.4 of the husband's guide); honey, dates, electrolytes |
| **Rest/sleep in latent labour** | Preserves the energy that drives active labour; overnight dozing at 2–3 cm is a strategy, not a defeat | §2.4 latent playbook; "rest is a task" |

### Tier 2 — Good evidence, use when appropriate

| Lever | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm water immersion / shower (active labour)** | Cochrane: less pain, possibly fewer epidurals; comfort keeps adrenaline down | Get in when surges are established (~5–6 cm guidance, [week-31](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) Section 5); confirm TMC's tub policy |
| **Peanut ball / pelvis-open side-lying** | Associated with shorter labour and fewer caesareans, especially with an epidural | Ask whether the labour ward has one ([week-31](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) §6); pillows are a fine substitute |
| **Vocalisation (low, open "oooooh/aaaah")** | Keeps jaw/pelvic floor released — the sphincter-law mechanism; pairs with breathing to damp adrenaline | Practised since Week 31 |
| **Breathing low and slow (long exhale)** | Parasympathetic activation → oxytocin-friendly state | The 4-in/6–8-out pattern, daily since Week 25 |
| **Nipple stimulation (in established labour or at term overdue)** | Triggers natural oxytocin release — the Ferguson pathway; small studies suggest it can support contractions | **Only with provider approval**, and *not* before term (it can over-stimulate the uterus). Note it exists so you're not surprised if a midwife suggests it; it is not a home remedy to start casually |
| **Position changes every 30 min** | Rotates pressure, promotes rotation/descent, re-engages a stalling pattern | The [week-31 flowchart](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) |

### Tier 3 — Provider-led, evidence-checked (know them, don't self-administer)

| Option | What it is | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| **Membrane sweep** | Provider sweeps the membranes at a cervical check (~39–40 wks) — releases local prostaglandins; modest reduction in going post-term | Ask at the 39/40-week visits (Session 13 follow-up in [STUDY.md](STUDY.md)); mildly uncomfortable, brief |
| **Sex (term)** | Prostaglandins in semen can soften the cervix | Already in [PROGRAM.md](PROGRAM.md) Week 40 overdue list — with provider approval |
| **Walking / curb walking / stairs (term)** | Gravity + pelvic mobility; evidence for *starting* labour is weak, but it supports good positioning and doesn't harm | Program staple since Week 28 |
| **Dates (6/day from 36 wks)** | Evidence targets better cervical *ripeness at admission*, shorter labour and less augmentation (Al-Kuran 2011, Kordi 2017) | The critical window is **now — weeks 36–40**; the honest breakdown is Session 13 |
| **Raspberry leaf tea** | Mixed evidence; may modestly shorten labour by improving uterine tone | 3 cups/day, with the [safety caveats](PROGRAM.md) |
| **Pitocin augmentation** | Synthetic oxytocin for a genuine stall — an *intervention*, not a failure | This is [CASE ⑪](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md)'s ladder; an epidural before it is a reasonable plan, agreed in advance (§5.4) |

### What does NOT speed dilation (skip the folklore)

Castor oil (cramping/dehydration without shortening labour), spicy food, pineapple at any dose that matters, bouncing hard on a ball to "shake baby down," acupressure self-experiments. None has decent evidence, and castor oil in particular can make labour *harder* by exhausting you before it starts.

## 5. Partner Checklist — What You Do, Centimetre by Centimetre

> The numbers are estimates — she won't be checked often. Read the *stage*, do the *checklist*.

### Latent (0 → ~6 cm) — at home
- [ ] **Protect sleep.** Dim room, eye mask, phone silent. If it's night, she rests and you nap within earshot.
- [ ] **Food + fluids** every hour or two: light carbs, electrolytes, honey.
- [ ] **Normal daily movement** when she's up: walks, gentle sway, shower for backache.
- [ ] **Don't fixate on timing.** Check the clock every 20–30 min, write it down, get on with life. Obsessive timing *is* adrenaline.
- [ ] **Hourly self-audit** (from §2.4): waters? bleeding? movement? 5-1-1? If any yes → [PART 2/3](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md).
- [ ] **Hold the gate.** You answer the phone; nobody visits; no "any news?"

### Active (~6 → 8 cm) — in the room
- [ ] **Set the cave**: lights off, playlist on, staff interruptions politely minimised.
- [ ] **Breathe with her** on every surge — in through the nose, long "aaaah" out. Start *before* the wave peaks, not after.
- [ ] **Position changes every ~30 min**, guided by the [flowchart](../weeks/week-31/reading.md): hands-and-knees for backache, lunges for rotation, supported squat for descent.
- [ ] **Counter-pressure / double hip squeeze** on back-labour contractions (practised Weeks 29–30).
- [ ] **Bladder + sips** every hour. Cold cloth on neck. Warm pack on sacrum.
- [ ] **Mantras, not chatter**: "You're doing it." "One at a time." "You're closer than you feel."
- [ ] **Keep your own tank full** — eat, drink, sit when you can. You're the anchor; anchors don't run on empty.

### Transition (8 → 10 cm)
- [ ] **Say the truth early:** "I can't do this" at this stage *means nearly done* — tell her exactly that, every time it comes up.
- [ ] **Less is more.** Short sentences. If she wants silence, be silent and present.
- [ ] **Ride the urges without pushing against an unready cervix** — supported squat or side-lying, breathe *through* the urge, tell the midwife (this is the [week-31](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) pre-push rule).
- [ ] **Expect nausea/shaking** — normal physiology of the steepest dilation, not a problem to fix.
- [ ] **Support whatever position she lands in** — side-lying with pillows, hands-and-knees, peanut ball. Hold steady, don't suggest a "better" idea every contraction.

### Pushing (10 cm →)
- [ ] **Follow the urge, not a count** — open-glottis, low sound out. Dilation is finished; descent is the work now.
- [ ] **Upright or side-lying** per the midwife's lead; be her physical prop.
- [ ] **Slow at crowning** — pant, jaw loose, let the tissue stretch (this is what the Week 34 [perineal work](../weeks/week-34/reading.md) trained for).

## 6. If Dilation Stalls — The Ladder

> Definition first: a **stall** means no meaningful progress in dilation/descent over a couple of hours *with contractions ongoing* — not one slow check, not "not as fast as expected." Slow-but-moving is a labour working; stopped is a labour asking for a change.

**Run the ladder top-down. Steps 1–5 are yours; steps 6+ belong to the care team.**

| # | Action | Why it can restart labour |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Change position** — hands-and-knees, side-lying, upright squat, lunge series | Rotates the baby, re-angles the head against the cervix; many "stalls" are rotation problems ([week-31](../weeks/week-31/reading.md) §7) |
| 2 | **Empty bladder** | The boring fix; physically unblocks descent |
| 3 | **Reset the room** — dim lights, quiet, fewer people, warm water | Drops adrenaline; oxytocin flows again when she feels safe and private |
| 4 | **Rest / doze 20–30 min** if she's deep in the red | Fatigue flattens contraction quality; a short rest with the pelvis open (side-lying + pillows) often restarts stronger contractions |
| 5 | **Refuel** — honey, sips, electrolytes | Contraction quality is metabolic |
| 6 | **Midwife assesses** position, station, contraction pattern — ask the four questions from [CASE ⑪](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md): *What are we trying to fix? How long until the next step? What's the alternative? Is the baby okay?* | The team may offer amniotomy, then oxytocin, in roughly that order |
| 7 | **Decision point** — augment, rest longer, or (rarely) the caesarean conversation ([CASE ⑦](LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md)) | A medically indicated intervention is a *tool*, not a failure of the plan |

**What to say to her if it's slow:** *the labour is doing what labours do; nothing about this is her fault.* Fear-at-the-stall is the one thief that compounds — she needs to hear it isn't a verdict.

**Reframe worth memorising:** "progress" reads in three columns — dilation, effacement, station. A midwife checking "still 6 cm" may also find fully effaced, +1 station, and baby rotated. Ask all three numbers every time a check is done.
