# 🤰 Back Soreness Guide — Week 29
## For the Partner | Practical Relief, Routines & When to Call

> **The short version:** You don't need to be a massage therapist to help. The three most effective things a partner can do for pregnancy back soreness are (1) **counter-pressure** — leaning your weight into her sacrum with the heel of your hand, (2) **the double hip squeeze** — steady inward pressure on her hip bones, and (3) **setting up her body in better positions** — pillow support, warm heat, and gentle daily movement. None of these require skill. They require *consistency, feedback, and your presence*.

---

## 1. Why Her Back Hurts Right Now (29 Weeks)

At 29 weeks, her baby is roughly the size of a butternut squash (~2.9 lb / 1.3 kg) — and she's carrying it all in front. That changes her body in ways that put real strain on her lower back:

| What's happening | How it feels |
|---|---|
| **Growing belly shifts her center of gravity forward** | She leans back slightly to balance — this arches her lower back and tightens the muscles there |
| **Hormone (relaxin) loosens her pelvic joints** | Joints move more than before, so the supporting muscles have to work overtime |
| **Abdominal muscles stretch and weaken** | Less "core" support means the lower back takes over the load |
| **Baby's weight presses down** | Adds constant pressure through the pelvis, sacrum, and low back |
| **Long periods of sitting/standing/sleeping in one position** | Muscles fatigue and stiffen, especially overnight |

**Why your help matters:** Most of this soreness is *muscular fatigue and joint strain* — which responds very well to the simple, non-skill-based techniques in this guide. The rest of it is *positioning* — how she sits, sleeps, stands, and moves throughout the day. Both are things you can directly support.

---

## 2. How to Talk to Her About It (Before You Touch Anything)

Back soreness in pregnancy is normal, but it's also *discouraging* — she's exhausted, her body keeps changing, and it can feel like it will never end. Start with connection, not solutions:

- **Ask an open question:** *"How does it feel today? Sharp or achy? Where exactly?"*
- **Ask before touching:** *"Can I put my hands on your lower back?"* — always, every time. Her body is not yours to grab, even with good intentions.
- **Let her direct:** *"Tell me where it hurts most, and how much pressure feels good."* Let her feedback be the instruction manual.
- **Say the reassuring truth:** *"This is normal at 29 weeks — your body is doing a huge amount of work."*
- **Don't say:** *"You should try this"* a dozen times, or *"It's because you sit too much."* Blame and advice overload both shut her down.

**The rule:** *Her comfort is the goal, not your technique.* If something isn't helping, stop and switch — no drama, no "but the guide said."

## 3. The Three "No-Skill" Partner Techniques

These are the backbone of this guide. They take **two minutes to learn and zero talent.** Practice them on a pillow first, then on her, then ask her to rate the pressure: *"Out of 10 — is this a 3 or a 7?"* Aim for a 6–7: firm and steady, never sharp or painful.

### Technique 1 — Sacral Counter-Pressure (for deep lower-back ache)
This is the #1 most effective comfort measure for pregnancy/labor back pain, and it's literally just *leaning*.

1. She sits on a chair leaning forward onto a table/pillow, or stands leaning over a counter — or lies on her side.
2. Find the **flat triangular bone at the base of her spine** (the sacrum — the bony area just above her tailbone/buttocks).
3. Place the **heel of your hand** flat on it.
4. **Lean your body weight in** — slow, steady, sustained pressure for 30–60 seconds.
5. Move the heel in slow circles or small up-and-down glides if she likes it.
6. Release slowly.

**Pro tips:**
- Use the **heel of your hand**, never your thumb (your thumb will cramp in 30 seconds).
- If your hands tire, use a **tennis ball or a rolled-up towel** against the wall — she leans back into it, you just spot her.
- Alternate hands, or use both palms stacked.

### Technique 2 — The Double Hip Squeeze (for hip + low-back tension)
This one is strangely powerful — it counteracts the pelvis spreading and gives her low back a break.

1. Stand behind her (or beside her if she's side-lying).
2. Place the **heels of your hands on the bony points of her hips** (the iliac crests — the hard "hip shelf" on each side).
3. Squeeze **inward and slightly upward** with steady, firm pressure.
4. Hold through her slow breaths — 3–5 slow breaths, then release slowly.
5. Ask: *"Harder or softer?"* — most women want harder.

### Technique 3 — The "Warm Hands + Still Touch" (for when she just aches)
When she's too tender for pressure, or you're not sure what to do, this is always safe:

1. Rub your palms together until they're warm.
2. Place one hand flat on her **lower back** and one on her **upper back/hip** — *still*, no kneading.
3. Breathe slowly with her — 10 slow breaths. Match your breathing to hers.
4. That's it. Warm, steady, present. Many women find this more soothing than any massage.

> **Skip pressure entirely if:** it makes the pain worse, she has skin irritation, a visible bruise, swelling in that spot, or she just doesn't want to be touched right now. Heat and positions (below) are the backup plan — and sometimes just *sitting with her* is the whole job.

## 4. Massage Without Skill — 6 Foolproof, Safe Moves

If you want to do more than the three techniques, these are **safe for pregnancy and impossible to get wrong.** Keep every move **slow, firm-but-gentle, and on the *muscle*, never directly on the spine.** Use a little warm oil or lotion (almond, coconut, or unscented) if she likes it.

> **Never** massage directly over the spine, the belly, or anywhere that's red, swollen, bruised, or painful to the touch.

1. **The Flat-Palm Warm-Up (60 sec):** Both palms flat on her lower back, one on each side of the spine. Press gently and hold — let your warmth sink in. Then make slow, wide circles with the whole palm. This warms the muscle before any deeper work.

2. **The "OK" Glides (2–3 min):** Make a loose OK sign with each hand (thumb + index finger circle), press the *heel* of your palm beside the spine, and glide slowly **upward from her lower back toward her shoulders**, then back down. Long, slow, rhythmic strokes. Think "warm honey," not "kneading dough."

3. **The Thumb-Weave (only on the muscles BESIDE the spine, 1–2 min):** With the heels of your hands on either side of the spine, walk your thumbs slowly up the two thick muscle bands beside the spine — small circles, gentle pressure, never ON the spine itself.

4. **The Glute Release (1–2 min per side):** The big buttock muscles often hold most of the ache. Use your **knuckles or the heel of your palm** to make slow circles over the fleshy part of the upper buttock (well clear of the spine and tailbone). This is one of the most-relieved spots for pregnancy back pain.

5. **The Side-Lying Hip Sweep (2–3 min per side):** She lies on her side, bottom leg straight, top knee bent. Sit behind her and "walk" your palm slowly down the **side of her upper hip and outer thigh** — from the top of the pelvis toward the knee — with gentle, firm pressure. This balances the two sides of the pelvis.

6. **The Finish (30 sec):** End where you started — flat, warm palms, still and steady, breathing together. A calm ending matters more than a "deep" one.

**Timing guide:** 10–15 minutes is plenty. Evening, after a warm bath, or before bed is ideal. If she falls asleep, that's a win.

## 5. The Daily "Back-Saver" Routine (10 Minutes, Together)

This is the *prevention* half of the guide — the daily habits that stop the soreness from building up. She does the movement, you do the setup, timing, and encouragement.

| When | What | You do |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | Gentle cat-cow on all fours (10 slow reps) — moves the spine and pelvis | Put a towel down, make sure knees are padded, count the breaths |
| **Midday** | 10–15 min walk (conversational pace) | Carry the water bottle, walk with her, slow down if she needs |
| **Afternoon** | 60–90 sec supported deep squat (hold a chair/doorframe) | Stand by as a spotter, offer a hand to get up |
| **Evening** | 5 min pelvic tilts or standing hip circles | Count reps, play her favorite music |
| **Before bed** | Side-lying release (Technique 5 above) or just the warm-hands hold | See Section 4 — it's your 10-minute "shift" |

**Keep it gentle:** Everything should be pain-free. If something hurts, she stops. Movement in pregnancy is about *relief*, not fitness — no bouncing, no straining, no holding breath.

## 6. The "Nest": Setting Up Her Body for Relief

Half of back relief is what her body does *between* the techniques. Set these up for her — this is the practical husband work that matters more than any massage:

### Sleeping (the big one)
- **Side-lying, ideally her left side**, with a pillow **between her knees** (keeps hips level), one **under her belly** (takes the weight off the lower back), and one **behind her back** (prevents rolling onto her back).
- A **full-body pregnancy pillow** does all three jobs at once — worth the money.
- If she's sore in the morning, the pillow setup is usually the first thing to fix.
- ⚠️ Avoid long stretches flat on her back (can compress the big vein and worsen backache/light-headedness).

### Sitting & standing
- **Sitting:** Small pillow or rolled towel behind her lower back; feet flat on a footrest (knees slightly above hips); no crossed legs for long periods.
- **Standing:** One foot on a low step (alternate feet) takes pressure off the lower back; avoid standing still for long stretches — shift weight, walk a few steps every 15 minutes.
- **Lifting:** Never bend at the waist — squat with a straight back, or just hand the heavy stuff to you.

### Heat & cold (the instant fixes)
- **Warm (not hot)** heating pad, warm towel, or microwaved rice sock on the lower back — 15–20 min. Never on the belly.
- **Warm bath or shower** (not hot — avoid raising core body temperature) is often the single best evening relief.
- **Cold pack** wrapped in a cloth (10–15 min) if it feels more like a sharp inflammation than a dull ache.

### Support gear (ask her OB/midwife)
- **Maternity support belt** — can take real weight off the lower back; worth asking about at the next checkup.
- **Supportive shoes** — flats or low heels with arch support beat flat sandals.
- **Referral to a prenatal physiotherapist or chiropractor** — a professional who specializes in pregnancy can teach you both better than any guide.

## 7. Her Self-Care Toolkit (Things You Can Offer, Not Push)

- **Gentle prenatal yoga or stretching** — the Week 29 physical guide has a full lower-back relief flow: cat-cow, pelvic circles, supported squats, side-lying rest.
- **Swimming or water walking** — the water takes the weight off her back entirely.
- **Hydration** — 2.5–3 L/day; muscle cramps and aches are worse when dehydrated.
- **Stay moving but rest often** — 10-minute "reset" breaks: stand, stretch, walk, re-pillow.
- **Ask her provider about** magnesium (supplements can help muscle tension — only with medical approval).

## 8. 🚨 When to Call the Provider (Do NOT wait on these)

Most back soreness is normal. But back pain can also be a sign of something urgent. **If any of these appear, call her OB/hospital now — don't wait for the next appointment:**

| 🚨 Call immediately if… | Why |
|---|---|
| **Back pain that comes in waves** — tightening, regular, or getting stronger | Could be preterm labor contractions (before 37 weeks this is urgent) |
| **Pain accompanied by bleeding or fluid leaking** | Possible labor or membrane rupture |
| **Pain with fever, chills, or burning when urinating** | Possible infection (kidney/urinary) |
| **Sudden or severe pain that doesn't ease with position changes/rest** | Needs assessment |
| **Pain with reduced baby movement** | Needs immediate fetal assessment |
| **Pain plus a sudden severe headache, vision changes, or swelling of face/hands** | Could be pre-eclampsia |
| **Leg pain/swelling in ONE leg, especially calf** | Could be a blood clot (DVT) — needs urgent check |

**When in doubt, call.** The triage line exists for exactly these questions. It's always better to be told "everything's fine" than to wait.

## 9. The Partner's Cheat Sheet (Print This Section)

**Before touching anything:** Ask *"Where does it hurt?"* → ask *"Can I touch?"* → let her direct the pressure.

1. **Sacral counter-pressure** — heel of your hand on the flat bone at the base of her spine, lean in, hold 30–60 sec
2. **Double hip squeeze** — heels of hands on her hip bones, squeeze in and slightly up, 3–5 breaths
3. **Warm hands + still touch** — warm palms on lower back, breathe with her, 10 slow breaths

**The daily routine:** Cat-cow in the morning → midday walk → supported squat → evening pelvic tilts → side-lying release before bed.

**The golden rules:**
- Consistency beats intensity — 10 gentle minutes daily beats one "pro" massage a week
- Her feedback is the instruction manual — pressure should be a firm 6–7/10, never painful
- Her comfort is the goal, not your technique — if it's not helping, switch
- You're not fixing her — you're walking through this with her. That's the whole job

## 10. One Last Thing

She's 29 weeks pregnant, she's growing a whole human, and her body is doing something extraordinary while being stretched, loosened, and loaded in ways it's never been before. Back soreness is the price of that — and it's temporary. The baby will come, the weight will lift, and the ache will fade.

What she'll remember from this time isn't whether your massage was "good." She'll remember that you showed up, that you asked, that you tried, that you were *there* — with your hands, your patience, and your presence. That's the whole guide, really.

**You've got this. She's got this. 💛**
