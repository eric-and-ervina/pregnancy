# Week 29 Exercise & Yoga Guide — Back-Pain-Safe Version

**An alternative to the regular Week 29 physical guide for days when severe back pain makes the usual routine feel impossible.**

This version keeps the same Week 29 goals — **gentle movement, baby positioning, and pelvic floor release** — but strips out everything that loads, twists, or jolts the back. When the pain settles, you can mix pieces of the [regular guide](physical.md) back in. When it's flaring, *this is the guide*.

> **Safety first — please read this twice:** Severe back pain in pregnancy deserves a conversation with your OB/midwife. Use this routine only with their clearance, and **stop and call your provider immediately if** back pain comes in waves (tightening, regular, getting stronger), is sudden/severe and doesn't ease with rest, or comes with bleeding, fluid leaking, fever, reduced baby movement, headache/vision changes, or one-sided calf swelling. See the [Back Soreness Guide](back-soreness-guide.md) for the full red-flag list.

> **The golden rule for this version:** *No pain through gritted teeth.* If a movement makes the back pain worse — even a little — skip it. Relief is the goal, not finishing the routine. A day where you only breathe, apply heat, and rest is a *successful* day on this plan.

---

# 🗓️ How to Use This Version (3 Tiers)

| Your day | What to do |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Severe pain day** | Only the Comfort Toolkit (Section 4): heat, warm shower, side-lying rest, pelvic floor release breathing, partner's warm hands. No walking, no "exercises." |
| 🟡 **Moderate pain day** | Add short, pain-free movement: 5–10 min walk or chair/standing mobility (Section 2), side-lying release with partner. |
| 🟢 **Better day (pain mostly settled)** | Add the full Back-Relief Flow (Section 3) and a 10–15 min walk. Still skip anything that hurts. |

**You can move between tiers day to day — or hour to hour.** That's the design, not a failure.

| Day | Main Focus |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Short walk (as tolerated) + chair/standing mobility |
| Day 2 | Side-lying rest + pelvic floor release breathing + partner release |

| Day 4 | Side-lying rest + partner release (the "lazy" day is allowed) |

| Day 6 | Optional: warm shower mobility + very gentle stretch |

**Totals (aims, not rules):** Walking 10–15 min, 2–4×/week only if pain-free · Chair/standing mobility 10 min, 3×/week · Back-Relief Flow 15–20 min, 2–3×/week · Pelvic floor release breathing daily · Side-lying rest daily.

# 🚶 1. Walking (only if it helps)

- **Short and split:** 10–15 minutes total, in 5–10 minute chunks. Two short walks beat one long one.
- **Flat, even ground only.** No hills, no stairs, no curbs this week.
- **The test:** If your back feels better or the same after 5 minutes, continue slowly. If it feels worse, stop and go to the Comfort Toolkit — walking is optional this week.
- **Pace:** Slow, conversational, hips swaying naturally. Don't "power through."
- **Instead of walking on bad days:** stand at the kitchen counter and gently rock side-to-side or in slow circles for 2–3 minutes — the counter takes the weight off your back while you still get gentle pelvic movement.

**Why it's still here:** Movement helps many pregnant backs by keeping the pelvic joints from stiffening — but only when it doesn't hurt. When it hurts, rest wins.

---

# 🪑 2. Chair & Standing Mobility (Back-Sparing Strength)

All of these keep your spine supported and neutral — nothing here bends, twists, or loads the lower back. Do **2–3 rounds, only what feels good.**

### M1. Seated Marching (chair)
- Sit tall on a sturdy chair with a small pillow behind your lower back. Feet flat, hands on thighs or the chair sides.
- Slowly lift one knee 2–4 inches, lower, then the other. **10 per side, slow.**
- Keeps the legs gently active with zero back load.

*Why:* Gentle circulation and leg stamina without asking your back to hold you up.

### M2. Seated Pelvic Tilt (chair)
- Sit tall. Gently tuck your tailbone and press your lower back toward the chair back, then release. **10 slow reps.**
- Tiny movement, big relief — this is the chair version of cat-cow.

### M3. Standing Calf Raises (at the counter)
- Hold the counter with both hands, feet hip-width apart.
- Rise slowly onto your toes, hold 1 second, lower slowly. **10–12 reps.**
- *Skip if standing still aggravates your back.*

### M4. Supported Wall Lean (hip flexor release)
- Stand facing the counter (or a wall). Step one foot back into a gentle lunge **with hands on the counter/wall** so your torso stays upright and supported.
- Press the back heel down and feel a gentle stretch in the front of the hip. **3 slow breaths per side.**
- *Only as deep as feels good — this is a stretch, not a workout.*

### M5. Side-Lying Clamshell (small range)
- Lie on your side, knees bent, pillow between knees and a pillow under your belly.
- Open the top knee only **a few inches** — small range, no hip rolling. **10 per side.**
- *If your back complains, skip — there are no required exercises this week.*

### M6. Wall Push-Up (high incline)
- Stand an arm's length from a wall, hands shoulder-width at chest height.
- Bend elbows and lower chest toward the wall, keeping your body in one line. **2 sets of 8–10.**
- *If your back aches in the plank-like position, do this seated or skip it.*

# 🧘 3. The Back-Relief Flow (15–20 minutes)

A gentle sequence built entirely around positions that **take weight off the lower back** — seated, side-lying, and counter-supported. Do it on moderate/better days, or cherry-pick 2–3 moves on bad days.

### 1. Seated Breathing with Back Support (3 min)
Sit in a chair with a pillow behind your lower back, feet flat, hands resting on your belly. Inhale 4 counts through the nose, exhale 6–8 through the mouth. Let the back rest against the support — you don't have to "sit up straight" today.

### 2. Seated Cat-Cow (10 slow reps)
Sitting tall with hands on your knees: inhale, gently arch and lift the chest; exhale, round the spine and tuck the chin. Move only as far as is comfortable — the spine barely needs to move for this to help.

### 3. Standing Pelvic Circles at the Counter (2 min each direction)
Hold the counter with both hands, feet hip-width. Circle the hips slowly — forward-side-back-side — like a hula hoop. The counter carries your weight so the back can stay soft.

### 4. Side-Lying Knee Drops (5 slow breaths per side)
Lie on your side with both knees bent and a pillow between them. Slowly roll the top knee open toward the ceiling a few inches, then lower with control. This is a tiny, back-friendly hip opener.

### 5. Optional: Elevated Cat-Cow (hands on a chair seat)
If being on all fours is comfortable for your back (it's a relief for some, agony for others): kneel with your hands on a chair seat or couch cushion instead of the floor — this raises your torso and takes strain off your wrists and lower back. 5–10 slow reps.

### 6. Optional: Supported Child's Pose (60–90 sec)
Knees wide, big toes together, folded blankets under your chest and forehead so your belly and upper body are fully supported. If folding forward pulls on the back, skip this — the side-lying rest below is the substitute.

### 7. Pelvic Floor Release Breathing, Side-Lying (5 min)
Lie on your left side, pillow between knees and under your belly. Inhale — imagine the pelvic floor softening and widening. Exhale long and slow — let go completely, jaw and shoulders dropping. This is *release* work, not Kegel work. (Full instructions in the regular guide, Section 7.)

### 8. Side-Lying Rest (3–5 min)
Stay where you are, breathe slowly, let your partner put a warm hand on your lower back. **This is a complete yoga session on a bad-pain day. Resting is the pose.**

# 🌡️ 4. The Comfort Toolkit (for severe pain days)

When the back is screaming, these are your exercises. Do them as often as needed, in any order.

- **Heat (not hot):** Warm heating pad, warm towel, or microwaved rice sock on the lower back for 15–20 min. Never on the belly.
- **Warm shower or bath (not hot):** Let the water run over your lower back. Keep it warm — avoid raising core temperature.
- **The pillow nest:** Side-lying on your left side with a pillow between the knees, one under the belly, one behind the back. Fix the nest before anything else.
- **Pelvic floor release breathing** (Section 3, #7) — 3–5 minutes, lying down.
- **Partner's warm hands:** 10 slow breaths with warm palms resting still on your lower back. No massage required.
- **Counter-pressure and the double hip squeeze** — the two partner techniques from the [Back Soreness Guide](back-soreness-guide.md). For many women with back pain, these bring more relief than any stretch.
- **Stand, shift, reset:** If you've been sitting or standing in one position, take a 2-minute "reset" — stand and rock gently at the counter, or re-pillow and roll to the other side.
- **Ask your provider about:** a maternity support belt, a prenatal physiotherapist referral, and magnesium. Don't self-prescribe — ask.

# 🤱 5. Baby Positioning (light version)

The full daily positioning routine from the regular guide is a **lot** with severe back pain. This lighter version keeps the baby-positioning goal without the strain. Do what you can, when you can.

1. **Side-Lying Release with partner (5 min per side)** — *this is now your #1 positioning move.* It releases hip tension, balances the pelvis, and happens lying down with pillows. Step-by-step: regular guide Section 5, or the Back Soreness Guide's *Side-Lying Hip Sweep* (Section 4, move 5).
2. **Standing pelvic circles at the counter (2 min)** — as in the Back-Relief Flow.
3. **Optional: Counter-Lean Inversion (30–60 sec × 2, only if it feels good):** Instead of the couch-edge version, lean forward over a kitchen counter or high table with your hands on it and let your belly hang. This takes the weight off your lower back *and* gives the baby gravity room. If it pulls or aches, skip it — no guilt.
4. **Pelvic floor release breathing (3 min).**
5. **Rest in the pillow nest.**

> **Skip the Forward-Leaning Inversion entirely if:** high blood pressure, severe heartburn/reflux, dizziness, recent heavy meal, provider says not to — or it just makes your back worse. This week, back comfort outranks positioning.

---

# 👨 6. Your Partner's Role This Week (it's bigger than usual)

On a severe-back-pain week, **your partner is the primary "equipment."** These are the highest-value things they can do:

- **The warm-hands hold** and **sacral counter-pressure** — the no-skill moves from the [Back Soreness Guide](back-soreness-guide.md). Ask first, let her direct pressure, aim for a firm-but-comfortable 6–7/10.
- **The pillow nest, every time:** between knees, under belly, behind back. Rebuild it whenever she moves.
- **Walk with her** on the short walks — carry the water, set the pace, don't push for more.
- **Set up the counter station:** clear a spot at the counter for the pelvic circles and counter-lean.
- **Do the Side-Lying Release** most evenings — it's both a back-pain treatment and this week's positioning work.
- **The most important job:** when she says it hurts, believe her, and let her stop. No "but the guide says."

# ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week (back-pain edition)

- **Any movement that increases the back pain** — this overrides every other rule.
- Deep squats, wall-sits, lunges, deadlifts, clamshells with big range.
- Hills, stairs, curbs, long or brisk walks.
- Twisting movements and deep forward folds.
- Lying flat on your back.
- Long stretches of standing still *or* sitting still.
- Bending at the waist to pick things up — squat with a straight back or hand it to your partner.
- Pushing through: no "no pain, no gain" this week — or ever in pregnancy.

# 🚨 When to Call the Provider (do not wait)

Most back pain at 29 weeks is muscular and joint strain — but some is urgent. **Call now, don't wait for the next appointment, if any of these appear:**

| Call immediately if… | Why |
|---|---|
| Back pain **in waves** — tightening, regular, getting stronger | Could be preterm labor contractions |
| Pain with **bleeding or fluid leaking** | Possible labor or membrane rupture |
| Pain with **fever, chills, or burning when urinating** | Possible kidney/urinary infection |
| **Sudden or severe** pain that doesn't ease with rest/position changes | Needs assessment |
| Pain with **reduced baby movement** | Needs fetal assessment |
| Pain with **sudden severe headache, vision changes, or face/hand swelling** | Could be pre-eclampsia |
| Pain with **one-sided calf swelling** | Could be a blood clot (DVT) |

When in doubt, call the triage line — that's what it's for.

---

# 🌟 The Bottom Line

Severe back pain doesn't mean zero movement — it means *different* movement. A few minutes of pelvic floor breathing, a warm towel, a pillow nest, and your partner's hands on your lower back **is a complete workout on a bad day.** The regular Week 29 routine will still be there when the pain settles; today's job is just to be comfortable and let your body rest.

You're not falling behind. You're adapting — which is exactly the skill this program is trying to build.
