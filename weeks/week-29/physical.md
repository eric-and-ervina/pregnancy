# Week 29 Exercise & Yoga Guide — No Birth Ball, No Bands Needed

The good news first: **you don't need a birth ball or resistance bands for Week 29.** Everything below uses your body weight, a wall, a sturdy chair, a sofa/couch edge, and — for one technique — your partner. Week 29 is about three things: **building leg stamina, sustained hip opening, and the daily positioning routine** that keeps giving your baby room to settle into the best seat.

> **Safety first:** Get clearance from your OB/midwife before starting or continuing this routine. Stop and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, strong regular contractions, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling, or reduced baby movement. Avoid lying flat on your back, deep twists, hot environments, and any movement that causes pain.

# 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking (35 minutes, 5–6 days)

- Walk at a **conversational pace** — you should be able to talk in full sentences.
- Add **gentle hills or stairs** if they feel comfortable; they build the leg endurance labor demands.
- Keep shoulders relaxed, swing your arms, wear supportive shoes.
- If 35 minutes feels like a lot, split it: 20 min morning + 15 min evening.
- **Form cue:** think "tall through the crown, tailbone heavy, knees soft." Let your hips sway naturally.

**Why:** Walking uses gravity to encourage the baby head-down, keeps the pelvic joints moving, and is the single best stamina builder you have.

# 🏋️ 2. Strength Work (3 sessions/week)

## Strength A — Squats, Hinges, Push

### A1. Supported Deep Squat (hold)
- Stand facing a sturdy chair back, doorframe, or heavy piece of furniture; hold on for balance.
- Feet slightly wider than hips, toes turned out a little.
- Lower slowly into a comfortable squat — **only as deep as feels good**.
- Keep heels down (place a rolled towel under them if they lift) and chest lifted.
- **Hold 30–60 seconds**, breathing slowly. Work toward 90 seconds across the week.
- Release by pressing through your heels and standing slowly.

*Modification:* Wall-sit — back flat on a wall, slide down until thighs are parallel or slightly above.

### A2. Wall Push-Ups (incline)
- Stand arm's length from a wall, hands shoulder-width apart at chest height.
- Bend elbows and lower your chest toward the wall; keep your body in one straight line.
- Press back up. **2–3 sets of 10–12.**

*Why:* Maintains upper-body strength for upright labor positions and carrying the baby later.

### A3. Sit-to-Stand Squats
- Sit on the edge of a sturdy chair, feet flat, knees over ankles.
- Stand up slowly without using your hands, then sit back down with control.
- **3 sets of 10–12.** Keep your chest up and exhale as you stand.

## Strength B — Glutes, Hips, Back

### B1. Lateral (Side) Lunges
- Step wide to the side, bending the stepping knee while the other leg stays straight.
- Push back to center. **8–10 per side, 2–3 sets.**
- Hold a chair or wall for balance if needed.

*Why:* Opens the pelvic outlet asymmetrically — exactly the kind of space a rotating baby needs.

### B2. Household-Item Romanian Deadlifts
- Hold two heavy water bottles, a filled backpack, or light dumbbells in front of your thighs.
- Push your hips *back* (as if closing a car door with your glutes), back flat, knees soft.
- Feel the stretch along the back of your legs, then squeeze glutes to stand.
- **3 sets of 10–12.**

*Why:* Strengthens the posterior chain that holds you upright as the belly grows.

### B3. Clamshells (no band needed)
- Lie on your side, knees bent to 45°, feet together, head on your arm.
- Open the top knee as high as comfortable **without rolling your hips back**.
- Hold 3 seconds at the top, then lower slowly. **15 per side, 2 sets.**

*Why:* Builds the gluteus medius — your pelvis's side stabilizer during walking and labor.

# 🧘 3. Prenatal Yoga (2 sessions/week — Sustained Hip Openers)

Each session: **25–35 minutes**. The theme this week is *holding* the openers — 5+ slow breaths per pose — so the pelvic floor learns to release under sustained pressure.

### Yoga A — Hip Opening Flow

1. **Seated breathing (3 min):** Sit on a cushion or chair, long spine. Inhale 4 counts through the nose, exhale 6–8 through the mouth. Drop your shoulders and jaw.
2. **Cat-Cow (10 reps):** On all fours; inhale gently lift chest and sit bones, exhale round the spine. Move with your breath.
3. **Low Lunge with hip circles (5 breaths each side):** Step one foot forward, back knee down or lifted; make slow circles with your hips; switch sides. Hold a chair/wall if needed.
4. **Standing Figure-Four (30–60 sec each side):** Stand facing a wall or chair; place one ankle over the opposite knee; sit hips back slightly with a long spine; feel the outer hip open. This is the wall-safe pigeon substitute.
5. **Seated Butterfly (2 min):** Soles together, knees out; sit tall; breathe into the inner thighs. Sit on a folded blanket so hips are above knees. Optional: gentle forward fold from the hips, never forcing.
6. **Supported Goddess Squat (30–45 sec × 2):** Wide stance, toes out; lower into a comfortable squat holding a chair or wall; chest lifted; slow breaths.
7. **Child's Pose (60–90 sec):** Knees wide, big toes together; fold forward, arms forward or by your sides; breathe into the lower back and pelvic floor.
8. **Side-Lying Rest (3 min):** Lie on your left side, pillow between knees; let the breathing slow down.

### Yoga B — Lower Back Relief + Positioning

1. **Seated breathing (3 min)** — as above.
2. **Cat-Cow (10 reps)** — as above.
3. **Standing Pelvic Circles (2 min each direction):** Hands on hips (or on a counter), circle the hips slowly like a hula hoop — your birth-ball substitute.
4. **Supported Deep Squat hold (60 sec × 2):** Doorframe or partner-supported, as in Strength A1.
5. **Low Lunge with side bend (5 breaths each side):** Step forward into a lunge, raise the opposite arm overhead, gently side-bend toward the front leg.
6. **Seated Wide-Leg Fold (5–8 breaths):** Sit with legs wide on a cushion; hinge forward only slightly, keeping the back long. No forcing.
7. **Pelvic Floor Release breathing (5 min):** Sit or lie on your side. Inhale, imagine the pelvic floor softening and widening; exhale long and slow, letting go. The *release* is the point.
8. **Side-Lying Rest (3 min).**

# 🔄 4. The Daily 15-Minute Positioning Routine (Spinning Babies–style)

Do this **every day** — it's the heart of Week 29.

### Step 1 — Cat-Cow (5 min)
10 slow reps on all fours, moving with your breath. Mobilizes the spine and pelvis.

### Step 2 — Forward-Leaning Inversion (3 min)
- **Setup:** Kneel at the edge of a couch or firm bed (or low table). Place your hands on the floor in front of you.
- Slowly walk your hands forward and lower your chest and head toward the floor, letting your belly hang heavy and relaxed.
- **Hold 30 seconds**, breathing deeply. Rest 30–60 seconds. Repeat **3 times**.
- Then walk your hands back up to kneeling, slowly.

> **Skip this if:** high blood pressure, severe heartburn/reflux, recent heavy meal, dizziness, or provider says not to. Have your partner spot you the first few times.

*Why it works:* Gravity creates space in the lower uterus so the baby can tuck its chin and roll its back forward.

### Step 3 — Deep Squat Hold (2 min)
Supported, heels down, 60–90 seconds total (can split into two holds). Breathe into the pelvic floor — jaw relaxed, low soft "ahhhh" on the exhale.

### Step 4 — Side-to-Side / Figure-8 Hip Rocks (2 min)
On all fours or standing holding a counter: sway the hips side to side, then trace slow figure-8s. Asymmetrical movement opens one side of the pelvis at a time.

### Step 5 — Pelvic Floor Release Breathing (3 min)
Sit comfortably. Inhale — imagine the pelvic floor softening and widening. Exhale long and slow — let go completely. This is *release* work, not Kegel work. Full step-by-step in Section 7 below.

# 👨 5. Partner Technique: Side-Lying Release (2–3 min per side)

Do this after the positioning routine, or in the evening before bed.

1. **You (mama):** Lie on your side with your bottom leg straight, top knee bent, head on a pillow, belly supported by a pillow if you like.
2. **Partner:** Sit behind you. Place one hand on your top hip (iliac crest).
3. **Partner:** With the other hand, use gentle but firm pressure to slowly "walk" down along the side of your upper hip and outer thigh — from the top of the pelvis toward the knee — releasing tension as they go.
4. **You:** Breathe slowly and let the hip soften. Keep the pressure *firm and slow*, never painful.
5. **Switch sides** and repeat.

**Why:** This balances the two sides of the pelvis, which helps a baby that favors one side (or sits slightly asynclitic) find a straighter, more centered path.

**Solo alternative:** Lie on your side and place a small massage ball (or rolled-up towel) under the upper hip, letting your weight sink into it for 30–60 seconds per side. Move it slowly along the outer hip and thigh.

# 👐 6. Abdominal Release (2–3 min per side)

**What it does:** Gently releases tension in the tissue around your belly — the light-touch, fascia-release counterpart to the Side-Lying Release. It softens the "pull" that can twist the uterus or tip the baby off-center.

**When to do it:** When your belly feels tight or lopsided, or daily before the positioning routine.

**Partner-assisted:**
1. Lie on your side (or sit comfortably propped up). Partner sits beside you, facing your belly.
2. Partner places one flat hand on the fullest/tightest side of your belly — often where the baby's back or bottom presses. The other hand rests on top.
3. Partner applies **light, steady pressure** — just the weight of the hands, like a warm towel. *Not* deep massage, *not* pressing into the baby.
4. You breathe slowly into the belly. On each exhale, feel the tissue under their hands soften and widen.
5. Hold **2–3 minutes** on the tight side, then 1 minute on the other side for balance.
6. Partner lifts hands slowly; take a few normal breaths before moving.

**Solo alternative:** Lie on your side with a small cushion or rolled-up towel tucked under the fullest side of the belly. Breathe into that spot for 2–3 minutes, letting the belly rest heavy on the support.

**Stop and reposition (or skip the day) if:** you feel pressure on the baby, discomfort, or any contraction-like tightening.

---

# 🌬️ 7. Pelvic Floor Release Breathing (daily, 5 minutes)

This is the *release* side of pelvic floor work — the partner to the "elevator squeeze" you practiced in earlier weeks. For birth, release matters more: a soft, long pelvic floor gives the head a sloped landing pad instead of a braced shelf.

1. **Position:** Sit comfortably (cushion, chair edge, or birth ball) or lie on your side. Long spine, soft shoulders and jaw.
2. **Inhale** slowly through the nose. Imagine the breath traveling down: lungs → belly → pelvic floor. As it arrives, picture the pelvic floor **softening, widening, bulging gently downward** — a hammock being loaded, not tensed.
3. **Exhale** long and slow through the mouth (6–8 counts). Keep the pelvic floor soft and open — *no Kegel*. If you catch yourself squeezing, consciously let go.
4. **Repeat 3–5 minutes.** With each exhale add more release: jaw drops, shoulders drop, sit bones widen.
5. **Finish:** Normal breath, gentle hip wiggle. Notice how the pelvic floor feels heavier and more relaxed than when you started.

**Release check:** One hand on your belly, one under your sit bones. On the inhale you should feel the belly rise and the pelvic floor soften against your hand. If you feel a lift/squeeze instead, exhale and drop it — "let it go like a heavy elevator to the ground floor."

# 🪜 8. Stair Climbing (2–3 flights, slow)

- Use a sturdy railing. Step up slowly, one step at a time, breathing normally.
- **2–3 flights total**, a few times a week (or on your walking route).
- If stairs aren't accessible, do 2–3 minutes of slow step-ups onto a low, sturdy step instead.

*Why:* Stair climbing opens the pelvis asymmetrically and builds the leg endurance you'll want for upright labor.

# ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- Holding your breath during effort — exhale on the work.
- Deep, aggressive stretching (relaxin makes joints loose; move slowly).
- Lying flat on your back for long periods.
- Overheating (no hot yoga, no hot baths).
- High-impact jumping or bouncing.
- Deep twists that compress the belly.
- Any movement that causes pain, pubic pressure, dizziness, or contractions — stop and rest on your side.

---

# 🌟 The Bottom Line

Week 29 is not about doing more — it's about **holding space longer**. Longer squats, longer breaths, longer releases, and a daily conversation with gravity that keeps telling your baby: *there's room, take your time, I'm ready.*

You do not need a birth ball, resistance bands, or a gym. You need a wall, a chair, a couch edge, a partner's hands, and fifteen quiet minutes a day. Consistency wins.

---

*Always listen to your body. Modify any movement that doesn't feel right, and clear new routines with your OB/midwife.*
