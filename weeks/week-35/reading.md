## 2. The Head-Down Milestone — What the Scan Actually Told You

Congratulations, and let's be precise about it, because "head down" carries more relief than it should and less information than you'd hope.

**What cephalic means.** The baby's head is at the bottom of the uterus, pointing toward the birth canal — the position the vast majority of term babies settle into, and the one that makes a straightforward vaginal birth possible. Your provider will write it as **cephalic** or **vertex** (vertex = head down, chin tucked to the chest, the ideal). At 34 weeks, roughly **three in four** babies are already head down; the rest *usually* turn over the following weeks. Yours has already done it, five or six weeks early.

**What it means, materially:**

| The scan confirms | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Not breech, not transverse** | The two positional scenarios that would have opened up ECV (manual turning), breech birth, or planned cesarean conversations are now off the table |
| **The program's positioning work has done its job** | The forward-leaning inversions, curb walking, hands-and-knees rocking and ball sitting since Week 29 exist largely to *encourage* exactly this. She's in position |
| **The pelvic environment is cooperating** | The head is engaged with (or heading into) the pelvis — the space it will travel through |

**What it does *not* mean** — and this is where a lot of couples relax too early:

- **Head-down ≠ occiput anterior (OA).** The baby can be head-down facing your spine (**OA** — shortest, easiest labour; back is at the front, so the smallest head diameter leads) or head-down facing your tummy (**OP** / occiput posterior — the "sunny-side up" or *back labour* position, which is typically longer and more painful). The scan at 34 weeks tells you which end is down; it doesn't reliably tell you which way she's *facing*, and babies can still rotate in the final weeks and even during labour.
- **Head-down ≠ engaged.** "Lightening" / engagement — the head dropping into the pelvis — often happens around **36 weeks in a first pregnancy**, and you'll feel it as *easier breathing but more pressure and bathroom trips*. Before then, she may still be floating. Head-down is the prerequisite; engagement is the next milestone.
- **Head-down is not a promise about the day.** Position is one lever among many. Babies who are confidently OA still have labours that vary. This is a reason for relief, not complacency.

**So what do you do with this news?** Nothing dramatic — that's the point. The positioning habits continue because they now have a *sharper* job: not "get her head down" but **"keep her OA and help her settle into the pelvis."** Section 3 is exactly that.

## 3. Keeping Her Optimal — Why the Positioning Work Doesn't Stop

The Week 34 news changes the *goal* of the positioning work, not its presence. The habits you've built since Week 29 now serve three jobs.

**Job 1 — encourage anterior rotation (OA).** If she's cephalic but posterior, the same daily habits that got her head down can still flip her to face your spine: hands-and-knees rocking, leaning forward over the birth ball, curb walking, and avoiding long stretches reclined on your back or slouched in a deep, knees-high sofa. The rule the program has repeated all along — **knees lower than hips whenever you sit** — now applies with more force, because the last weeks are the ones where rotation is decided.

**Job 2 — encourage engagement and descent.** The forward-leaning inversion, deep squat, and the gentle ball bounce all coax the head deeper into the pelvis. Descent (station) is the metric that actually ends the birth ([`DILATION-PLAYBOOK.md`](../../program/DILATION-PLAYBOOK.md) §1) — a baby 10 cm dilated but still high is not close; a baby 7 cm with a well-descended head is.

**Job 3 — keep the pelvis mobile.** The pelvic joints are their most relaxed now under relaxin. Curb walking, the asymmetric lunges, and the figure-8s on the ball keep the sacroiliac joints moving so the birth canal can widen when it needs to.

**What to keep daily, unchanged:** cat-cow, deep squat hold, forward-leaning inversion (if not contraindicated), curb walking, hands-and-knees rocking, and the Daily Ball Block. Nothing is dropped just because she's head-down.

> ⚠️ **If the head-down position is new or borderline, ask at this week's visit:** *"Is the head well engaged, and is she OA or posterior?"* Knowing whether you're maintaining an ideal position or still trying to improve one changes nothing about the routine — but it changes how you read the first hours of labour, and the answer goes in the antenatal booklet.

## 5. Part 2: The Toolkit, Technique by Technique

These are the same physical skills the program has taught since Week 29 — now assembled into one reference he can use without asking. **[`physical.md`](physical.md) §6 rehearses every one of them.**

| Technique | When she'll want it | How he does it |
|---|---|---|
| **Sacral counter-pressure** ([Week 29](../week-29/reading.md)) | Back labour / OP pressure; any strong contraction | Heel of the hand (or a tennis ball) on the sacrum — the flat bone at the base of the spine — firm, steady pressure *into* her, leaning his weight in. Hold through the whole surge, release on the exhale |
| **Double hip squeeze** ([Week 30](../week-30/reading.md)) | Active labour; contraction peaks she can't ride | Both hands on the outer hips, pressing *in and slightly together*, thumbs toward her spine. Knees bent, she leans forward. Squeeze as the wave builds, hold through the peak, release as it fades |
| **Perineal / lower-back warm compress** | Any stage; especially second stage | Warm (not hot) cloth on the lower back or perineum; re-warm every few minutes |
| **Cold cloth on the forehead / back of the neck** | Hot, flushed, irritable, "don't touch me" | **Offered, not applied.** "Would you like the cool cloth?" Let her place it |
| **Breathing *with* her** | From early labour | He breathes the same slow rhythm, low in the belly, out through the mouth. Matching, not commanding — she mirrors his rhythm without being told |
| **Vocalisation coaching** | Active labour / transition | One phrase only, repeated: *"Low and slow, like fogging a mirror."* Demonstrates the low "ooooh" — jaw loose, no high strained sound |
| **Position change prompts** | Every 30–45 min in active labour | He suggests, then physically helps shift her: ball → lean-over-ball → hands-and-knees → side-lying. Upright and forward-leaning beats flat any day (Week 31) |
| **The rebozo / scarf** ([Week 30](../week-30/reading.md)) | Back pain, asymmetry, tension | A long scarf under her belly or around her hips, gently sifting or lifting. Round ligament comfort and gentle positioning |
| **Water** (shower or tub) | Active labour, before an epidural decision | He runs it, keeps it warm, and stays close — water is often the single best analgesic available without drugs |
| **Food, drink, bathroom** | Whole labour | Bites (dates, honey stick, fruit), sips (straw is easier), and a nudge to empty the bladder — a full bladder stalls descent |

**The one rule over all of them:** *one technique per contraction.* Layering four at once reads to her as chaos. Pick the one she's responding to and stay there.

## 6. Part 3: Reading Her Cues

This is the skill no checklist can teach, and it's the one Session 12 spends the most words on. The trick is that her needs change *between* phases, and the sign is her **mood**, not her words.

| If she is… | She likely wants | She likely does NOT want |
|---|---|---|
| **Chatty, joking** (early labour) | Company, distraction, food, a walk, normal life | Quiet, intensity, talk of "progress," an audience |
| **Focused, quiet** (active labour) | One hand on her back, dim lights, water between waves, one calm voice | Questions, instructions, cheerleading, conversation |
| **Irritable — "don't touch me"** | Space, silence, a cool cloth *offered* not applied | Touch, sympathy, being re-asked anything |
| **"I can't do this"** (transition) | Eye contact, three-word sentences, unwavering presence | Debating the epidural, explaining physiology, leaving the room |
| **Pushing, single-minded** | His steady voice and his hands holding her upright | Any instruction to "push harder" or count to ten |

**The three things he should do less of than he expects:** guiding, talking, and deciding. The three he should do more of: watching her face, keeping his own breath slow, and *asking* rather than acting.

The single most useful line in the whole manual: **"What do you need right now?"** — asked once, calmly, and then acted on without commentary.

## 7. Part 4: The Transition Mindset (Her Drill, His Support)

Transition (roughly 8–10 cm) is the shortest phase and the one whose reputation precedes it. [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) assigns a specific mental rehearsal this week, and it's worth doing properly because it desensitises the scariest-sounding part of labour.

**What transition actually is, physically:** the cervix finishing its opening, the head descending hard, plus a surge of hormones that commonly produces **nausea, shaking, a sense of overwhelm, and the exact thought "I can't do this."** That thought is not a warning sign — it is a *normal, predictable symptom of the phase that means you are almost done.* By the time you feel it, you're typically 30–60 minutes from pushing.

**Her drill (10 min/day this week, inside the birth-rehearsal visualization):** Visualise the peak of intensity deliberately, and rehearse three moves inside it:

1. **Drop the jaw.** A clamped jaw pairs with a clamped pelvic floor — the "sphincter law" from Week 25. Loosen the jaw and the floor follows.
2. **Low sound, long exhale.** The low "ooooh" has been trained since Week 33; in transition it becomes the metronome that keeps her out of panic-breathing.
3. **Say the mantra.** One of the 2–3 phrases chosen in Week 34, said inside the wave. "I open. I release. I welcome." / "Each wave brings me closer to my baby." / "I am powerful. I am safe."

**His script in transition** (Section 6, bottom row — this is the whole job): one calm voice, eye contact, three-word sentences, no debate. *"I'm right here." "You're doing it." "Look at me."* Nothing longer. Nothing that asks her to do anything new.

## 8. Part 5: Advocacy — The Exact Phrases

Being the advocate is not being confrontational; it's being **organised** and **slow**. The professionals expect these sentences and respect them.

| Situation | What he says |
|---|---|
| Offered an intervention she hasn't asked for | *"Let me check with her before we agree to anything."* |
| Feeling pressured to speed things up | *"She'd like another hour before we discuss that. Can we revisit it then?"* |
| Needs time to think | *"We need ten minutes to discuss this together."* (A legitimate, always-valid sentence.) |
| Wanting to understand the urgency | *"Is this an emergency, or do we have time to talk it through?"* |
| Protecting the labour room | *"We'd like dim lights and just the two of us in the room for now."* |
| Declining something not medically urgent | *"We're declining that for now, and we understand the risks. Please note it."* |
| Requesting the comfort measures | *"Can we have warm compresses and slow delivery of the head when it's time?"* |
| GBS-positive, labour suspected | *"She's GBS-positive — we'll come in as soon as labour is suspected so the antibiotics have time."* |

**The distinction he must hold:** between *"this is scary but normal"* and *"this is actually dangerous."* The red-flag list is in [`LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md`](../../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) and at the bottom of this file. Everything not on that list is a conversation; everything on it is an immediate call.

## 9. Part 6: Taking Care of Himself

A tired, hungry, anxious partner is a *worse* partner, and labour can run 12–20+ hours. This is a legitimate part of the assignment, not self-indulgence.

- **Eat and drink** — real food, kept ready. Don't wait to be offered.
- **Rest when she rests** — early labour at home is his chance to nap; sleep in shifts if family are helping.
- **Use the bathroom** whenever there's a lull — never mid-labour at the hospital.
- **Charge the phones**; keep the power bank handy. Photos are his job too, but only when everything else is calm.
- **Manage his own fear** with the same breathing she uses. If he fights panic, she feels it and mirrors it.
- **Have a script for freezing:** *"Hands on her back. Slow breath. I'm right here."* When he can't think, he defaults to the one physical action he can always do.

## 10. This Week's Appointment: Weekly Visits Begin

From this week the schedule compresses to **every week** — the home stretch of monitoring. The routine visit is short, and mostly reassuring if you come prepared.

**What happens:** weight and blood pressure, urine dip (protein and glucose), fundal height, the baby's heartbeat, and usually a position check. From around 36–37 weeks your provider may start **cervical checks** (dilation, effacement, station) — optional, and skippable if you'd rather not know.

**What to bring and ask (write the answers into the antenatal booklet):**

- **The head-down follow-up:** *"Is she well engaged, and is she OA or posterior?"* (Section 2–3.)
- **The GBS timing question**, if not already resolved in Week 34: *"When is the swab, and will the result still be valid at delivery?"*
- **The 24-hour number and room policy:** confirm the maternity line and how many support people are allowed.
- **The birth plan:** hand over the one-page version and ask them to file it. Confirm the "no routine episiotomy / warm compresses / slow delivery of the head" preferences.
- **The 41-week threshold:** *"If I go past 40 weeks, what's your induction policy, and what monitoring do you recommend?"* Worth knowing now, not at 41 weeks.
- **Any symptom you've been downplaying** — and in Singapore's heat specifically, ask about **swelling** and **blood pressure** if either has changed.

> ℹ️ **One labelling quirk to ignore:** your [`costs.md`](../../tracking/costs.md) third-trimester table labels the GBS swab as "week 35" and the first weekly visit as "week 36," while [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md) and [`calendar.md`](../../tracking/calendar.md) put weekly visits from this week. The **dates** agree; only the labels drift. Go by the dates your provider gives you, and let the costs table catch up.

## ⚠️ When to Call, Not Read

**Call TMC Maternity / your OB on-call immediately — day or night — for:** any regular contractions before 37 weeks · water breaking (note the time and colour) · bright red bleeding · reduced or changed fetal movement · severe headache with visual changes, or upper-abdominal pain · sudden swelling of face or hands · fever · a gut feeling that something is wrong. A false alarm is the correct outcome of that call, every time.

**And the week-35-specific one:** if you're helping with perineal massage or any internal work, stop at once for sharp pain, a lingering sting, or any bleeding — and call the provider rather than pushing on.
