# Week 35 Exercise & Yoga Guide — Peak Practice: The Ball Goes Full, the Partner Takes the Wheel

> **Where you are:** Week 35 (27 Sep – 03 Oct 2026) · 35w 0d → 35w 6d (D245–251) · ~35 → 29 days to EDD (1 Nov)
> **Program reference:** [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 35 — Phase 3 "Intensive Preparation" peak practice + [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md)
> **👶 Baby:** ~5.25 lb / 2.4 kg (honeydew) — kidneys and liver fully functional, fat layers smoothing the skin. Kick counts continue; know her normal pattern.
> **✅ Head-down confirmed at the Week 34 scan.** Your baby is cephalic — the single biggest positional variable in a first labour is behind you. The ball work, inversions and curb walking don't stop; they now have a sharper job: keep her **OA** (facing your spine) and help her settle **into** the pelvis (see [`reading.md`](reading.md) §2–3).
> **🎉 New this week:** the **Birth ball hits its full prescription — 15 minutes a day** (it ran early at 10 min in Week 34), the **perineal block steps up to 4–5×/week**, and the programme's headline item is the **full mock-labour rehearsal** — a dress rehearsal of the whole event with your partner on Day 6. There's no new movement to learn this week; the work is doing the existing ones a little more, resting between them, and running the rehearsal.
> The reading that pairs with this file is [`reading.md`](reading.md) — the partner's coaching manual and the head-down milestone.

**Safety first:** Clear this routine with your OB/midwife. Stop any movement and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, regular contractions before 37 weeks, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling (especially one-sided), decreased baby movement, or severe headache/visual changes. Never lie flat on your back for long periods. Breathe continuously — exhale on effort, never hold your breath. The Forward-Leaning Inversion is off the menu if you have high blood pressure, significant heartburn, or your provider has flagged baby's position for special handling. **Perineal massage is not done if you have placenta previa, an active vaginal infection or herpes outbreak, unexplained bleeding or a fluid leak, or once your waters have broken** — see [`reading.md`](reading.md) §3.

**Ball safety:** inflate firm — when she sits on it, hips slightly *higher* than knees, thighs roughly level. Roll it a hand-width from a wall for the first few sits, barefoot on a non-slip floor, and get on and off slowly from a braced kneel or the edge of the bed — never from standing while it rolls. No bouncing while breathless or queasy; gentle and rhythmic, never jarring. It's a sitting/leaning tool, not a balance toy.

**Rest is now a training decision, not a gap between sessions.** The Week 35 instruction is explicit: *focus on rest between activities.* Walking holds at 30–40 minutes rather than climbing; the strength block has given way entirely to mobility and endurance; and on a low day, the Daily Pelvic Routine plus a 20-minute walk genuinely *is* the workout. From here, consistency matters more than intensity, and showing up tired to a session is not discipline — it's a withdrawal from the energy account you'll need on the day.

**🌡️ Heat note (Singapore):** at 35 weeks, a 40-minute walk at midday is a different physical event than it was at 25. Walk early morning or after sundown, carry water, and shorten without guilt if you're flushed or swollen by the end.

## 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking — 30–40 Minutes

**How to do it:**
- Warm up 5 min easy
- Middle 20–30 min at a conversational pace — full sentences possible
- Cool down 5 min easy

The 30–40-minute band is deliberate and flat: this is not the week to push the distance. Keep the pace easy, walk in the cool hours, carry water, and shorten to 20–25 minutes (or split into two short walks) when pelvic pressure, back discomfort, or fatigue flares. **Curb walking** (5 min each side, one foot up) stays inside the circuit — the asymmetric pelvis rocks the sacrum and alternately opens the inlet, which is the mechanical point.

## ⭐ 2. The Birth Prep Circuit — 20 Minutes, Every Day (Same Seven Steps)

Same seven steps, same order. The squat holds at 90–120 seconds; step 5 runs on the ball for its full share.

| # | Step | Time | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cat-cow** | 10 slow rounds | Mobilizes the spine and pelvis; a gentle start |
| 2 | **Deep squat hold** | 90–120 sec | Opens the pelvic outlet; teaches the birth position |
| 3 | **Forward-Leaning Inversion** | 30 sec × 3 | Un-weights the uterine ligaments; helps the head settle down and forward |
| 4 | **Curb walking** | 5 min each side | Asymmetric pelvic opening; encourages rotation and descent |
| 5 | **Birth ball hip circles** | 2 min each direction | Sitting on the ball, knees lower than hips — circles, then figure-8s |
| 6 | **Pelvic floor release breathing** | 3 min | The landing pad — release on demand |
| 7 | **Child's pose** | 60 sec | Close the circuit; decompress the lower back |

**Refinements this week:**
- **FLI continues daily** (week 35's explicit line). Never on a full stomach, never if dizzy or heartburny, always rise back to kneeling between passes — never straight up. With her head already down, its job is now to help her stay nose-forward and settle deeper. Skip without guilt if it aggravates anything; the other six steps stand alone.
- **The squat is the mantra-and-vocalisation slot.** Exhale "oooooh," let the floor drop, say the chosen phrase once. Familiarity, not a strength feat.
- **Deep squat past 120 seconds?** Only if it's genuinely comfortable. If pubic or pelvic pain flares, switch to a wide-knee supported sit and keep everything else.
- **The three add-ons (below) run right after step 7** — they take under four minutes together and are on the "never skip, even on a bad day" list.

### 🎉 The Daily Ball Block — now the full 15 minutes

The ball is at its Week 35–40 prescription. Slot it wherever it fits — evening while watching something is the natural home — and it doubles as a second, seated round of the pelvic floor release breathing.

| # | Drill | Time | How | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated circles** | 3 min each direction | Feet wide and flat, hips slightly higher than knees, shoulders still, move only the pelvis. Slow and deliberate | The core open-pelvis move — now the real thing |
| 2 | **Figure-8s** | 3 min | Trace a lazy infinity sign (∞) with the hips, letting the belly hang forward on the crossover | Rotates the sacrum through both oblique planes; mobilizes the SI joints |
| 3 | **Gentle bouncing** | 3 min | Small, rhythmic, soft bounces, breathing low and slow — never while breathless or queasy | Encourages baby to settle into the pelvis; a low-stakes rehearsal of early-labour ball sitting |
| 4 | **Lean-over rest** | 3 min | Kneel with the ball in front, chest and forearms on it, knees wide, belly hanging. Exhale and melt | The abdominal-release / belly-hang posture — unloads the spine and pelvic floor; a primary labour position |
| 5 | **Pelvic floor release breathing on the ball** | 3 min | Sit tall, eyes soft, inhale into the belly and pelvic floor, long exhale with the low "oooooh" | Pairs the ball with the signature skill — release on demand, seated exactly as she may sit in early labour |

> **Tired / queasy / no-space day:** cut to items 4–5 (6 min), or swap the whole block for the chair version of step 5. Walking and circuit doses don't change to chase ball minutes.

### The daily add-ons (~4 min total)

| Add-on | Dose | How | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pelvic floor "quick flicks"** | Rapid contract-release × 10, once daily | Sitting or side-lying: squeeze *quickly*, release *fully and slowly*. The release is longer than the squeeze | Trains the awareness that pushing asks for — *letting go on demand*, not grip strength |
| **Lunges with pelvic tilt** | 10 each side | Standing, step into a comfortable lunge, hands on hips, gently tuck the pelvis (pubic bone toward the navel) as you sink. Hold 2 sec, return | Opens the pelvic inlet and mobilizes the sacroiliac joint. Hold a wall or chair if balance is off |
| **Hands-and-knees rocking** | 2 min daily | On all fours, rock the pelvis gently front-to-back — bigger and slower than cat-cow, no spinal rounding | Encourages anterior positioning, takes weight off the pelvic floor and lower back |

> If quick flicks cause leaking, heaviness, or a bulging sensation, stop the flicks and mention it at the appointment — that's a pelvic floor signal worth checking, not a reason to train harder.

## 🩷 3. The Perineal Prep Block — 5 Minutes, 4–5× This Week

Full protocol, evidence and contraindications are in the Week 34 guide: [`../week-34/reading.md`](../week-34/reading.md) §3–4. This is the physical execution, stepped up a frequency this week.

**Frequency:** 4–5 sessions of 5 minutes (Days 1, 3, 4, 5, and the partner-assisted one on Day 6). From week 36 it becomes daily.

1. **Prep:** bladder empty, hands washed, nails short, a warm shower or warm compress first. Oil: sweet almond, coconut, vitamin E, or a water-soluble lubricant. Not petroleum jelly; nothing warming or fragranced.
2. **Position:** semi-reclined with knees bent and apart on pillows, one foot on the bath edge, or side-lying. A mirror helps placement.
3. **Technique:** thumbs or fingers **1–1.5 inches (2–4 cm) inside**, pressing *down toward the rectum* and *out to the sides* — the direction the head will travel. Sweep a slow **U-shape** from 6 o'clock → 3 o'clock → 9 o'clock and back, **holding each end of the U for 60–90 seconds**. Two to four minutes of actual massage per session.
4. **Breathe under the stretch:** exhale, drop the jaw, consciously let the pelvic floor melt *against* the pressure. The coordinated "stretch + release" is the skill — not the depth of the stretch.
5. **Target sensation:** stretching, pressure, mild burning. **Stop for sharp pain, a lingering sting, or any bleeding** — less pressure, more oil, or stop for the day.

**Partner-assisted session (Day 6, once this week):** one or two lubricated fingers, 1–1.5 inches in, pressing downward toward the rectum — not inward. He watches *her* (jaw, breathing, face) as much as the tissue, slows down when she tenses, and she controls pressure and pace throughout with "more / less / stop." This is a rehearsal: on the day he may be applying warm compresses and supporting the perineum.

## 🏋️ 4. Strength — Maintenance Only (1–2 Sessions, Optional)

Week 35 drops the formal strength block; mobility and endurance are the goal, and the Week 34 loads were already reduced to the floor. If two light sessions feel good, keep them; if the week is full or you're tired, walking plus the circuit *is* the plan, and skipping the strength work costs you nothing. **No added load, no third session, and any carrying that feels like effort in the belly rather than the muscles is put down.**

### Optional Circuit A — Day 1
- **Bodyweight squats** — 12 × 2 rounds (sit back to the chair, touch, rise; exhale up)
- **Wall push-ups** — 12 × 2 rounds
- **Farmer's carry** — 2 × 30 sec with *light* bags (~2 kg per hand) — the first thing to cut

### Optional Circuit B — Day 3
- **Side lunges** — 8 each side × 2 rounds (wide stance, hips back over the bent knee)
- **Romanian deadlifts (household hinge)** — 12 × 2 rounds, one book, flat back, no lock-out strain
- **Clamshells** — 15 each side × 2 rounds, 2-sec pause at the top

Any leaking, heaviness or bulging in the pelvic floor during a lift means stop that lift and mention it at the next appointment.

## 🧘 5. Gentle Stretching & Prenatal Yoga (Daily stretch · 2 longer sessions)

Week 35 asks for **gentle daily stretching** — hip flexors, hamstrings, inner thighs, chest — as a comfort habit separate from the formal sessions. Ten minutes on the days without yoga; the long-hold sequence on Days 2 and 4.

**Daily 10-minute stretch (any day):**
1. Kneeling hip flexor lunge — 45 sec each side
2. Seated or standing hamstring stretch — 45 sec each side, back long, no rounding
3. Baddha konasana / butterfly — 60 sec, knees supported
4. Standing or seated chest opener (hands clasped behind, gentle lift) — 30 sec × 2

**Yoga sequence (~25 min, Days 2 & 4):**
1. **Cat-cow** — 8 rounds, slow
2. **Malasana / yogi squat** — supported against the wall or over a rolled blanket, 60–90 sec
3. **Upavistha konasana / wide-angle seated** — 60–90 sec, spine long
4. **Baddha konasana / butterfly** — 90 sec, knees supported, gentle forward fold
5. **Kneeling hip flexor lunge** — 45 sec each side, back knee on a cushion
6. **Happy baby** — 60 sec, holding behind the thighs, lower back flat
7. **Side-lying with pillows** — 3 min of pelvic floor release breathing to close

**Water session (1× if accessible):** swimming or water walking, 20–30 minutes. Twenty minutes in chest-deep water unloads the joints and the pelvis entirely. It counts fully as a session.

## 🎭 6. The Full Mock-Labour Rehearsal (Partner, Day 6, ~40 Minutes)

This is the week's headline. It is the only rehearsal that puts the partner's hands, her breathing, and the position changes all together, timed to real contractions, in the room you actually have. Run it once, calmly, in the evening — and treat the debrief as seriously as the drill.

### Before you start (5 min): the two-minute brief

- She states, in her own words, what she wants in each mood: **chatty** (early labour), **focused/quiet** (active), **irritable** (don't touch me), **overwhelmed** ("I can't do this"). He writes the answers down — see [`reading.md`](reading.md) §6.
- Confirm the three things he'll avoid: guiding, talking, deciding. And the one he'll do more of: *asking.*
- Put the pocket card ([`reading.md`](reading.md) §11) where he can see it.

### The rehearsal (5 rounds, ~30 min)

1. **Round 1 — early-labour mode (5 min):** she sits on the ball, slow circles. He times **one 60-second surge** and coaches with a single phrase: *"Slow breath, out through the mouth."* Then 90 seconds of real rest — he offers water and a position change. **He practises keeping it light and not timing every twinge.**
2. **Round 2 — active-labour mode (8 min):** she goes hands-and-knees for one surge, then **leaning forward over the birth ball** for the next. He applies **sacral counter-pressure** ([Week 29](../week-29/reading.md)) for one surge and the **double hip squeeze** ([Week 30](../week-30/reading.md)) for the next — **one technique per surge, never both.** He practises *not* talking through a surge unless asked.
3. **Round 3 — silence round (5 min):** he says nothing at all for one full timed surge while she breathes low and says her mantra. His only job is presence and a hand on her back. Most partners find this the hardest round — which is the point.
4. **Round 4 — transition drill (5 min):** she deliberately acts tense (clenched jaw, held breath, shoulders up) and says *"I can't do this."* He coaches the correction, three words at a time: *"Drop your jaw. Long breath out. I'm right here."* No debate, no physiology, no leaving the room.
5. **Round 5 — position-change practice (5 min):** she moves deliberately between ball sit → lean-over-ball → hands-and-knees → side-lying, mounting and dismounting slowly each time, while he times **two 60-second surges** and helps only as invited. This is the hospital rehearsal: a change every few contractions, steady hands nearby, never grabbing.

### The one-thing wind-down (5 min): advocacy role-play

He says the sentences from [`reading.md`](reading.md) §8 out loud once, slowly: *"Let me check with her." "We need ten minutes." "Is this an emergency, or can we talk it through?"* Out loud once is what makes it retrievable at 3 a.m.

**Debrief in one sentence each:** what worked, what to change. Done — and write the two numbers in pen (TMC's 24-hour maternity line and the OB's after-hours line) if they're not already in [`LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md`](../../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) PART 0.

## 🥗 8. This Week's Supporting Habits

The [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 35 prescription:

- **Dates: 6 Medjool/day (or 70 g)** — continue. If you have gestational diabetes, confirm with your provider
- **Raspberry leaf tea:** 3 cups/day (provider's blessing if you have a history of preterm labour or strong Braxton Hicks)
- **Evening primrose oil:** ~2,600 mg as directed — **provider approval only**, never with placenta previa or unexplained bleeding
- **Labour-day snacks, prepped:** honey sticks, coconut water, dates, fruit, energy balls — a small stash for the bag *and* one by the bed
- **Protein 80–100 g + vitamin C** — collagen synthesis, tissue elasticity, iron absorption. Blood volume peaks around now
- **Hydration 3 L/day** — more in the heat; the most effective single fix for cramping and false contractions
- **Electrolytes:** coconut water, banana, a pinch of sea salt in water
- **Small, frequent meals** — the stomach is compressed; five mini-meals beat three big ones
- **Sleep** is a training target, not a leftover. Aim for it like a session

## ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- **Treating rest as optional** — "focus on REST between activities" is the week's actual instruction. Tired is data, not weakness
- **Added load, PRs, or pushing walking distance** — mobility and endurance only from here
- **Pushing through sharp pain or bleeding during massage** — stretching and mild burning is the target, not stoicism
- **Perineal massage through a contraindication** — placenta previa, active infection or herpes outbreak, unexplained bleeding or fluid leak, or after waters break. Call first
- **Ignoring regular contractions** — at 35 weeks, regular timeable contractions, any bleeding, any fluid leak, or any change in movement is a call-now
- **FLI on a full stomach, with dizziness/heartburn, or with flagged position** — skip it; the rest runs fine without it
- **Ball bouncing while breathless or queasy, or mounting a rolling ball** — gentle and slow, from a braced kneel
- **Quick flicks if they cause leaking or heaviness** — stop the flicks, keep the release work, mention it at the appointment
- **Holding your breath** anywhere — exhale on effort, "aaaaah" on every practice surge
- **Flat-on-back work** — side-lying and tilted positions only
- **Skipping the rehearsal because it feels awkward** — it's the one practice that trains the actual day; run it once, badly, tonight rather than perfectly, never

## 🌟 The Bottom Line

Week 35 is the peak-practice week, and it looks deceptively quiet. No new movement arrives; the ball simply reaches its full 15 minutes, the perineal block reaches 4–5×, and the loads stay on the floor. What changes is *who* is training. The mock-labour rehearsal hands the techniques to the person who'll hold them for the longest hours — sacral pressure, the hip squeeze, the silence round, the three-word script for transition — and drills the one skill no checklist contains: reading her face well enough to know whether to speak or to shut up. Underneath it all sits the Week 34 news, and it's good news: **she's head down.** That's the biggest positional variable of a first labour resolved early, so the positioning habits shift from *turning* her to *keeping* her — nose forward, settling into the pelvis, ready for engagement. Twenty-nine days out, readiness now means two trained people, one packed bag, and a baby already pointing in the right direction.
