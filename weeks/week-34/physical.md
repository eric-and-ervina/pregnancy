# Week 34 Exercise & Yoga Guide — The Tissue Block: Perineal Prep Joins the Circuit

> **Where you are:** Week 34 (20–26 Sep 2026) · 34w 0d → 34w 6d (D238–244) · ~42 → 36 days to EDD (1 Nov)
> **Program reference:** [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 34 — Phase 3 "Intensive Preparation" continues + [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md)
> **👶 Baby:** ~4.75 lb / 2.2 kg (cantaloupe) — lungs nearly mature, vernix thickening, practicing sucking. Kick counts continue; know her normal pattern.
> **🎉 New this week:** the **gym/birth ball has arrived**. Every ball exercise the program has been writing chair-and-floor substitutes for since Week 26 is now the real thing — and the Week 35 prescription (ball work 15 min/day) starts today. Step 5 of the circuit gets a proper ball upgrade, a 10-minute **Daily Ball Block** is added, and every position in the Cue-Reading Rehearsal can be rehearsed on the ball exactly as it will be used in labour. Chair and floor versions are kept in the circuit notes as the fallback for tired or queasy days.
> Week 34 keeps the 20-minute **Birth Prep Circuit** from last week and bolts the **perineal prep block** onto it, plus three small daily items: **pelvic floor quick flicks**, **lunges with pelvic tilt**, and **hands-and-knees rocking**. Heavy lifting comes out. The reading that pairs with this file is [`reading.md`](reading.md) — perineal preparation, tissue health, and the GBS swab.

**Safety first:** Clear this routine with your OB/midwife. Stop any movement and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, regular contractions before 37 weeks, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling (especially one-sided), decreased baby movement, or severe headache/visual changes. Never lie flat on your back for long periods. Breathe continuously — exhale on effort, never hold your breath. The Forward-Leaning Inversion is off the menu if you have high blood pressure, significant heartburn, or your provider has flagged baby's position for special handling. **Perineal massage is not done if you have placenta previa, an active vaginal infection or herpes outbreak, unexplained bleeding or a fluid leak, or once your waters have broken** — see [`reading.md`](reading.md) Section 3.

**Ball safety (read before first use):** inflate firm — when she sits on it, hips slightly *higher* than knees, thighs roughly level; that's the height that keeps the pelvis open. A firm ball is a stable ball; a soft one sinks and tips. Roll it a hand-width from a wall for the first few sits, barefoot on a non-slip floor, and get on and off slowly from a braced kneel or the edge of the bed — never from standing while it rolls. No bouncing while breathless or queasy; gentle and rhythmic, never jarring. It's a sitting/leaning tool, not a balance toy — one careless mount at 34 weeks undoes a lot of good days.

On a rough day, the Daily Pelvic Routine plus 20 minutes of walking *is* the workout. Consistency beats intensity; every week from here, rest is a legitimate training decision.

## 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking — 40 Minutes

**How to do it:**
- Warm up 5 min easy
- Middle 25–30 min at a conversational pace — full sentences possible
- Optional gentle intervals if you feel good: 3 min steady + 1 min brisker
- Cool down 5 min easy

Keep the 40-minute standard on good days, shorten to 25–30 minutes at the same easy pace when pelvic or back discomfort flares, or split it into two 20-minute walks. Curb walking (5 min each side, one foot up) stays inside the circuit — the asymmetric pelvis rocks the sacrum and alternately opens the inlet, which is the mechanical point, not a stamina drill.

## ⭐ 2. The Birth Prep Circuit — 20 Minutes, Every Day (Same Seven Steps)

Same seven steps in the same order as Week 33, with the squat nudged up from 90 seconds toward 120. The sequence moves top-to-bottom through the body and finishes with the skill birth actually demands: release.

| # | Step | Time | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cat-cow** | 10 slow rounds | Mobilizes the spine and pelvis; a gentle start |
| 2 | **Deep squat hold** | 90–120 sec (build toward it) | Opens the pelvic outlet; teaches the birth position |
| 3 | **Forward-Leaning Inversion** | 30 sec × 3 | Un-weights the uterine ligaments; baby's repositioning window |
| 4 | **Curb walking** | 5 min each side | Asymmetric pelvic opening; encourages rotation and descent |
| 5 | **Birth ball hip circles** | 2 min each direction | Sitting on the **new gym ball**, knees lower than hips — circles, then figure-8s (chair version kept as the fallback below) |
| 6 | **Pelvic floor release breathing** | 3 min | The landing pad — release on demand |
| 7 | **Child's pose** | 60 sec | Close the circuit; decompress the lower back |

**Refinements this week:**
- **The ball has landed (step 5):** sit on the gym ball, feet wide and flat, hips slightly higher than knees, spine tall. Two minutes of slow circles each direction, then 1–2 minutes of figure-8s, then 1–2 minutes of gentle rhythmic bouncing — small, soft, "boiling-water" bounces, never jarring. That's ~5–6 minutes. The chair/floor version stays in the toolkit for tired, queasy, or no-space days — same joints, same dose.
- **Deep squat (step 2) is now aiming at 90–120 seconds.** Heels down (towel under them if needed), knees wide, belly between the thighs, jaw slack. Progress by 10–15 seconds every few days. If pubic or pelvic pain flares, switch to a wide-knee supported sit and keep everything else.
- **Heavier, deeper squat holds are the perfect place to practice the mantras and the low vocalisation** — exhale "oooooh," let the pelvic floor drop, and say your chosen phrase once. Familiarity, not a strength feat.
- **FLI:** never on a full stomach, never if dizzy or heartburny, always rise back to kneeling between passes — never straight up. Skip it without guilt if it aggravates anything; the other six steps stand alone.

### 🎉 The Daily Ball Block — 10 minutes (new; the Week 35 ball prescription opens early)

The ball is finally in the house, so the "Birth ball: 15 min daily" line in [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Weeks 35–40 starts *now* at a 10-minute dose. Slot it wherever it fits — evening while watching something is the natural home — and it can double as a second, seated round of the pelvic floor release breathing.

| # | Drill | Time | How | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated circles** | 2 min each direction | Feet wide and flat, hips slightly higher than knees, shoulders still, move only the pelvis. Slow and deliberate | The core open-pelvis move the program has asked for since Week 26 — now the real thing |
| 2 | **Figure-8s** | 2 min | Trace a lazy infinity sign (∞) with the hips, letting the belly hang forward on the crossover | Rotates the sacrum through both oblique planes; mobilizes the SI joints |
| 3 | **Gentle bouncing** | 2 min | Small, rhythmic, soft bounces, breathing low and slow — never while breathless or queasy | Encourages baby to settle into the pelvis; a low-stakes rehearsal of early-labour ball sitting |
| 4 | **Lean-over rest** | 2 min | Kneel with the ball in front, chest and forearms on it, knees wide, belly hanging. Exhale and melt | The abdominal-release / belly-hang posture — unloads the spine and pelvic floor; a primary labour position to rehearse |
| 5 | **Pelvic floor release breathing on the ball** | 2 min | Sit tall, eyes soft, inhale into the belly and pelvic floor, long exhale with the low "oooooh" | Pairs the ball with the program's signature skill — release on demand, seated exactly as she may sit in early labour |

> **Tired / queasy / no-space day:** cut the block to items 4–5 (4 min), or swap the whole block for the chair version of step 5. Walking and circuit doses don't change to chase ball minutes.

### The three Week 34 add-ons (~4 min total)

Do these right after step 7, or spread through the day — they're small and they don't need a slot of their own.

| Add-on | Dose | How | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pelvic floor "quick flicks"** | Rapid contract-release × 10, once daily | Sitting or side-lying: squeeze *quickly*, release *fully and slowly*. The release is longer than the squeeze | Trains the fast-twitch coordination and the awareness that pushing asks for. It's about *letting go on demand*, not grip strength |
| **Lunges with pelvic tilt** | 10 each side | Standing, step into a comfortable lunge, hands on hips, and gently tuck the pelvis (pubic bone toward the navel) as you sink. Hold 2 sec, return | Opens the pelvic inlet and mobilizes the sacroiliac joint — a labour position you can rehearse standing. Hold a wall or chair if balance is off |
| **Hands-and-knees rocking** | 2 min daily | On all fours, rock the pelvis gently front-to-back — bigger and slower than cat-cow, no spinal rounding | Encourages anterior positioning, takes weight off the pelvic floor and lower back, and is a position she may well labour in |

> If quick flicks cause leaking, heaviness, or a bulging sensation, stop the flicks and mention it at the appointment — that's a pelvic floor signal worth checking, not a reason to train harder.

## 🩷 3. The Perineal Prep Block — 5 Minutes, 3–4× This Week

Full protocol, evidence and contraindications are in [`reading.md`](reading.md) Sections 3–4. This is the physical execution:

**Frequency this week:** 3–4 sessions of 5 minutes (Days 1, 4, 5, and 6 for the partner-assisted one). From week 36 it becomes daily.

1. **Prep:** bladder empty, hands washed, nails short, a warm shower or warm compress first. Oil: sweet almond, coconut, vitamin E, or a water-soluble lubricant. Not petroleum jelly; nothing warming or fragranced.
2. **Position:** semi-reclined with knees bent and apart on pillows, one foot on the bath edge, or side-lying. A mirror helps placement for the first few sessions.
3. **Technique:** thumbs or fingers **1–1.5 inches (2–4 cm) inside**, pressing *down toward the rectum* and *out to the sides* — the direction the head will travel. Sweep a slow **U-shape** from 6 o'clock → 3 o'clock → 9 o'clock and back, **holding each end of the U for 60–90 seconds**. Two to four minutes of actual massage per session.
4. **Breathe under the stretch:** exhale, drop the jaw, and consciously let the pelvic floor melt *against* the pressure. This coordinated "stretch + release" is the skill — not the depth of the stretch.
5. **Target sensation:** stretching, pressure, mild burning. **Stop for sharp pain, a lingering sting, or any bleeding** — less pressure, more oil, or stop for the day.

**Partner-assisted session (Day 6, once this week):** one or two lubricated fingers, 1–1.5 inches in, pressing downward toward the rectum — not inward. He watches *her* (jaw, breathing, face) as much as the tissue, slows down when she tenses, and she controls pressure and pace throughout with "more / less / stop." This is a rehearsal, not a novelty: on the day, he may be applying warm compresses and supporting the perineum.

## 🏋️ 4. Strength Maintenance (2 Sessions — Reduced Load)

The Phase 3 instruction holds, and this week it tightens: **maintain but reduce heavy lifting; focus on mobility and endurance.** Circuit C from Week 33 is dropped — two sessions is the plan, and a third session of reduced work isn't better than two solid ones.

### Circuit A — Day 1
- **A1. Bodyweight squats** — 12 × 2 rounds (sit back to the chair, touch, rise; exhale up)
- **A2. Wall push-ups** — 12 × 2 rounds
- **A3. Farmer's carry** — 2 × 30 sec with *light* bags (~2 kg per hand), tall spine. This is the one to cut first if the belly feels the load

### Circuit B — Day 3
- **B1. Side lunges** — 8 each side × 2 rounds (wide stance, hips back over the bent knee)
- **B2. Romanian deadlifts (household hinge)** — 12 × 2 rounds, one book, flat back, no lock-out strain
- **B3. Clamshells** — 15 each side × 2 rounds, 2-sec pause at the top

**Load rules:** if a weight feels like effort in the *belly* rather than the muscles, it's too heavy — put it down. Exhale on every effort. Any leaking, heaviness or bulging in the pelvic floor during a lift means stop that lift and mention it at the next appointment. Reduce load or rounds rather than skipping sessions.

## 🧘 5. Prenatal Yoga — Long Holds, Hip Focus (2× This Week)

Twenty to thirty minutes on Days 2 and 4, as last week: sustained opening, holds of 5+ breaths, hips and inner thighs, nothing that needs unsupported balance. On Day 4 this doubles as the week's gentle-stretch session — end at step 7 and stop there.

**Sequence (~25 min):**
1. **Cat-cow** — 8 rounds, slow
2. **Malasana / yogi squat** — supported against the wall or over a rolled blanket, 60–90 sec
3. **Upavistha konasana / wide-angle seated** — 60–90 sec, spine long
4. **Baddha konasana / butterfly** — 90 sec, knees supported, fold forward gently
5. **Kneeling hip flexor lunge** — 45 sec each side, back knee on a cushion
6. **Happy baby** — 60 sec, holding behind the thighs, lower back flat
7. **Side-lying with pillows** — 3 min of pelvic floor release breathing to close

**Water session (1× if accessible):** swimming or water walking, 20–30 minutes. Twenty minutes in chest-deep water unloads the joints and the pelvis entirely. It counts fully as a session.

## 🎭 6. The Cue-Reading Rehearsal (Partner, Day 6, ~30 Minutes)

Week 33's walk-through taught the *timeline*. Week 34's rehearsal teaches the thing no timeline can: **reading her cues.** The program's instruction for the partner this week is specific — learn when to talk vs. be silent, and when to offer water vs. counter-pressure.

### Before the rehearsal: the two-minute brief

Ask her to state, in her own words, what she wants in each of these moods. Write the answers down; they'll be truer than anything he guesses:

| If she is… | Likely wants | Likely does NOT want |
|---|---|---|
| **Chatty / joking** (early labour) | Company, distraction, food, a walk | Quiet, intensity, talk of "progress" |
| **Focused and quiet** (active labour) | One hand, dim lights, water between waves, no conversation | Questions, instructions, cheerleading, an audience |
| **Irritable / "don't touch me"** | Space, silence, a cool cloth *offered* not applied | Touch, sympathy, being re-asked anything |
| **"I can't do this"** (transition) | One calm voice, eye contact, three-word sentences, no arguments | Debating the epidural, explaining physiology, leaving the room |

### The rehearsal (5 rounds)

1. **Round 1 — early-labour mode (5 min):** she chooses a low-intensity position — the natural one now is **sitting on the birth ball**, slow circles. He times one 60-second surge and coaches with a single phrase: *"Slow breath, out through the mouth."* Then 90 seconds of real rest — he offers water and a position change.
2. **Round 2 — active-labour mode (8 min):** she goes hands-and-knees for one surge, then **leaning forward over the birth ball** for the next. He applies [Week 29](../week-29/reading.md) sacral counter-pressure for one surge, and the [Week 30](../week-30/reading.md) hip squeeze for the next — **one technique per surge**, never both. He practises *not* talking through a surge unless she asks. He also learns the day-one skill of ball support: hands hovering at her shoulders while she's on the ball, ready to steady, never grabbing.
3. **Round 3 — silence practice (5 min):** he says nothing at all for one full timed surge while she breathes low and says her mantra. His only job is presence and a hand on her back. Most partners find this the hardest round — which is exactly the point.
4. **Round 4 — transition drill (5 min):** she deliberately acts tense on the ball (clenched jaw, held breath, shoulders up) and he coaches the correction: *"Drop your jaw. Long breath out. I'm right here."* Then he practises catching himself before offering anything she hasn't asked for.
5. **Round 5 — position-change practice (5 min, new):** she moves deliberately between ball sit → lean-over-ball → hands-and-knees → back to the ball, mounting and dismounting slowly each time, while he times two 60-second surges and helps only as invited. This is the hospital rehearsal: position changes every few contractions, with the ball as the hub — and steady hands nearby, never grabbing.

**Debrief in one sentence each:** what worked, what to change. Done.

**Also this week (paperwork, 10 min):** confirm the two numbers written in pen, not remembered — **TMC's 24-hour maternity line and the OB's after-hours delivery-on-call line** — and add the **GBS line** to the one-page birth plan once the swab is back ([`LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md`](../../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) §0.1 and §1.4).

## 🥗 8. This Week's Supporting Habits

The [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 34 prescription, which is also the tissue-health checklist from [`reading.md`](reading.md) Section 5:

- **Dates: 6 Medjool/day (or 70 g)** — the clinically meaningful dose opens now. If you have gestational diabetes, confirm with your provider first
- **Raspberry leaf tea:** 3 cups/day (provider's blessing if you have a history of preterm labour or strong Braxton Hicks)
- **Evening primrose oil:** ~2,600 mg as directed — **provider approval only**, never with placenta previa or unexplained bleeding
- **Protein 80–100 g + vitamin C** — collagen synthesis, tissue elasticity, and iron absorption. Blood volume peaks around now
- **Hydration 3 L/day** — more in the heat; the most effective single fix for cramping and false contractions
- **Electrolytes:** coconut water, banana, a pinch of sea salt in water
- **Small, frequent meals** — the stomach is compressed; five mini-meals beat three big ones
- **Sleep** is a training target, not a leftover. Aim for it like a session

## ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- **Perineal massage through a contraindication** — placenta previa, active infection or herpes outbreak, unexplained bleeding or fluid leak, or after waters break. Call the provider first; massage later
- **Pushing through sharp pain or bleeding during massage** — stretching and mild burning is the target, not stoicism
- **Ignoring regular contractions** — at 34 weeks, regular timeable contractions, any bleeding, any fluid leak, or any change in baby's movement is a call-now, not a workout adjustment
- **Added load, PRs, or a third strength session** — heavy lifting comes out this week on purpose
- **Quick flicks if they cause leaking or heaviness** — stop the flicks, keep the release work, mention it at the appointment
- **FLI on a full stomach, with dizziness/heartburn, or with flagged position** — skip it; the rest runs fine without it
- **Holding your breath** anywhere — exhale on effort, "aaaaah" on every practice surge
- **Flat-on-back work** — side-lying and tilted positions only
- **Skipping the tissue block because it's awkward** — five minutes, three times this week. If it's genuinely unbearable, say so at the appointment and let the provider offer the alternative; don't just quietly drop it

## 🌟 The Bottom Line

Week 34 adds the last physical unknown to the routine — and, this time, the last piece of equipment too. The circuit keeps opening the pelvis, now from the saddle of the ball the program has been substituting for since Week 26; the Daily Ball Block brings the Week 35 ball prescription forward a week; the strength work steps back to maintenance; and five minutes of tissue work, three or four times, starts preparing the exact millimetre of anatomy that has to stretch at the end. Alongside it, the add-ons — quick flicks, lunges with pelvic tilt, hands-and-knees rocking — train release and awareness rather than grip. And the rehearsal this week isn't about the timeline any more; it's about the partner learning to read her without being told, in the same positions she'll actually labour in. Meanwhile the GBS swab turns a hypothetical plan into a specific one: one line in pen, one earlier departure, nothing else changed. Six weeks out, this is what readiness looks like — unglamorous, specific, and done.
