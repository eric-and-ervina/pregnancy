# Week 32 Exercise & Yoga Guide — The Fantastic Four & the Mock Contraction

> **Where you are:** Week 32 (06–12 Sep 2026) · 32w 0d → 32w 6d (D224–230) · ~56 → 50 days to EDD (1 Nov)
> **Program reference:** [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 32 + [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md)
> **👶 Baby:** ~3.75 lb / 1.7 kg (jicama) — most babies settle head-down now; kick counts continue (know her normal pattern).
> This guide adapts the Week 32 prescription for home use — no gym and no equipment required: body weight, a wall, a sturdy chair, a couch, and a pillow pile. A birth ball is still optional. It doubles as the physical rehearsal for [`reading.md`](reading.md), this week's golden-hour study, and is the last week of the Phase 2 strength block before the program pivots to the Week 33+ Birth Prep Circuit.

**Safety first:** Clear this routine with your OB/midwife. Stop any movement and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, regular contractions before 37 weeks, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling (especially one-sided), decreased baby movement, or severe headache/visual changes. Never lie flat on your back for long periods. Breathe continuously — exhale on effort, never hold your breath. The Forward-Leaning Inversion is off the menu if you have high blood pressure, significant heartburn, or your provider has flagged baby's position for special handling. If a given day is rough, the Daily Pelvic Routine + 20 minutes of walking is the whole workout; consistency beats intensity at 32 weeks.

## 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking — 40 Minutes with Gentle Intervals

**How to do it:**
- Warm up 5 min easy
- **Gentle intervals:** 3 min conversational pace + 1 min noticeably brisker (not huffing — you should still be able to speak in full sentences) — repeat through the middle 25–30 minutes
- Cool down 5–10 min easy

The intervals are borrowed from the Week 30 pattern ([`week-30/physical.md`](../week-30/physical.md)) — they build the surge/rest rhythm that labor actually runs on: effort arrives, effort recedes, you recover on the recovery. If intervals aggravate pelvic or back discomfort, do the whole walk at conversational pace and call it done; the 40 minutes matter more than the speed profile.

## 🏋️ 2. Strength Maintenance Circuits (3 Sessions/Week)

Same five pillars as Week 31 (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry), same home format — this week the instruction is simply *hold your ground, don't add load*. Two full rounds of each circuit instead of grinding singles.

### Circuit A — Day 1
- **A1. Bodyweight Squats** — 12–15 reps × 2 rounds (sit back to the chair, touch, rise; exhale up)
- **A2. Wall Push-Ups** — 12–15 × 2 rounds
- **A3. Farmer's Carry** — 2 × 30 sec, shopping bags or ~2–4 kg per hand, tall spine

### Circuit B — Day 3
- **B1. Side Lunges** — 8 each side × 2 rounds (the rotation work from [`reading` week 31](../week-31/reading.md), still in the body)
- **B2. Romanian Deadlifts (household hinge)** — 12 × 2 rounds, one heavy book, flat back
- **B3. Clamshells** — 15 each side × 2 rounds, 2-sec pause at top

### Circuit C — Day 5
- **C1–C3. One round each of A1 → B1 → A2**, then repeat the trio: two total rounds, minimal rest between exercises, 60–90 sec between rounds.

Reduce load or reps rather than skipping sessions if you're tired — the point of Week 32 is to arrive at Week 33 still strong, not PR'd.

## ⭐ 3. The Fantastic Four (3–4× This Week, ~12 Minutes)

This is the Week 32 signature: the full Spinning Babies "Fantastic Four" sequence, run as one continuous routine. Its purpose is twofold — comfort now (releasing the tension patterns that make 32 weeks achy) and position optimization (giving baby room to settle head-down, facing your spine). Do it in order; each step prepares the next.

### 1. Forward-Leaning Inversion (FLI) — 30 sec × 3

Kneel at the couch or bed edge, forearms down on it, walk the knees back until hips stack over shoulders and the head hangs. 30 seconds, breathing slowly; rise carefully to kneeling (not straight up). Three passes, resting between. **Never** on a full stomach, never if dizzy, keep a partner or wall in reach the first times. This briefly un-weights the ligaments holding the uterus so baby has room to re-settle — the single highest-leverage 90 seconds in the routine.

### 2. Side-Lying Release — each side, ~1–2 min

Lie on your side at the edge of the bed or couch with the bottom leg straight; extend the top leg off the edge so it hangs supported (partner can hold the top ankle/foot, or rest it on a low stack of pillows). Let the top hip lengthen and "hang" until you feel the side of the hip/IT band soften — breathe into it rather than forcing. Switch sides. This releases the lateral tension that tilts the uterus, one of the main reasons babies sit crooked.

### 3. Abdominal Lift & Release (gentle) — ~1 min

On hands and knees (or standing with hands on a counter), inhale and let the belly soften and drop toward the floor — a gentle, passive "hang" of the abdomen with each breath, no bracing, no breath-holding. The idea is to create gentle space between the top of the uterus and the ribcage so baby has an easier angle to tuck chin and descend. Keep it small and comfortable; this is a breath-led move, not a stretch you push.

### 4. Pelvic Floor Release — 5 min

Lie on your side (pillows between knees) or sit supported. Diaphragmatic breathing with the exhale directed *into* the pelvic floor: inhale, belly and ribs expand; exhale, consciously let the pelvic floor melt and widen — the "flower opening" from Week 30. This is the routine's landing pad: after the three balance moves, the floor learns the only lesson that matters for birth — release on demand.

**When:** Days 2, 4, 6 minimum (built into the schedule above); add a 4th pass any day you skipped strength. Not immediately after a large meal, and skip the FLI if it aggravates heartburn — the other three still stand alone.

## 🔄 4. The Daily Pelvic Routine (Every Day, ~15 Minutes)

| Step | What | Time |
|------|------|------|
| 1 | **Cat-cow** | 10 slow rounds |
| 2 | **Pelvic rocks on all fours** | 20 reps — bigger, slower tilts than cat-cow; mobilizes the sacrum (PROGRAM.md Week 32 addition) |
| 3 | **Deep squat hold** — towel under heels if needed, hold a doorframe or chair | Build toward 2 min (start where Week 31 left you: ~60–90 sec) |
| 4 | **Pelvic floor release breathing** | 5 min |

**Progressing the squat to 2 minutes:** add 10–15 seconds every few days rather than jumping. Two full minutes in a deep squat is not a strength feat — it's a familiarity project: heels down, belly resting between the thighs, jaw slack, breath dropping into the belly. If pubic/pelvic pain flares, hold the supported wide-knee sit instead and keep everything else.

**Birth-ball bonus (if you have one):** 15–20 min daily — sitting, hip circles, figure-8s, gentle bouncing, and 2 min of leaning forward over the ball (belly-hang posture, the same idea as the abdominal release). This is the PROGRAM.md Week 32 ball prescription; no ball = the squat + pelvic routine covers the same joints.

## 🧘 5. Gentle Stretch Block (Days 2 & 4, ~10 Minutes)

Tacked onto the Fantastic Four days or the walk cool-down:

1. **Child's pose** — 60 sec, knees wide, belly settling between the thighs
2. **Kneeling hip flexor lunge** — 45 sec each side, back knee down on a cushion, gentle tuck
3. **Wide-angle seated forward fold** — 60 sec, spine long, fold only to comfortable
4. **Chest-opener at the wall** — 45 sec, forearms on the wall above shoulder height, softening the sternum down (posture reset for a belly-heavy week)

## 🎭 6. The Mock-Contraction Rehearsal (Partner, 1–2× This Week, ~15 Minutes)

The Week 32 mind-body assignment from [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md): practice breathing through *discomfort* — deliberately, with coaching — so the surge-breathing pathway exists before labor builds it.

### Part 1 — Solo simulation (during any squat hold or wall sit)

Sink into the 2-minute squat (or a wall sit with thighs burning). The burn or shake is the point: treat it as a 60-second "surge."

- Breathe the Week 30 contraction pattern: slow in through the nose, long out through the mouth with a low "aaaaah"
- Jaw slack, shoulders down, sound low — a tight jaw is a tight pelvic floor
- When it intensifies (~the last 20 seconds), the exhale gets *longer*, not faster

Two or three simulated surges per session is plenty. You are teaching the nervous system that strong sensation is survivable with breath, not that pain is fun.

### Part 2 — Partner rehearsal

The husband runs a timed mock contraction:

1. **He times 60 seconds** — announces "surge starting," counts nothing aloud during it except the last 10 seconds softly
2. **You breathe** through it in a position of choice (squat, lean-over-ball, hands-and-knees)
3. **He coaches with two tools only:** the Week 29 counter-pressure (heel of hand on sacrum) and one short cue he'll actually use on the day — e.g., *"slow breath, out through the mouth"* or *"shoulders down, I'm here."*
4. **60 seconds of rest** — real rest: he brings water, you change position
5. **Repeat × 3–5 "surges."** On the last one, he adds the low "oooooh" vocalization cue

Debrief in one sentence each: what helped, what distracted. This is a rehearsal of the *communication*, not the pain — and it's the full partner-coaching loop from Weeks 29–31 (counter-pressure, hip squeeze, breathing cues) assembled into one sequence for the first time. Log how it felt; the pattern that emerges is what you'll actually use at 3 a.m. in September-October.

**Partner's paperwork job:** after the rehearsal, finalize the one-page birth-preferences document together (the after-birth block is drafted in [`reading.md`](reading.md) Section 7) — print ×3 and file it with the antenatal booklet per [`LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md`](../../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md).

## ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- **Adding load or chasing PRs** — this is the maintenance cap before Week 33 pivots to the Birth Prep Circuit
- **FLI on a full stomach, with dizziness, or with high blood pressure/flagged position** — skip it; the rest of the Fantastic Four runs without it
- **Holding your breath** anywhere — exhale on every effort, "aaaaah" on every simulated surge
- **Flat-on-back work** — side-lying and tilted positions only
- **Forcing the abdominal release or the side-lying hang** — these are soft-tissue *invitations*, not stretches to push through
- **Skipping the Fantastic Four on busy days** — 12 minutes; it's the highest-leverage block of the week
