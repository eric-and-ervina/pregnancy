# Week 33 Exercise & Yoga Guide — The Birth Prep Circuit Begins

> **Where you are:** Week 33 (13–19 Sep 2026) · 33w 0d → 33w 6d (D231–237) · ~49 → 43 days to EDD (1 Nov)
> **Program reference:** [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 33 — **Phase 3 "Intensive Preparation" begins** + [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md)
> **👶 Baby:** ~4.2 lb / 1.9 kg (pineapple) — growing fast, immune system developing, bones hardening. Kick counts continue; know her normal pattern.
> This guide opens Phase 3, so the shape of the week changes: the Phase 2 strength block is now **maintenance**, and the daily work becomes the **20-minute Birth Prep Circuit** — cat-cow, deep squat, forward-leaning inversion, curb walking, birth ball, pelvic floor release, child's pose. Still no gym and no equipment: body weight, a wall, a sturdy chair, a couch, a curb, and a pillow pile. A birth ball stays optional. It doubles as the physical rehearsal for the labor timeline in [`reading.md`](reading.md).

**Safety first:** Clear this routine with your OB/midwife. Stop any movement and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, regular contractions before 37 weeks, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling (especially one-sided), decreased baby movement, or severe headache/visual changes. Never lie flat on your back for long periods. Breathe continuously — exhale on effort, never hold your breath. The Forward-Leaning Inversion is off the menu if you have high blood pressure, significant heartburn, or your provider has flagged baby's position for special handling. If a day is rough, the Daily Pelvic Routine plus 20 minutes of walking is the entire workout; at 33 weeks, consistency beats intensity, and *every* week from here on, rest is a legitimate training decision.

## 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking — 40 Minutes, Non-Negotiable

**How to do it:**
- Warm up 5 min easy
- Middle 25–30 min at a conversational pace — you can speak full sentences
- Optional gentle intervals if you feel good: 3 min steady + 1 min brisker, repeated
- Cool down 5 min easy

The program calls this non-negotiable for a reason: 40 minutes daily keeps the cardiovascular engine warm for a multi-hour event and is one of the most reliable ways to keep baby descending and well-positioned. If pelvic or back discomfort flares, shorten to 25–30 minutes at the same easy pace, take it indoors, or split it into two 20-minute walks. What you don't do is skip it entirely for a whole week.

**Curb walking (built into the circuit):** 5 minutes each side, one foot on the curb and one on the road. The asymmetric pelvis rocks the sacrum and opens the inlet alternately, which is the mechanical point — it's not a stamina drill. Hold a rail or your partner's arm if balance feels off (relaxin is high and your centre of gravity has moved).

## ⭐ 2. The Birth Prep Circuit — 20 Minutes, Every Day

This is the Week 33 signature and the spine of Phase 3. Seven steps, twenty minutes, in order — the sequence moves top-to-bottom through the body and finishes with the one skill birth actually demands: release.

| # | Step | Time | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cat-cow** | 10 slow rounds | Mobilizes the spine and pelvis; a gentle start |
| 2 | **Deep squat hold** | 90 sec (build toward it) | Opens the pelvic outlet; teaches the birth position |
| 3 | **Forward-Leaning Inversion** | 30 sec × 3 | Un-weights the uterine ligaments; baby's best repositioning window |
| 4 | **Curb walking** | 5 min each side | Asymmetric pelvic opening; encourages rotation and descent |
| 5 | **Birth ball hip circles** | 2 min each direction | Soaking the pelvis into a wide, open position (chair substitute below) |
| 6 | **Pelvic floor release breathing** | 3 min | The landing pad — release on demand |
| 7 | **Child's pose** | 60 sec | Close the circuit; decompress the lower back |

### How to run it well

1. **Cat-cow (10 rounds)** — hands and knees, wrists under shoulders. Inhale, drop the belly and lift the tailbone; exhale, round the spine and tuck the chin. Slow. Big breath-led arcs, not small pumps.
2. **Deep squat hold (90 sec)** — feet slightly wider than hip-width, hold a doorframe, chair, or your partner. Heels down (towel under them if needed), knees wide, belly resting between the thighs, jaw slack. Work toward 90 seconds by adding 10–15 seconds every few days. If pubic or pelvic pain flares, switch to a wide-knee supported sit and keep everything else.
3. **Forward-Leaning Inversion (30 sec × 3)** — kneel at the couch or bed edge, forearms down, walk the knees back until hips stack over shoulders and the head hangs. Breathe slowly for 30 seconds, then rise carefully to kneeling *between* passes — never straight up. Never on a full stomach, never if dizzy, and keep a wall or partner within reach. Skip it entirely (without guilt) if it aggravates heartburn or your provider has flagged position — the other six steps stand alone.
4. **Curb walking (5 min each side)** — or the indoor equivalent: one foot on a sturdy low step, one on the floor, walking slowly. Prefer a corridor; hold a rail.
5. **Birth ball hip circles (2 min each direction)** — sit tall on the ball, knees lower than hips, make slow deliberate circles, then figure-8s and gentle bouncing. **No ball?** Sit on a sturdy chair or the floor with knees lower than hips and do the same circles with the pelvis, or swap for 2 min of kneeling hip circles on all fours. The ball is convenient, not essential.
6. **Pelvic floor release breathing (3 min)** — side-lying with pillows between the knees, or sitting supported. Inhale, belly and ribs expand; exhale, consciously let the pelvic floor melt and widen — the "flower opening" from Week 30. This is the skill, not the stretch: birth is a release event, and a pelvic floor that can only tighten is the thing to avoid.
7. **Child's pose (60 sec)** — knees wide, belly settling between the thighs, forehead down, arms long. Breathe into the back ribs. End here every time.

**When:** every day. Attach it to something already fixed in the day — after the walk, before the shower, right after HypnoBirthing practice. Twenty minutes bolted onto an existing habit happens; twenty minutes that needs its own slot does not.

## 🏋️ 3. Strength Maintenance (2–3 Sessions This Week)

The Phase 3 instruction from [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md): **maintain but reduce load slightly as the belly grows.** Same five pillars as Weeks 31–32 — squat, hinge, push, pull, carry — two rounds, nothing added. The goal is arriving at week 37 still mobile and strong, not making progress.

### Circuit A — Day 1
- **A1. Bodyweight squats** — 12 × 2 rounds (sit back to the chair, touch, rise; exhale up)
- **A2. Wall push-ups** — 12 × 2 rounds
- **A3. Farmer's carry** — 2 × 30 sec, shopping bags or ~2–3 kg per hand, tall spine

### Circuit B — Day 3
- **B1. Side lunges** — 8 each side × 2 rounds (wide stance, hips back over the bent knee)
- **B2. Romanian deadlifts (household hinge)** — 12 × 2 rounds, one heavy book, flat back, no lock-out strain
- **B3. Clamshells** — 15 each side × 2 rounds, 2-sec pause at the top

### Circuit C — Day 5 (optional)
- **One round each of A1 → B1 → A2 → B3**, then repeat the quartet: two rounds, minimal rest between exercises, 90 sec between rounds.

**Load rules:** if a weight feels like effort in the *belly* rather than the muscles, it's too heavy. Exhale on every effort. Any leaking, heaviness or bulging in the pelvic floor during a lift means stop that lift — and mention it at your next appointment. Reduce load or rounds rather than skipping sessions; three sessions of less is the plan, not three sessions of more.

## 🧘 4. Prenatal Yoga — Long Holds, Hip Focus (2× This Week)

Twenty to thirty minutes, two days this week (Days 2 and 4). Phase 3 shifts yoga from movement to **sustained opening**: holds of 5+ breaths, hips and inner thighs, nothing that requires balancing on a changing centre of gravity without support.

**Sequence (~25 min):**
1. **Cat-cow** — 8 rounds, slow
2. **Malasana / yogi squat** — supported against the wall or over a rolled blanket, 60–90 sec
3. **Upavistha konasana / wide-angle seated** — 60–90 sec, spine long, fold only to comfortable
4. **Baddha konasana / butterfly** — 90 sec, soles together, knees supported by cushions, fold forward gently
5. **Kneeling hip flexor lunge** — 45 sec each side, back knee on a cushion, gentle tuck
6. **Happy baby** — 60 sec, holding behind the thighs (not the feet), lower back flat
7. **Side-lying** with pillows — 3 min of pelvic floor release breathing to close

**Water session (1× if accessible):** swimming or water walking for 20–30 minutes. Twenty minutes in chest-deep water unloads the joints and the pelvis entirely, which at 33 weeks feels less like exercise and more like a holiday. It counts fully as a session.

## 🎭 5. The Timeline Walk-Through (Partner, Day 6, ~30 Minutes)

This week's mental assignment from [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) — full birth-rehearsal visualization, 10 min daily, plus vocalization practice — gets its physical anchor here. The reading hands you the labor timeline; this is where you walk it out loud, in the body, once a week.

### Part 1 — Solo vocalization practice (daily, 5 min)

During any squat hold, wall sit, or long hip opener: low, deep sounds on every exhale — "oooooh," then "aaaaah." Not a performance; the point is that a **low** sound keeps the jaw and throat open, and the jaw and the pelvic floor are wired together. A high-pitched, held-breath grunt is the opposite of what you want on the day. Practice the low one until it's the default.

### Part 2 — The Walk-Through (with partner, ~30 min)

He narrates the timeline from [`reading.md`](reading.md) Section 2, and you rehearse each phase in the position you'd actually be in for it:

1. **Early labor** — he times a **60-second surge**. You breathe through it standing, swaying, or leaning on him. Coaching cue: *"Slow breath, out through the mouth."* Then 90 seconds of real rest — water, position change.
2. **Active labor** — he changes your position: hands-and-knees for one surge, then a birth-ball or chair lean for the next. He applies [Week 29](../week-29/reading.md) counter-pressure on the sacrum and the [Week 30](../week-30/reading.md) hip squeeze — one technique per surge, not both at once.
3. **Transition** — he switches to **three-word sentences**, close in, eye contact: *"You're doing it."* / *"Almost there, love."* / *"I'm right here."* One more timed surge, longer exhale, low vocalization.
4. **Pushing** — you practice the **open-glottis exhale-push** in a supported squat: breathe *down* with the effort, sound out, no breath-holding. Two practice pushes, nothing strenuous.
5. **Debrief in one sentence each** — what helped, what got in the way. That's the whole rehearsal.

**Partner's paperwork job this week:** the birth-preferences document was finalized last week ([`week-32/reading.md`](../week-32/reading.md) Section 7). This week's task is the **timeline card**: print Section 10 of [`reading.md`](reading.md) and file it in the antenatal booklet folder alongside the preferences page, per [`LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md`](../../program/LABOUR-DAY-GUIDE.md) §0.4. Then confirm the two numbers from §0.1 that get written in pen, not remembered: TMC's 24-hour maternity line and the OB's after-hours delivery-on-call line.

## 🔄 6. The Daily Pelvic Routine (Folds Into the Circuit)

The Week 32 pelvic routine is now steps 1, 2 and 6 of the Birth Prep Circuit — cat-cow, deep squat, pelvic floor release breathing. On any day the full circuit doesn't happen, this trio is the minimum:

| Step | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cat-cow | 10 rounds |
| 2 | Pelvic rocks on all fours | 20 reps, bigger and slower than cat-cow |
| 3 | Deep squat hold | build toward 90 sec |
| 4 | Pelvic floor release breathing | 3–5 min |

**Progressing the squat:** you were heading for 2 minutes at Week 32; at 90 seconds daily through Phase 3, 2 minutes arrives naturally. Heels down, belly between the thighs, jaw slack. Familiarity, not a strength feat.

## 🥗 7. This Week's Supporting Habits (From Phase 3)

Not exercise, but they're why the exercise works — the [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 33 prescription:

- **Raspberry leaf tea:** 3 cups/day (confirm with your provider if you have a history of preterm labour or very strong Braxton Hicks)
- **Dates:** 4–6 Medjool daily, building the habit — the full 6/day window opens at week 36
- **Evening primrose oil:** ~2,600 mg as directed — **provider approval only**, and not with placenta previa or unexplained bleeding
- **Hydration:** 3 L/day — more in tropical heat; the single most effective fix for cramping and false contractions
- **Protein and vitamin C:** 80–100 g protein daily plus citrus, capsicum, guava — supports tissue elasticity for the perineal work starting next week
- **Sleep:** this is now a training target. Aim for it like you'd aim for a session.

## ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- **Ignoring regular contractions** — at 33 weeks, regular timeable contractions, any bleeding, any fluid leak, or any change in baby's movement is a call-now, not a workout adjustment
- **Added load or PRs** — strength is maintenance only from here
- **FLI on a full stomach, with dizziness/heartburn, or with flagged position** — skip it; the rest of the circuit runs fine without it
- **Holding your breath** anywhere — exhale on effort, "aaaaah" on every practice surge
- **Flat-on-back work** — side-lying and tilted positions only
- **Forcing the squat depth or the pelvic floor release** — 90 seconds is a familiarity project, not a stretch to win
- **Skipping the circuit because the day was busy** — 20 minutes; it's the highest-leverage block of the week, and Phase 3 lives or dies on the daily habit

## 🌟 The Bottom Line

Week 33 starts Phase 3 by taking everything you've built and putting it on a single 20-minute daily track. Walking stays. Strength steps back. The circuit becomes the routine you'll keep all the way to 36 weeks: open the pelvis, unweight the ligaments, breathe the pelvic floor into release, and finish in child's pose. Alongside it, one rehearsal a week with your partner turns the labor timeline from a document into muscle memory — the phases, the positions, the three-word sentences, the low sounds. Five weeks from now you'll want both: a body that opens on cue, and a nervous system that already knows the shape of the day.
