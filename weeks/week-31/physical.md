# Week 31 Exercise & Yoga Guide — The Labor-Position Rehearsal

> **Where you are:** Week 31 (30 Aug – 5 Sep 2026) · 31w 0d → 31w 6d (D217–223) · ~63 → 57 days to EDD (1 Nov)
> **Program reference:** [`PROGRAM.md`](../../program/PROGRAM.md) Week 31 + [`THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md`](../../program/THIRD-TRIMESTER-GUIDE.md)
> **👶 Baby:** ~3.3 lb / 1.5 kg (coconut) — all five senses working, rapid weight gain begins; kick counts continue (know her normal pattern).
> This guide adapts the Week 31 prescription for home use — no gym, no ball, no bands: body weight, a wall, a sturdy chair, and a couch. It doubles as the physical rehearsal for [`reading.md`](reading.md), this week's labor-positions study.

**Safety first:** Clear this routine with your OB/midwife. Stop any movement and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, regular contractions before 37 weeks, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling (especially one-sided), decreased baby movement, or severe headache/visual changes. Never lie flat on your back for long periods. Breathe continuously — exhale on effort, never hold your breath. Squats are progressive: if 60 seconds is too much, hold 30 and build. If you have severe back pain on a given day, use the gentler version in [`week-29/physical-back-pain.md`](../week-29/physical-back-pain.md).

## 🚶‍♀️ 1. Daily Walking — 40 Minutes

**How to do it:**
- Warm up 5 min at an easy pace
- 25–30 min at a comfortable brisk-but-conversational pace; add a gentle 2-minute stride increase halfway if it feels good (no huffing)
- Cool down 5–10 min easy, shoulders relaxed, arms swinging
- Wear supportive shoes; bring water; if it's hot or humid, walk indoors or early morning/evening

**Why walking matters more this week:** Every step is engagement work (reading Section 2, movement #1). The stride gently rocks the pelvis, and the sway is the same motion as the labor dance — so think of this walk as 40 minutes of rotation practice, not cardio.

**Curb walking** (added this week): on a low curb or a stair's bottom step, walk slowly along it so one foot is on the curb and one on the ground — 10 minutes total, alternating direction each minute. This asymmetrical step is a *lunge by walking*: it opens one pelvic side at a time, exactly the rotation help baby needs at internal rotation. If curbs feel wobbly, hold your partner's arm or a wall.

## 🏋️ 2. Full-Body Strength Circuits (3 Sessions/Week)

No gym, no bands: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry — the five movement pillars, each done for time.

### Circuit A — Day 1 (Legs, Carry & Push)

- **A1. Bodyweight Squats** — 12–15 reps. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out; sit back to a sturdy chair, touch, rise. Exhale on the way up. This is your deep-squat practice partner.
- **A2. Chair Rows** — 12 reps. Facing the back of a sturdy chair, lean back and pull yourself up to it with straight arms (like a reversed push-up). Keeps posture muscles strong for leaning-forward positions.
- **A3. Wall Push-Ups** — 12–15 reps. Hands on the wall at shoulder height, chest to wall, exhale on push. Upright-ish strength for the pushing-phase chest position.
- **A4. Farmer's Carry** — 2 rounds, 30 sec each. Two equally-weighted shopping bags (or one heavy book each hand, ~2–4 kg total per hand), walk the length of the room and back, upright tall spine. The carry builds the endurance your upper body needs for long side-lying and leaning positions.

### Circuit B — Day 3 (Hips, Rotation & Pull)

- **B1. Side Lunges** — 8 each side. Step wide to the right, sit into that hip, chest up; push back to center; repeat left. This is the *lunge* from the active-labor menu — one pelvic side opens at a time.
- **B2. Romanian Deadlifts (household hinge)** — 12 reps. Holding one heavy book (or two), hinge at the hips, back flat, weight to mid-shin, squeeze glutes up. Trains the upright hinge that protects your back in deep squat work.
- **B3. Clamshells** — 15 each side. Side-lying, knees bent 45°, open the top knee without rolling the hips. Add a 2-second pause at the top. Hip stabilizers for side-lying labor positions.
- **B4. Rotation with Reach** — 10 each side. Sitting or standing, arms out, rotate your torso toward the side you just lunged. Trains the rotation that powers internal rotation (reading Section 2).

### Circuit C — Day 5 (Full-Body Finish)

Repeat A1, B1, and A2 as a 3-exercise circuit, 2 rounds: 12 squats → 8 side lunges each side → 12 chair rows, minimal rest between, 60–90 sec rest between rounds. This is your "active labor" conditioning — work, brief recovery, work — which is exactly the surge/rest rhythm of labor.

## 🧘 3. Prenatal Yoga (2 Sessions/Week)

### Yoga A — Hip Opening & Rotation (Day 2)

1. **Malasana (Yogi Squat)** — 60 sec (progressive). Heels down if possible, feet wide, elbows inside knees, chest open. If heels lift, roll a towel under them. Breathe slow and deep — this is the wide-open pelvic position from reading Section 1.
2. **Upavistha Konasana (Wide-Angle Seated)** — 60 sec. Sit with legs wide, spine long, fold forward only as far as is comfortable; hands on the floor. Opens the inner thighs and pelvic floor.
3. **Happy Baby** — 60 sec. On your back (tilt a cushion under one hip), grab the outer edges of your feet, knees to armpits, gently rock. Pelvic-opening final pose. If this is too much belly, skip to a supported deep squat.
4. **Cat-Cow × 10** — on all fours, inhale drop belly look up, exhale round the back. Mobilizes the sacrum — the same movement pattern as hands-and-knees labor positions.
5. **Hip circles** (standing, hands on hips) — 1 min each direction. Stirring-the-pot circles; the labor-dance movement.

### Yoga B — Length, Balance & Release (Day 4)

1. **Forward-Fold with Supported Hands** — 60 sec. Hinge forward over a chair back or couch edge, back flat, letting the belly hang. The *forward-lean* posture that helps baby rotate to the front (OA).
2. **Half-Squat with Rotation** — 60 sec. Squat to a comfortable depth, rest a hand on a knee, rotate the other arm toward the ceiling, switch. Combines squat + rotation work.
3. **Side-Lying Stretch** — 2 min each side. Lie on your side, top leg forward, reach the top arm overhead in a gentle banana stretch. Opens the torso for side-lying labor.
4. **Pelvic Rocks** — 20 reps. On all fours, tilt the pelvis forward/back slowly. Mobilizes the sacrum and low back — Week 32 makes this a core daily move, so start now.
5. **Breathing in a Supported Squat** — 3 surge-length breaths (60 sec each). In the deep squat, breathe the Week 30 pattern C: in through the nose, out long and slow through the mouth with "aaaaah." This is your pushing rehearsal.

## 🔄 4. The Daily Pelvic Routine (Every Day, ~15 Minutes)

This is the Week 31 non-negotiable — a focused 15 minutes every single day:

| Step | What | Time |
|------|------|------|
| 1 | **Cat-cow** | 10 slow rounds |
| 2 | **Deep squat hold** (towel under heels if needed) | 60 sec |
| 3 | **Forward-Leaning Inversion** (spotter/partner nearby; see below) | 30 sec × 3 |
| 4 | **Side-lying + pillows** — knees bent, pillow between knees, Sleep Breathing | 5 min |
| 5 | **Pelvic floor release** — diaphragmatic breathing into the pelvic floor (below) | 5 min |

**Step 3 — Forward-Leaning Inversion (FLI):** Kneel at the couch or bed, forearms on the edge, walk the knees back until your hips are over your shoulders and your head hangs low — *never* with a full tummy or while dizzy. 30 seconds, three times, breathing slowly. This is the Spinning Babies staple that gives the baby room to rotate and settles an OP (sunny-side-up) baby. Keep a wall or partner behind you for the first few times.

**Step 5 — Pelvic floor release breathing:** The *opposite* of a Kegel. Lie or squat comfortably, inhale gently, then as you exhale, consciously **let go** of the pelvic floor — imagine it melting open, like a flower opening (the Week 30 visualization). This is not "pushing"; it is *releasing*. It is the single most important pelvic skill for labor: an open pelvic floor lets the head descend without resistance, and it is what "low vocalization" protects.

**If your pelvic/pubic pain is high this week:** swap the deep squat for a supported wide-knee sit (feet on the floor, knees apart) and keep the rest of the routine — the FLI and side-lying don't load the pubic joint.

## 👨 5. The Labor Dance (with Partner, 1–2× This Week, 3–5 Minutes)

Put on one slow song. Stand face to face, arms around each other, foreheads touching if comfortable:

1. **Slow sway** — 1 min. Weight shifts side to side through the feet, hips following naturally.
2. **Hip circles** — 1 min. One big slow circle per several breaths; reverse.
3. **Figure-8s** — 1 min. Draw lazy "8"s on the floor with your hips.
4. **Vocalization practice** — during the sway, let out a low, open "oooooh" on the exhale, then "aaaaah." Jaw slack, sound from the belly, not the throat.

That's it. This is the birth-day pain-management tool disguised as a slow dance, and it is also the partner's first real coaching rehearsal: his hands, his rhythm, your sway — all already familiar.

**Partner's extra skill this week:** practice the Week 29 counter-pressure (heel of hand on the sacrum during "surges") and the hip-squeeze — now on the *standing* sway, so he learns to support her in the positions she'll actually use. Review [`week-29/back-soreness-guide.md`](../week-29/back-soreness-guide.md) once and read aloud the position flowchart in [`reading.md`](reading.md) once — that read-aloud is most of his prep done.

## ⚠️ What to Avoid This Week

- **Flat-on-back work** — always tilt or side-lying; even "Happy Baby" gets a hip cushion
- **Holding your breath** — exhale on every effort; continuous breathing only
- **Straining to the point of dizziness** — FLI and deep squats are progressive; back off and tell your provider if dizzy
- **Heavy lifting** — carries are light (2–4 kg per hand); heavier loads are a no
- **Bouncing or jerky movement** — everything slow and controlled
- **Wear-and-tear on knees/hands** — pad knees with a cushion; wrists can rest on forearms in all-fours work

## 🌟 The Bottom Line

By the end of this week, the labor positions stop being a reading assignment and become **a body you already know**. The squat is familiar, hands-and-knees is familiar, the sway and the "aaaaah" are familiar. On birth day, when the surges come and the world shrinks to the room, you won't be learning anything new — you'll be remembering what you rehearsed here. That is the whole point of Week 31.
