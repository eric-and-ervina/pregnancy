# Prenatal Yoga Instructions

> **Important:** Always consult your healthcare provider before beginning or continuing a yoga practice during pregnancy. Stop immediately if you feel pain, dizziness, or discomfort. Avoid lying flat on your back after the first trimester, and never force a stretch.

---

## 🦋 1. Butterfly Pose (Baddha Konasana)

**Benefits:** Opens the hips and inner thighs, improves circulation to the pelvic region, and can help prepare the body for labor.

### Instructions:

1. **Starting position:** Sit on the floor (or on a folded blanket/cushion for hip support). Extend your legs in front of you.
2. **Bring soles together:** Bend your knees and draw the soles of your feet together, allowing your knees to fall open to the sides.
3. **Adjust your feet:** Hold your feet or ankles. Place your heels as close to your pelvis as is comfortable — there's no need to force them close.
4. **Lengthen your spine:** Sit tall through the crown of your head. Gently draw your shoulders back and down.
5. **Forward fold (optional):** On an exhale, hinge forward from the hips (not the waist), walking your hands forward along the floor. Only go as far as feels comfortable. Keep your back long.
6. **Gentle movement:** You can gently flutter your knees up and down like butterfly wings for 20–30 seconds to release tension.
7. **Hold:** Remain in the pose for 5–10 slow, deep breaths.
8. **Release:** Inhale back to upright. Use your hands to gently bring your knees together before extending your legs.

### Prenatal Modifications:
- Sit on a yoga block or bolster to elevate the hips and reduce strain on the lower back.
- Place cushions under each knee for support if the inner thigh stretch feels intense.
- In the 2nd/3rd trimester, keep a wider angle between the knees to make room for the belly.

## 🐦 2. Pigeon Pose (Eka Pada Rajakapotasana)

**Benefits:** Deeply stretches the hip flexors, glutes, and piriformis; relieves lower back and sciatic tension common in pregnancy.

### Instructions:

1. **Starting position:** Begin on all fours (tabletop position) with wrists under shoulders and knees under hips.
2. **Bring right leg forward:** Slide your right knee forward toward your right wrist. Angle your right shin inward — the foot can rest near the left hip or closer to the left wrist, depending on flexibility.
3. **Extend left leg back:** Slide your left leg straight back, lowering the top of the left thigh and shin to the floor. Keep the left hip pointing forward (not rotating open).
4. **Square your hips:** Gently press both hip points evenly toward the floor. Place a folded blanket or block under the right hip/glute if it doesn't reach the ground.
5. **Upright or fold:**
   - *Upright:* Keep your torso tall, hands on the floor or on blocks beside you.
   - *Forward fold:* Walk your hands forward and lower your torso over your front leg, resting on forearms or a bolster.
6. **Breathe:** Hold for 5–8 deep breaths. With each exhale, feel the hips soften and release.
7. **Switch sides:** Tuck the back toes, lift the hips, and carefully draw the right leg back. Repeat on the left side.

### Prenatal Modifications:
- **Use a bolster or stacked blankets** under the front hip and thigh for support — never let the hip "hang" in the air.
- **Widen the front shin angle** to accommodate the growing belly.
- In the 3rd trimester, consider doing this pose against a wall or using a chair for the front leg (Figure-4 stretch) instead of going to the floor.
- Avoid deep forward folds if they compress the belly.

## 🏋️ 3. Supported Squat (Malasana with Support)

**Benefits:** Strengthens the legs and pelvic floor, opens the hips and pelvis, encourages optimal baby positioning, and builds stamina for labor.

### Instructions:

1. **Starting position:** Stand with feet slightly wider than hip-width apart. Turn toes out at a comfortable angle (roughly 30–45 degrees).
2. **Lower down:** On an exhale, bend your knees and lower your hips toward the floor, as if sitting into a small chair. Keep your chest lifted and spine long.
3. **Depth:** Lower only as far as is comfortable. You do NOT need to go all the way down. A half-squat is perfectly effective.
4. **Support options (choose one):**
   - Place a **yoga block** (on its highest setting) under your sit bones to "sit" on.
   - Roll a **blanket or bolster** and place it under the hips for support.
   - Stand with your **back against a wall** and slide down into a supported squat.
   - Have a **partner or chair** in front of you to hold for balance.
5. **Arm position:** Bring palms together at heart center (prayer position), using elbows to gently press knees outward. Or rest hands on thighs/blocks.
6. **Engage gently:** Draw the tailbone slightly down and in. Engage the pelvic floor lightly (as if doing a gentle Kegel).
7. **Breathe:** Hold for 5–10 breaths. Inhale to lengthen the spine; exhale to sink a little deeper (only if comfortable).
8. **Release:** Press through your heels to rise back to standing, or carefully come to all fours.

### Prenatal Modifications:
- **Never go deeper than comfortable** — the hormone relaxin makes joints more mobile, increasing injury risk.
- Keep a **wider stance** in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters to make room for the belly.
- If balance is an issue, perform the squat **facing a wall** with hands on it, or hold a sturdy chair.
- Avoid holding the squat for extended periods if you feel pelvic pressure or have been advised to limit pelvic floor strain.
- Rise slowly to avoid dizziness.

---

## 🧘 General Safety Reminders for Prenatal Yoga

| Do ✅ | Don't ❌ |
|---|---|
| Breathe steadily and deeply | Hold your breath |
| Use props liberally (blocks, bolsters, blankets) | Push through pain |
| Modify as your body changes each trimester | Do deep twists or backbends |
| Stay hydrated before, during, and after | Overstretch (relaxin increases flexibility) |
| Listen to your body above all else | Lie flat on your back (after 1st trimester) |
| Practice in a cool, well-ventilated space | Practice in hot/humid conditions (no hot yoga) |

---

*These poses can be practiced individually or sequenced together: Butterfly → Pigeon (both sides) → Supported Squat. Finish with a few minutes of seated breathing or a supported side-lying rest.*
