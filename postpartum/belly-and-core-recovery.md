# Ervina's Postpartum Belly & Core Recovery Program

> **For:** Ervina — first baby, planned unmedicated vaginal birth at Thomson Medical Centre.
> **Goal:** A belly that looks and functions the way it did before pregnancy — flat, firm, and strong — by 6–12 months after birth.
> **This is education, not medical advice.** Dr Paul has the final say at every stage. If anything here conflicts with what he or a women's-health physiotherapist tells her, their instruction wins.

---

## 1. The honest expectation

For Ervina's profile, returning close to her pre-pregnancy belly is a **realistic, high-probability outcome** — not a guarantee of being literally indistinguishable up close.

In her favour:

- Age 30, first pregnancy, singleton, no complications so far
- 160 cm, 48.6 kg pre-pregnancy (BMI 19.0)
- 58.75 kg at 37w6d (+10.15 kg) — tracking in the lower third of the recommended gain range for her BMI, projected ~59.6 kg / ~11 kg total at term. There is not much extra fat or skin stretch to reverse
- Planned vaginal birth

What this program can and cannot do:

| It can | It cannot |
|---|---|
| Reconnect and strengthen the deep core and pelvic floor | Guarantee stretch marks will fade completely |
| Narrow a diastasis and — more importantly — restore tension across it | Change skin genetics or elasticity |
| Rebuild abdominal, glute and whole-body strength | Undo a caesarean scar (though it can still restore flatness and function around one) |
| Guide a gradual return to pre-pregnancy weight | Make confinement month the right time to chase a flat belly |

**Judge the result at 12 months, not at 6 weeks or 3 months.** Weight usually resolves first, core function second, skin last.

---

## 2. What has to recover — four separate things

Treating the postpartum belly as "fat to lose" is the main mistake. Four things are going on at once:

1. **The uterus.** ~1 kg at birth, back to near pre-pregnancy size by ~6 weeks. This alone is why she will still look pregnant in week 1–2.
2. **Fluid, blood volume, baby, placenta, amniotic fluid.** Roughly 5–6 kg leaves at birth and over the first 2 weeks.
3. **The abdominal wall and diastasis recti.** The linea alba down the midline stretches and the two sides separate. What matters most is not just the width of the gap, but whether she can generate **tension** across it. A soft, protruding belly after the weight is gone is usually this, not fat.
4. **Skin.** Stretched for 9 months. It remodels over 6–12 months and is the slowest and least trainable component.

The program below is sequenced in that order: heal → reconnect → rebuild → strengthen.

---

## 3. The non-negotiable rules

1. **Gates, not dates.** Every phase has a gate to pass before progressing. A date on the calendar never overrides pain, bleeding, doming, or pelvic-floor symptoms.
2. **No doming/coning.** If an exercise makes a ridge or bulge rise along the midline of her belly, that exercise is too hard *today*. Regress it — don't push through it. This single rule is the core of the whole program.
3. **No pain, no pressure, no leaking.** Sharp pain, pelvic heaviness/dragging, vaginal bulging, or leaking urine during an exercise = stop and regress. If it persists, that's a physio referral, not more effort.
4. **Nothing in the vagina and no swimming/baths until cleared** — the Chapter 7 rule, and it applies while training too.
5. **Bleeding is a load gauge.** Lochia that was lightening and turns heavy/bright red again after activity means she did too much. Rest 24–48 h and resume one level lower (see [Chapter 7 — Her Recovery](../course/07-her-recovery.md) §1 for the emergency thresholds).
6. **Sleep and food come before sessions.** A missed session because the baby didn't sleep costs nothing. Training exhausted and under-fuelled costs recovery.
7. **Severe tear exception.** If she has a 3rd/4th-degree perineal tear, an episiotomy with complications, or a caesarean, Phases 0–1 are **physio-guided only** — follow the branch in §10 and Dr Paul's instructions, not the standard track.

## 4. The program at a glance

| Phase | When | Focus | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Heal & connect** | Days 1–7 | Walking, breathing, gentle pelvic-floor connection | Daily, 5–10 min |
| **1 — Reconnect** | Week 2 → 6-week check | Deep core reconnection, walking build-up, glutes | Daily connection work + 3×/week, 10–20 min |
| **— GATE —** | **6-week check with Dr Paul** | **Clearance + diastasis check. Do not start Phase 2 without it.** | — |
| **2 — Rebuild** | Weeks 7–12 | Progressive core rehab + bodyweight/light strength | 3–4×/week, 20–30 min |
| **3 — Strengthen** | Months 4–6 | Loaded strength training, harder core work, return to normal exercise | 3×/week, 30–45 min |
| **4 — Consolidate** | Months 6–12 | Normal training, final fat/skin phase, maintenance | Her normal routine |

Nutrition rules run alongside all phases — see §9.

## 5. Phase 0 — Heal & connect (Days 1–7)

**Goal:** blood flow, uterine involution, and re-establishing the brain-to-pelvic-floor connection. Nothing here should feel like a workout.

**Daily:**
- **Walking:** short and frequent beats long. Day 1–2: around the ward/house. By end of week 1: 5–10 min walks, 2–3×/day, easy pace, stop before fatigue.
- **360° breathing (connection breath)** — 2–3 sets of 5 breaths, several times a day:
  1. Lie on her back or side, knees bent, one hand on her ribs, one on her lower belly.
  2. Inhale through the nose: ribs expand in all directions, belly rises gently, pelvic floor relaxes/lengthens.
  3. Exhale slowly through pursed lips: pelvic floor lifts gently and the lower belly draws in — as if doing up a tight pair of trousers. No bearing down, no holding her breath.
  4. Fully relax before the next breath. The relaxation is half the exercise.
- **Gentle pelvic-floor contractions** (only if no severe tear and no catheter): on the exhale above, add a gentle lift — imagine stopping the flow of urine and lifting — at ~30–50% effort. 5–8 reps. It should never hurt. Soreness = wait another day or two.
- **Ankle pumps and position changes** when in bed for long stretches — clot prevention, same reason the midwives want her walking.
- **Getting out of bed:** roll to her side, push up with her arms. No straight sit-ups from lying flat — that spikes pressure straight down the midline.

**Not yet:** crunches, planks, lifting anything heavier than the baby, "sucking in" all day (constant gripping fatigues the pelvic floor and backfires).

## 6. Phase 1 — Reconnect (Week 2 → 6-week check)

**Gate to start:** lochia lightening, perineal pain improving day to day, walking comfortably.

**Walking:** add ~5 min/day as tolerated, building toward **30 min continuous easy walking by week 6**.

**Connection work — daily, 5 min:**
- 360° breathing with pelvic-floor lift: 3 × 8 breaths
- Pelvic-floor endurance: lift and hold 5 sec, rest 5 sec × 8; plus quick lifts: 1 sec on / 1 sec off × 8. Build holds toward 8–10 sec by week 6. Full release between every rep — a pelvic floor that can't relax is as problematic as a weak one. If she has pelvic pain, tailbone pain, or pain with sitting, skip holds and tell Dr Paul — some pelvic floors need relaxation work, not strengthening.

**Strength circuit — 3×/week, 10–20 min.** Exhale and connect (pelvic-floor lift + lower belly in) on the effort of every rep:

| Exercise | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Heel slides | 2 × 8/side | Lying on back, slide one heel out slowly while the lower belly stays drawn in and the pelvis doesn't rock |
| Glute bridge | 2 × 10 | Ribs down, squeeze glutes, no back arch at the top |
| Side-lying leg lift / clam | 2 × 10/side | Slow; pelvis stacked and still |
| Wall sit or sit-to-stand from a chair | 2 × 8 | Only as perineal comfort allows |
| Bird-dog (hands and knees) | 2 × 6/side | From week 3–4 if comfortable; slide the leg out first, lift only if no doming |

**Progress rule:** when a full session produces no doming, no symptoms during it, and no symptom flare or bleeding increase in the 24 h after, she can progress the next session. Any flare = repeat the previous level.

### The diastasis self-check (do at ~week 6, before the check-up)

1. Lie on her back, knees bent, head resting.
2. Place two fingers horizontally across the midline just above the navel.
3. Lift just her head (not a full sit-up). Feel whether the fingers sink into a soft gap or are pushed up by tension.
4. Repeat at the navel and just below it.
5. Note two things: **width** (how many fingers fit) and **depth/tension** (soft and deep, or firm and shallow).

Bring the result to Dr Paul. As a rough guide: ~2 fingers with good tension that is improving is a normal Phase 2 starting point. A gap wider than ~3 fingers, very soft/deep with no tension, or an visible bulge, = ask for a women's-health physio referral instead of self-managing.

## 7. GATE — The 6-week check

Do not start Phase 2 on schedule alone. At the check ([Chapter 7](../course/07-her-recovery.md) §7), ask Dr Paul specifically:

- [ ] Cleared for progressive exercise and loading?
- [ ] Diastasis — width, and can she generate tension across it?
- [ ] Pelvic floor — any prolapse signs, leaking, or heaviness to address?
- [ ] Perineum / scar fully healed?
- [ ] If any of: doming that won't resolve, leaking, pelvic heaviness, back/pelvic pain, or a wide soft gap — **referral to a women's-health physiotherapist.**

**Strong recommendation even if everything looks fine:** one assessment with a women's-health physio around week 6–8. She self-checks width; a physio checks function. It is the highest-value single appointment in this program.

## 8. Phase 2 — Rebuild (Weeks 7–12)

**Gate to start:** Dr Paul's clearance + can do Phase 1 work with no doming and no symptoms.

**Structure:** 3–4 sessions/week, 20–30 min. Two alternating sessions, plus walking most days (30–45 min).

**Session A — core + glutes**
| Exercise | Dose | Progression within the phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dead bug | 3 × 6/side | Heel taps → straight-leg lowers → with band/weight in hands |
| Side plank | 3 × 20–40 sec/side | From knees → from feet |
| Glute bridge → single-leg bridge | 3 × 10 | Two legs → marching bridge → single-leg |
| Pallof press (band/cable) | 3 × 8/side | Start light — this is anti-rotation, the belly should stay quiet |

**Session B — squat, hinge, carry**
| Exercise | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Squat to chair → bodyweight squat → goblet squat (light) | 3 × 8–10 | Load only when the bodyweight version is symptom-free |
| Romanian deadlift pattern with light dumbbells | 3 × 8–10 | Hip hinge, flat back, exhale on the effort |
| Bird-dog (loaded progression) | 3 × 6/side | Full arm+leg reach, no pelvis sway |
| Suitcase carry | 3 × 20–30 m/side | Light dumbbell, walk tall, no leaning away |

**Rules for this phase:**
- Exhale on exertion for every loaded rep. Never hold breath and bear down (Valsalva) yet.
- Front-loaded positions (full front plank, push-ups from the floor, heavy carries in front) come back **only** when she can hold tension without doming — test with a short plank at the start of a session; a midline ridge means not yet, keep using incline push-ups (hands on a bench/bed) meanwhile.
- Traditional crunches and sit-ups stay out for the whole phase. They train the surface muscle by pushing it outward — the opposite of what a healing midline needs.

**Gate to Phase 3:** dead bug with straight-leg lowers and a 30-sec full side plank with no doming, no leaking, no heaviness — on two separate sessions, not just once.

## 9. Nutrition alongside the program

Core rehab flattens the belly's *function*; returning to ~48.6 kg handles the rest. The two run together, in this order:

- **Weeks 0–6: no deficit.** She is healing tissue and (if breastfeeding) establishing supply. Eat to appetite from the confinement plan — protein at every meal.
- **Protein target throughout:** ~1.6 g/kg of bodyweight/day (≈ 80–95 g/day for her) — this is what rebuilds abdominal and pelvic tissue and protects muscle while fat comes off.
- **From week 6–8, only if weight loss is still needed and feeding is established:** a *small* deficit — roughly 250 kcal/day, targeting **0.25–0.5 kg/week maximum**. Faster loss risks milk supply, muscle, and skin keeping up.
- **If breastfeeding:** expect the last 1–2 kg to be stubborn until feeding reduces. That is hormonal fat storage doing its job, not the program failing.
- **Fluids and fibre** — the confinement plan covers this; constipation and straining are direct pelvic-floor load, so they are a program issue, not just comfort.
- **No waist trainers, no aggressive "detox" teas, no skipping meals.** A postpartum binder/support garment is fine for comfort in Phases 0–1 (see §11) — it is not a fat-loss tool.

## 10. If the birth changes — the caesarean branch

If she delivers by caesarean instead, the goal and the high-probability outlook still stand. The timeline shifts by roughly 4–6 weeks and these rules replace the standard track until Dr Paul clears otherwise:

- Walking still starts day 1 (clot prevention) — short, frequent, supported.
- **Lifting limit: the baby, nothing heavier,** for the first weeks.
- Phases 0–1 become breathing + walking + gentle connection only, timed around the wound — no bridge/bird-dog loading until the wound is healed and cleared.
- Scar care per Dr Paul; once fully closed and cleared, gentle scar mobilisation (physio can teach this) helps the "shelf" above the scar settle.
- Phase 2 starts only after explicit clearance at the 6-week (or later) check, and front-loading is reintroduced last of all.
- Same severe-tear logic applies to a complex vaginal birth: physio-guided, not self-paced.

## 11. Belly binding — what it is and isn't

Postpartum binding (Malay *bengkung* / jamu wrap, Chinese 束腹带, modern support garments) is common in Singapore confinement practice. The honest summary:

- **Can:** feel supportive, reduce the "everything is loose" feeling, remind her of posture, help her feel comfortable walking in Phases 0–1.
- **Cannot:** close a diastasis, strengthen a muscle, burn fat, or shrink the uterus faster.
- **Rules if she uses one:** snug, never tight; never during sleep; off for her connection/core work — the muscles must do the job themselves in training, or they never relearn it; stop if it increases pelvic pressure, heaviness, reflux, or numbness.

## 12. Phase 3 — Strengthen (Months 4–6)

**Gate to start:** the Phase 2 gate (§8), passed twice.

Now she trains like a person again, with load progressed gradually:

- **3×/week full-body strength, 30–45 min:** squat pattern, hip hinge/deadlift pattern, push, pull, carry — starting light (roughly where a beginner would) and adding load week to week only while bracing produces no doming and no symptoms.
- **Bracing evolves:** exhale-on-effort gives way to a normal strong brace for heavier sets as Dr Paul/physio are happy and symptom-free training is established. Valsalva-heavy maximal lifting is a month 6+ conversation, not a month 4 one.
- **Core as accessory, 2×/week:** loaded dead bugs, full side planks, Pallof press heavier, farmer's/suitcase carries.
- **Impact:** running, jumping, and high-impact classes wait until she can pass the standard screens symptom-free — e.g. walk 30 min, balance 10 sec/side, single-leg calf raises, jog in place 1 min, forward bound — all with no leaking, heaviness, or pain. For most, that's month 3–4 at the earliest, physio-confirmed if unsure.
- **Eric note:** this is *not* his v6.0 program transplanted. The patterns rhyme (squat, hinge, press), but her progression is gated by pelvic-floor symptoms, not an RPE chart, and her loads rebuild from light — his training maxes are irrelevant here.

---

## 13. Phase 4 — Consolidate (Months 6–12)

- Normal training of her choice, full loading as tolerated. If she wants to train alongside Eric's schedule, this is the phase where that becomes realistic.
- Remaining change in this window is mostly **skin and the last of the fat** — slow, and driven more by time, sleep, protein, and total weight than by any special exercise.
- **Maintenance minimum if life with a baby eats training time:** 2×/week strength + daily walking is enough to hold everything Phases 1–3 built.
- Re-check diastasis tension at 12 months. If a soft, wide gap or a visible bulge persists despite a full, well-executed program, that's a physio/surgical-opinion conversation — rare for her profile, and not a program failure.

## 14. Tracking — measure monthly, not daily

Daily weighing and waist-measuring will lie to her (fluid, feeding, sleep, gut). Track these, **monthly**, same conditions:

| Measure | How | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Morning, weekly average if she weighs more often | Trending toward ~48.6 kg by month 6–9 |
| Waist | Tape at navel level, relaxed, normal exhale — from week 6 | Steadily narrowing |
| Diastasis | Self-check (§6) at week 6 and month 3, 6, 12 | Narrowing *and* getting firmer/shallower |
| Function | Can she do it without doming/symptoms? | Dead bug → side plank → loaded squat/hinge → impact |
| Photo | Front + side, same light/distance, monthly | For her eyes — the day-to-day mirror won't show what the month-to-month photo will |

**Eric's job in tracking:** keep the log if she wants one, never comment on a single day's number, and never compare her to her pre-pregnancy photos out loud. The comparison that helps is month-over-month, offered when she asks.

## 15. Stop and call — red flags at any phase

Stop training and contact Dr Paul / TMC (numbers in [Chapter 10](../course/10-emergencies.md)) for:

- Bleeding that suddenly increases, turns bright red again, soaks a pad in an hour, or large clots
- Fever ≥ 38 °C, foul-smelling discharge
- Worsening pain anywhere — perineum, wound, pelvis, back — instead of improving
- Wound redness, warmth, swelling, opening, or drainage (caesarean)
- A vaginal bulge, or pelvic heaviness that is getting worse, not better
- Leaking that starts or worsens with training and doesn't settle after regressing a level
- Chest pain, breathlessness, or a swollen painful calf — **emergency, same day**

And the emotional red flags from [Chapter 7](../course/07-her-recovery.md) §8 apply for the whole 12 months, not just the first weeks.
