Below is a **Week 26 equipment-free plan** for exercises and yoga.  
No birth ball and no resistance band needed — just your body, a wall, a chair, and optionally a towel/doorframe.

> **Safety first:** Get clearance from your OB/midwife if you have any pregnancy complications. Stop exercise and call your provider if you have bleeding, fluid leakage, strong regular contractions, dizziness, chest pain, calf swelling, or reduced baby movement.

---

# 🤰 Week 26 Goal

**Build stamina, open the hips, strengthen legs and back, and practice pelvic floor relaxation.**  
At Week 26, you do not need intense workouts. You need **consistent, comfortable, pregnancy-safe movement**.

---

# 🗓️ Weekly Structure for Week 26

Do this **5 days per week**:

| Day | Main Focus |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Walking + Strength A |
| Day 2 | Walking + Prenatal Yoga A |
| Day 3 | Walking + Strength B |
| Day 4 | Walking + Prenatal Yoga B |
| Day 5 | Walking + Gentle Yoga C or restorative stretching |
| Day 6 | Optional short walk/rest |
| Day 7 | Rest or very gentle stretching |

You can also combine them if needed:  
For example, walk in the morning and do strength/yoga later.

---

# 🚶‍♀️ Daily Walking

### Target:
**30 minutes, 5 days per week**

### How to do it:
- Walk at a comfortable pace.
- You should be able to talk in full sentences.
- If you feel tired, split it: **15 minutes morning + 15 minutes evening**.
- Wear supportive shoes.
- Stay hydrated.

### Why:
Walking builds cardiovascular stamina for labor and helps baby settle into a good position.

---

# 🏋️ Equipment-Free Strength Work

Do **2 strength sessions per week**.  
You can repeat the same session twice or alternate Strength A and Strength B.

---

## 💪 Strength A — Legs, Push, Back

### 1. Sit-to-Stand Squats or Bodyweight Squats

**Reps:** 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps  
**Optional finish:** Hold the top position for 20–30 seconds.

#### How:
- Stand in front of a chair, feet hip-width apart.
- Slowly sit back as if sitting into the chair.
- Keep heels down.
- Knees track over toes, not collapsing inward.
- Stand up by pressing through your heels.
- Exhale as you stand.

#### Pregnancy cue:
As you lower down, imagine your pelvic floor gently lengthening downward.  
As you stand, gently lift the pelvic floor.

#### Modification:
If squats feel hard, use the chair fully: sit down completely, then stand up.

---

### 2. Wall Push-Ups

**Reps:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps

#### How:
- Stand facing a wall.
- Place hands on the wall slightly wider than shoulders.
- Step feet back so your body is in a gentle plank line.
- Bend elbows and lower chest toward the wall.
- Push back to start.
- Keep your core gently engaged.
- Exhale as you push away.

#### Modification:
Easier: stand closer to the wall.  
Harder: step feet farther back.

#### Avoid:
Do not let your lower back sag.

---

### 3. Doorway Rows — Resistance Band Substitute

**Reps:** 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps

This replaces seated resistance band rows.

#### How:
- Stand in a sturdy doorway.
- Hold the door frame with both hands, one hand on each side.
- Place feet close to the base of the door frame.
- Lean back slightly with straight arms.
- Bend your elbows and pull your chest toward the doorway.
- Squeeze shoulder blades together.
- Slowly straighten arms.

#### Important:
Make sure the door frame is stable. Do not use a door that can swing open.

#### Easier version:
Stand more upright, less lean.

#### Harder version:
Lean back a little farther.

#### No doorway?
Use a strong towel wrapped around a sturdy pole, railing, or heavy furniture leg. Hold both ends and row backward.

---

### 4. Low Step-Ups

**Reps:** 8–12 reps each leg, 2 sets

#### How:
- Use a low, stable step or the bottom stair.
- Step up with one foot.
- Press through the heel and stand tall.
- Step down slowly.
- Keep hips level.
- Hold a wall or chair for balance if needed.

#### Modification:
If balance feels off, do marching in place instead:
- Lift one knee, then the other.
- Swing arms gently.
- Do 1–2 minutes.

---

### 5. Side-Lying Hip Abduction

**Reps:** 10–15 reps each side, 2 sets

#### How:
- Lie on your side with legs stacked or slightly bent.
- Keep your body in a straight line.
- Lift the top leg slowly upward.
- Do not roll your hips backward.
- Lower slowly.

#### Why:
Strengthens outer hips and glutes, which helps pelvic stability.

---

## 💪 Strength B — Legs, Glutes, Posture

### 1. Supported Squat Hold

**Duration:** 3 sets of 20–30 seconds

#### How:
- Stand with feet slightly wider than hips.
- Hold a chair, wall, or counter for support.
- Lower into a comfortable squat.
- Keep heels down if possible.
- Keep chest lifted.
- Breathe steadily.

#### Important:
Do not force a deep squat if you feel pelvic pressure or pain.

---

### 2. Incline Push-Ups on Counter or Table

**Reps:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps

#### How:
- Place hands on a stable counter, table, or wall.
- Step feet back.
- Lower chest toward the surface.
- Push back up.
- Keep body long and stable.

---

### 3. Standing Scapular Squeezes / Wall Angels

This is another band-free back exercise.

#### Option A: Scapular Squeezes

**Reps:** 2 sets of 15 reps

- Stand tall.
- Bend elbows to 90 degrees, arms by your sides.
- Squeeze shoulder blades together.
- Hold 2 seconds.
- Release.

#### Option B: Wall Angels

**Reps:** 2 sets of 8–10 reps

- Stand with back near a wall.
- Bring arms up like a goalpost.
- Slide arms up and down the wall slowly.
- Only go as far as comfortable.
- Do not force your lower back to flatten painfully.

---

### 4. Glute Bridges or Side-Lying Clamshells

Choose one.

#### Glute Bridge

**Reps:** 2 sets of 10–12 reps

- Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat.  
  *At 26 weeks, if lying on your back feels uncomfortable, skip this and do clamshells instead.*
- Press through heels.
- Lift hips until body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees.
- Squeeze glutes.
- Lower slowly.

#### Side-Lying Clamshells

**Reps:** 10–15 reps each side, 2 sets

- Lie on your side with knees bent.
- Keep feet together.
- Open top knee like a clamshell.
- Keep hips stacked.
- Lower slowly.

---

### 5. Standing Pelvic Tilts

**Reps:** 2 sets of 15 reps

#### How:
- Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees soft.
- Gently tuck your pelvis under.
- Then gently release to neutral.
- Move slowly.
- Do not over-arch your lower back.

#### Why:
Helps reduce lower back tension and improves pelvic mobility.

# 🧘 Equipment-Free Prenatal Yoga

Do **3 yoga sessions per week**.  
Each session can be **20–30 minutes**.

You do not need a birth ball. Use a chair, wall, or floor.

---

## 🌸 Yoga A — Hip Opening and Pelvic Mobility

### 1. Comfortable Seat + Breathing

**Time:** 2–3 minutes

- Sit on a cushion or chair.
- Sit tall.
- Inhale through nose for 4 counts.
- Exhale slowly for 6–8 counts.
- Relax shoulders and jaw.

---

### 2. Cat-Cow

**Reps:** 10 slow reps

#### How:
- Come to hands and knees.
- Inhale: gently lift chest and sit bones, keep neck long.
- Exhale: round your upper back gently, tuck chin slightly.
- Move with your breath.

#### Modification:
If knees hurt, do this standing with hands on thighs.

---

### 3. Puppy Pose or Extended Tabletop

**Time:** 5–8 breaths

#### How:
- From hands and knees, walk hands forward.
- Keep hips over knees.
- Rest forehead on the floor if comfortable.
- Breathe into your back and ribs.

#### Modification:
Keep hands on a chair or counter instead of the floor.

---

### 4. Supported Goddess Squat

**Time:** 30–45 seconds, repeat 2 times

#### How:
- Stand with feet wider than hips.
- Turn toes out slightly.
- Lower into a comfortable squat.
- Keep chest lifted.
- Hold a chair or wall if needed.
- Breathe slowly.

#### Why:
Opens inner thighs and pelvic floor.

---

### 5. Low Lunge with Hip Circles

**Time:** 5 breaths each side

#### How:
- Step one foot forward into a lunge.
- Back knee can stay down or lifted, whichever is comfortable.
- Keep front knee over ankle.
- Make small circles with your hips.
- Switch sides.

#### Modification:
Use a wall or chair for balance.

---

### 6. Standing Figure-Four Hip Stretch — Wall Pigeon Substitute

**Time:** 30–60 seconds each side

This is easier than floor pigeon pose.

#### How:
- Stand facing a wall or chair.
- Place one ankle above the opposite knee.
- Sit hips back slightly.
- Keep chest lifted.
- You should feel a stretch in the outer hip/glute.

---

### 7. Seated Butterfly

**Time:** 1–2 minutes

#### How:
- Sit tall.
- Bring soles of feet together.
- Let knees fall open.
- Keep spine long.
- Breathe into inner thighs and pelvic floor.

#### Modification:
Sit on a folded blanket or cushion.

---

### 8. Child’s Pose

**Time:** 60 seconds

#### How:
- Kneel with knees wide.
- Bring big toes together or keep them apart.
- Fold forward.
- Rest arms forward or beside your body.
- Breathe into your lower back, ribs, and pelvic floor.

#### Pregnancy cue:
On each inhale, imagine the pelvic floor softening and expanding.  
On each exhale, release tension.

---

### 9. Side-Lying Rest

**Time:** 2–3 minutes

- Lie on your left side or whichever side is comfortable.
- Bend knees.
- Rest head on arm or pillow.
- Breathe slowly.

---

## 🌸 Yoga B — Lower Back Relief and Gentle Strength

### 1. Standing Side Stretch

**Time:** 5 breaths each side

- Stand tall.
- Reach one arm overhead.
- Gently lean to the opposite side.
- Keep hips steady.
- Switch sides.

---

### 2. Cat-Cow

**Reps:** 10 reps

Same as before.

---

### 3. Standing Pelvic Circles — Birth Ball Substitute

**Time:** 2 minutes each direction

This replaces hip circles on a birth ball.

#### How:
- Stand with feet hip-width apart.
- Hands on hips.
- Make slow circles with your hips, like a hula hoop.
- Keep movement smooth.
- Circle clockwise, then counterclockwise.

#### Variation:
Do this while holding a counter or sink for support.

#### Why:
Loosens the pelvis and encourages comfortable baby positioning.

---

### 4. Supported Chair Squat

**Time:** 30–45 seconds, repeat 2 times

- Hold a chair or wall.
- Lower into a comfortable squat.
- Keep feet flat if possible.
- Breathe slowly.
- Stand up slowly.

---

### 5. Low Lunge with Side Bend

**Time:** 5 breaths each side

- Step one foot forward into a lunge.
- Raise the opposite arm overhead.
- Gently bend toward the front leg side.
- Keep the stretch comfortable.
- Switch sides.

---

### 6. Seated Wide-Leg Fold

**Time:** 5–8 breaths

#### How:
- Sit with legs wide.
- Sit tall.
- Hinge forward slightly from hips.
- Keep back long.
- Do not force a deep fold.

#### Modification:
Sit on a cushion. Keep the fold very gentle.

---

### 7. Happy Baby — Modified

**Time:** 5–8 breaths

#### How:
- Lie on your back only if comfortable.  
  If uncomfortable, skip this.
- Bend knees.
- Hold outside edges of feet or ankles.
- Let knees fall toward armpits.
- Keep lower back relaxed.

#### Alternative:
Do a seated butterfly instead.

---

### 8. Child’s Pose

**Time:** 60 seconds

Focus on long exhales.

---

### 9. Side-Lying Relaxation

**Time:** 2–3 minutes

Breathe slowly and rest.

---

## 🌸 Yoga C — Restorative and Calming

Use this on a tired day.

### Sequence:

1. **Seated breathing:** 3 minutes  
2. **Cat-cow:** 10 reps  
3. **Standing pelvic circles:** 2 minutes each direction  
4. **Supported goddess squat:** 30 seconds × 2  
5. **Standing figure-four stretch:** 30–60 seconds each side  
6. **Seated butterfly:** 1–2 minutes  
7. **Child’s pose:** 60 seconds  
8. **Side-lying rest:** 3–5 minutes  

This session should feel calming, not exhausting.

- Hands on hips.
- Circle hips slowly.
- Keep knees soft.

---

## 2. Standing Pelvic Clock

**Reps:** 10 reps each direction

Imagine your pelvis is a clock.

- Tilt pelvis forward toward 12 o’clock.
- Tilt back toward 6 o’clock.
- Tilt right toward 3 o’clock.
- Tilt left toward 9 o’clock.
- Then make small circles.

This is excellent for pelvic mobility.

---

## 3. Supported Squat Hip Rocks

**Duration:** 1 minute

- Hold a sink, counter, or chair.
- Lower into a comfortable squat.
- Gently rock hips side to side.
- Keep breathing.

---

## 4. Seated Chair Pelvic Rocks

**Duration:** 1–2 minutes

- Sit on a sturdy chair.
- Feet flat.
- Rock pelvis forward and back.
- Then make small circles.
- Keep shoulders relaxed.

This is the closest simple substitute for sitting on a birth ball.

# 🌬️ Pelvic Floor Work for Week 26

Do this daily.

## Pelvic Floor Elevator

**Reps:** 10 reps × 3 sets per day

### How:
1. Sit or lie comfortably.
2. Inhale slowly.
3. Feel your belly, ribs, and pelvic floor expand.
4. Exhale gently.
5. Lightly lift the pelvic floor, like stopping urine flow.
6. Then fully release.

### Important:
The **release** is very important for birth.

Do not only squeeze. Practice this pattern:

> **Inhale: soften and lengthen downward.**  
> **Exhale: gentle lift.**  
> **Then fully let go.**

If you feel pelvic pressure, pain, or tightness, focus more on relaxation and breathing, not hard squeezing.

# 🐈 Cat-Cow Daily

**Reps:** 10 reps daily

This is one of the best simple movements for pregnancy.

### How:
- Start on hands and knees.
- Inhale: gently arch/lift chest.
- Exhale: round upper back.
- Move slowly.
- Keep neck comfortable.

### Why:
- Mobilizes spine and pelvis.
- Relieves lower back tension.
- Helps baby move into a good position.

# 🧎 Child’s Pose Daily

**Duration:** 60 seconds daily

### How:
- Kneel with knees wide.
- Fold forward.
- Rest arms forward or by your sides.
- Breathe into lower back and pelvic floor.

### Pregnancy modification:
Keep knees wide to make space for your belly.

# 🍃 Daily Breathing Practice

Even though you asked about exercise/yoga, breathing is very important for Week 26.

## Target:
**10 minutes daily**

### Simple pattern:
- Inhale through nose: 4 counts  
- Exhale through mouth: 6–8 counts  

### Cue:
Long exhale = calmer nervous system = better labor preparation.

# ⚠️ What to Avoid in Week 26

Avoid:

- Exercising until exhausted.
- Holding your breath during effort.
- Deep, aggressive stretching.
- Hot yoga or overheating.
- Long time flat on your back if it feels uncomfortable.
- High-impact jumping.
- Deep twists that compress the belly.
- Unstable balance work.
- Any movement that causes pain, bleeding, dizziness, or contractions.

# Final Tip

For Week 26, the most important things are:

1. **Walk regularly.**
2. **Practice squats comfortably.**
3. **Open hips gently.**
4. **Strengthen back and legs without equipment.**
5. **Train pelvic floor release, not just squeezing.**
6. **Breathe slowly and keep jaw relaxed.**

You do not need a birth ball or resistance band to prepare well. Consistency with simple movements is more important than equipment.
